Nº 44

Know Your Rhythm

Teaching my body the art of taking it slow.

Washing the dishes and brushing my teeth are two activities that always make me nervous. Suddenly, my nervous system gets triggered, as if I were running late for something incredibly important. For a while now, I’ve been trying to bring awareness to it—praying while washing the dishes, paying attention to the sensations, seeking enjoyment—but the activity, absurd as it may seem, kept giving me anxiety.

These past few days, for some reason, my nervous system has felt especially out of balance. At one point, while washing the dishes, I felt that stress rise, and something inside me said, “Enough.” and I continued cleaning, but at a snail’s pace.

All my movements were the same as usual, but I had dramatically slowed down, including my breathing. After a few seconds cleaning a dish (or minutes, who knows), I suddenly felt my nervous system relax noticeably. At last, I found the serenity and presence I had been seeking. That’s when I realized that in this case, awareness wasn’t enough; I also had to change my behavior, my intention. I had to adjust my rhythm.

I then thought about how many times throughout the day I feel stressed. I understood that the problem wasn’t only psychological but also physical. I was moving too fast. If you run, your heart speeds up. If you breathe slowly, you relax. The tempo at which we move sends a signal to our nervous system, and it reacts, without knowing if you’re running late for work or being chased by a tiger.

In today’s world—and I feel this especially in the United States—everything pushes you to accelerate. It’s in the air. There’s a constant sense that you’re never doing enough, and so you have to do whatever you’re doing now faster, so you can get to the next thing as quickly as possible.

Today, I washed the dishes again at 10% speed, so slowly that my ego complained. Perfect. This is my small revolutionary act for the day. I choose to move extremely slow.

Thinking about this brought to mind the poem “Love Your Rhythm” by Rubén Darío, which goes like this:

Love your rhythm and let your actions rhyme

under its law, just like your verses;

you are a universe of universes

and your soul, a fountain of songs.

The celestial unity you presuppose

will bring forth diverse worlds within you,

and as your scattered numbers resonate,

they will Pythagorize in your constellations.

Hear the divine rhetoric

of the bird in the air, and the nocturnal

geometric radiance you intuit;

kill the indifferent taciturnity

and string pearl upon crystal-clear pearl

where truth pours out its urn.

I’ve kept it close to my heart recently, but today I saw it in a new light: to truly love something, you must first know it, and knowing it is also loving it. “Know yourself” is ultimately the same as “Love yourself.” This week I understood that I can’t love my rhythm without first knowing it.

Once again, the question arises: How do I want to live? I’m certain it’s not in a hurry. Maybe the answer isn’t to move like a snail (although it’s tempting), but I also know it’s not the frantic rhythm I usually subject myself to, triggering a near-chronic state of stress in my body. Somewhere in between lies my rhythm.

And that’s my small reflection for today. I’ll end with a Chinese proverb that just came to mind:

“If one desires speed, one will not reach.”

With all my love,

A.
