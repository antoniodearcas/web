112. I'm not sure I'm capable

Who are you choosing as your guide?

I feel deeply grateful because I can see how all the pieces are falling into place. On the path toward God, all the facets of life, once scattered and often at odds with one another, gradually unify under a single purpose.

All that is required to move in that direction is to follow the Holy Spirit, which is the part of your mind that has not forgotten that you are a child of God.

The other voice in your mind, loud and incoherent, is the ego, and it leads in the opposite direction: toward complexity, separation, and exhaustion.

Here is a letter addressed to the part of your mind that must make the key decision: Who do I listen to?

"I'm not sure I'm capable."

The other day I invited a member of Entre el Cielo y la Tierra (Between Heaven and Earth) to lead one of our group sessions herself, guiding a meditation she regularly practiced on her own. That was her response, tender and honest.

I want to share with you what I told her, because it came with the clarity of the Spirit not only for her, but also for me and for everyone.

I know there is something inside you that wants to come out into the world, something you want to do, a project waiting to be born. I share this message so that fear does not win that battle.

The key is in that "I'm not sure I'm capable." I know you are capable, if you weren't, we wouldn't even be having this conversation. The opportunity would not have arisen. You know it too, because one part of your mind (the Holy Spirit) has absolute faith in you, because it knows who you are and Who accompanies you at every moment.

The thing is, there is another part of your mind (the ego) that says something diametrically opposite: it tells you that you are weak, vulnerable, limited, and that it's better not to expose yourself to the world, lest the world discover and confirm that dark, small image.

When you say "I'm not sure I'm capable," what you are really saying is: "I don't know who I am: what the Holy Spirit tells me, or what the ego tells me."

Faith is holding on to the Spirit and acting from that place, despite the ego's complaints. And in acting, we discover that our faith was grounded in truth: who you are has no limit, you are perfect and loved exactly as you are, you are gifted with infinite power to do whatever you set your mind to, and everything always turns out far better than you had imagined.

That said, do what feels right to you. Often, when I feel pressured to do something, I ask myself: "What would love do?" There is no need to force anything. The goal is not to do things, it is to know who you are. You are love. Align with it, and if from that place doing scary things arises, wonderful. If not, it is wiser to say "no thank you" and embrace yourself knowing that all is well.

(I think I needed to remember this myself, so this message is as much for me as it is for you 🤍)

With all my love,

Antonio
