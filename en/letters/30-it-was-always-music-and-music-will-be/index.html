30. It Was Always Music, and Music Will Be

I sit down to write, almost overwhelmed, with my heart expanded like a small burning sun, its light whiter than white. I sit down to write before the clouds of this human ego, still bound by doubt and fear, obscure what I now see clearly.

It has always been music, and music will be.

God has given me many gifts. It seems that whatever I attempt to do, I do well (except playing football, that seems to be for another incarnation). But this abundance of gifts has made it very difficult to decide what to do with my life.

In a moment of crisis, in the middle of the pandemic and after several consecutive failures, spirituality appeared as an answer to the question, "What do I want to do?" A few days ago, I discovered the Sanskrit word Samvega, which means something like the relentless drive, the insatiable desire of the soul that cannot continue living without going deeper. It’s the force that led Siddhartha to abandon his life as a prince and become Buddha and, in a more humble dose, what made me direct my whole life towards the search for Truth, Love, Beauty.

Four years after that turn of heart (perhaps the right word is "conversion"), the doubts I had around the question "Who am I?" seem to be calming down, and the second great question has returned to me: "What do I want to do?"

When I asked myself this question last time, four years ago, I looked for specific things that would happen in a hypothetical future: working for such a company, forming a band, studying such a degree, etc. This led to the period of deepest anxiety I’ve ever experienced, so I ended up abandoning the question and focusing on the day-to-day.

This time, instead of seeking the answer within my own mind, I offered my question as a prayer to the Great Mind, asking for direction and guidance. My question was limited because it came from my limited human ego. But the answers that come from God are not limited because God is infinite, and everything that comes from Him is equally infinite. Therefore, the answers I have been receiving over the past few months have not been concrete things but eternal principles, values on which to base my life now and that will be just as valid 40 years from now.

Returning to today and the purpose of this letter, the reason why my heart (the main center of communication between my mind, my soul, and God) is now on fire is that I have just had a conversation where something tremendous was revealed to me.

It has always been music, and music will be.

It happened during a conversation with my music production teacher, Sherry Li.

God sees in us our true essence, beyond what we perceive as shadows, sins, faults, insecurities, limitations, fears... God sees through all that, and seeing who we really are, has infinite faith in us.

That is exactly what I felt when speaking to my teacher. After talking a little about my spirituality, the following Socratic dialogue began:

She asked me if my understanding of myself is that of becoming a vehicle for God.

I said yes.

She asked if God’s role is to expand His love in the world.

I said yes.

She asked if I intended to do that through music.

I said yes.

She asked if reaching the greatest number of people was equivalent to expanding and sharing that love as much as possible.

And there, right there, I hit a wall.

I perfectly understood her reasoning, and intellectually it was evident that the answer was “yes.” But, on a more subtle level, I saw a ray of light hitting a dark cloud. The cloud was the belief that it was better not to be ambitious, that it was more spiritual not to aspire to reach so many people because being famous was not compatible with a spiritual existence. The belief was that the most reasonable and safe thing to do was to settle for being a small, indie artist, sharing my music with a close circle, keeping a low profile in the industry, and not aspiring to reach more people.

I remember the moment I accepted that belief. At the time, it was coherent with my understanding of what “a spiritual life” was supposed to be. But my understanding has changed dramatically. It has been the light emanating from very famous people (Dr. Hawkins, Bowie, Beethoven, Mozart, Sufjan Stevens, Paul Simon, The Beatles…) that has illuminated my path. If they had limited their careers in the way I was doing, my life would undoubtedly be darker.

I also saw that this aim to shoot low didn’t come from humility, as I might have thought in the past, but from a lack of clarity, conviction and, above all, from fear.

To reach that level of success, a total commitment to the art is needed. So, there was the fear of focusing too much on one thing, preferring the safety of having my eggs in several baskets and not having to decide. But freedom is not about having endless options and being able to do whatever one wants. That’s anarchy, the chaos that comes from a life driven by our desires, which are ever-changing, insatiable, and contradictory. No, freedom, in this context, is having total certainty about what one must do, choosing that option as if it were the only one, and doing so without a trace of fear.

All these revelations kept lighting up in my mind like a sequence of fireworks as my teacher, my friend, the Holy Spirit, guided me towards that freedom.

After following her reasoning, which clashed with those limiting beliefs, I paused for a second to reflect. "There are two things you’ve said that don’t quite fit into my understanding," I said. I never expressed the second one, which I don’t remember now, and I barely finished talking about the first.

“The first is that although I have always considered my ability to write songs as the greatest gift God has given me…”

And that’s when the revelation happened.

I would have finished the sentence by saying "[…] in reality, I have many gifts, and I don’t give more priority to music than to writing or even cooking, for example." But I myself said the words, “My ability to write songs is the greatest gift God has given me.” At that moment, I clearly saw what my path was, what God was reminding me of. In Spanish, the word remember is recordar, which directly translates as to re-heart, to pass again through the heart. In that moment, I remembered.

I was waiting for God or someone to give me the answer, as if coming from outside of me. I didn’t want to decide because I realized that fear still played a role in my decisions. But at that moment, the answer came from within me, from the part inside me that is one with Truth, the part of me free from fear and, therefore, guided only by Love. The part of me that can say, "The Father and I are one."

I told Sherry what I had just seen, and she said, “Well, all your gifts come together when you choose music, don’t they?” and it’s true, painting, engineering, writing, teaching, and the silent transmission of love and truth, all these fuse into a single path when the central path is music.

After recognizing the truth of all this, I felt the world around me change in quality. From solid to gas. Where once there was Sherry and a restaurant full of people, now there were only God and my soul on an empty stage.

This is the second time this has happened to me this month, and both times my body adopted exactly the same posture, one of inwardness, of extreme vulnerability, based not on fear but on the understanding that trust imposes itself relentlessly as a way of life. My body shrank in the overwhelming sensation of being before something infinite and infinitely loving. I closed my eyes and disappeared for a few seconds. Then I opened them and gave Sherry a hug, much longer than would have been appropriate with a teacher but much shorter than the one I would have given to Christ. Considering that at that moment I was hugging both, the balance came to about 10 seconds, perhaps.

After the conversation ended, I walked home, or rather, I flew. A new revelation about what to do with this life had come to my heart. The last one of this caliber was two years ago when I understood that my life involved marriage. The beauty of these revelations is that they didn’t show anything new; they simply showed a vision of my life free of fear, they reminded me of what had always been there in truth. The truth is eternally here and now, behind the false beliefs we hold. As Rumi said, “Your task is not to seek for love, but merely to seek and find all the barriers within yourself that you have built against it.”

For me, it was always been music, and music will be.

Today, I held my destiny in my chest, seeing a clear path before me, and in my mind echoed the phrase “All in.”

This is the path for me. In it, all my gifts unify, and it demands the best of my abilities. It is my path because it is the one that will require me to develop my potential, both human and divine, to the fullest.

As this rearrangement of my priorities occurred, my mind immediately began to reorder the house. I clearly saw where my time and energy should go.

This revelation also includes, not the belief, but the knowledge that there is nothing that can obstruct my path. Nothing in this world, no fear, no trauma, no insecurity can stop me. No challenge can be too great. My capacity (and here I speak for all, for you, who are of my same nature), my capacity is infinite. There is no limitation except what my beliefs impose, and no one is in charge of my beliefs except me, and I am free to change them if I see they are obstructing the expansion of love, truth, and beauty in my life.

I still don’t know where I will be in 5 years (a question I’m asked a lot lately). But I do know that music will be my main occupation. I now know that my days must revolve around cultivating my voice, my songs, my ability as a producer, and the depth of my heart.

The feeling that God has revealed this path to me and the feeling that I make this decision freely and with the highest degree of commitment and integrity are the same feeling. It does not come from outside because God is not outside of me. I am in the Universe; the Universe is in me. This decision about how to orient my life comes from the point where both parts of that sentence are simultaneously true.

This is my will, and I accept it because, as far as my understanding reaches at this moment, it is The Will for me.

Music has always been there, pulling me out of shyness, pulling me away from my path as an engineer, pulling me out of the void I fell into during the pandemic. Music is the vehicle through which God has spoken to me when He wanted to do so with words. My two great epiphanies included a moment when God spoke to me through a song (“To Build a Home” and “13th Century Metal”).

Now I understand that music is the most authentic religion, the one that will never sell itself as such, yet still will be able to set our hearts on fire. It’s the language beyond languages, beyond all form; it is the purest poetry, prior to images; it is the language of the soul, and, at its highest peak, it is the expression of a cosmos that is self-aware, expressing what it knows itself to be in essence.

After 10 years of doubts, of trying and failing, of building and destroying, of traume, illness and healing, after many deaths and rebirths, today I finally say a complete 'Yes' to my calling as a musician. I dedicate the rest of my life to this noble discipline, relinquishing doubt, a misguided guide, and facing fear, the illusory wall that separates this world from heaven, head-on. I ask You, Lord, to guide me, to continue igniting this flame, and to dissolve any obstacle that tries to hold me back. Relying on faith and with my heart as a torch, I walk step by step.

Thank you, Sherry.

Thank you to the Father, the Creator.

Thank you to the Son, the Creation.

Thank you to the Holy Spirit, the tuner of consciousness that reveals the oneness of Creation and Creator.

Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will.

With all my love,

A.
