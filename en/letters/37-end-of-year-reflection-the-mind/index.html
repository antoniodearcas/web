End-of-Year Reflection: The Mind

5 questions about each area of your life to end the year with clarity

THE MIND

1. What new knowledge or skills have I acquired this year that have added value to my life?

2. What topics or problems have occupied my mind repeatedly? What have I learned from them?

3. What have I done to exercise my mind or help it become more balanced?

4. What do I feed my mind with?*

5. What things interest me and make me feel enthusiastic right now?

REFLECTION

*What do I mean by this question?

Our consciousness is innocent. It’s like a computer without antivirus, saying “yes” to any program you want to install. All it takes is hearing an idea once for our mind to accept and integrate it. From a young age, we are bombarded with all kinds of programs. Programs based on truth promote life. Thoughts based on falsehood seek to destroy it. We’re full of both.

Here’s an example of two thoughts—two programs—that, after hearing them just once, became part of me. One based on truth, and the other on falsehood.

"Si tu veux, tu peux"

"If you want, you can." A French teacher used to tell me this when I was a kid. It’s a great program to install in a child’s mind, which is still pure potential. It instills a sense of unlimited capability and courage. It also makes you realize you have a will, the ability to set an intention, and points to the unlimited power of that will. If something inside you resisted this idea, you know there’s a program installed that contradicts your infinite capability and the power of your intention. I can feel it within myself as I write this, but I don’t listen to that voice anymore.

"You don’t have what it takes to do X"

Although I probably heard this more than once in the past, there was one instance when it hit me particularly hard. One of the first people I met in the music industry, someone I trusted, told me I didn’t have what it takes to carry out a project on my own. Immediately, all my inner demons stood up and said, “It’s true!” The program was installed and began its destructive work.

To uninstall it, I had to dive very deep within myself to uncover the truth it was trying to hide. Once the truth is uncovered, falsehood’s days are numbered. Truth doesn’t need protection; falsehood needs your cooperation to survive. St. John explains it like this:

"The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it." (John 1:5)

I also like Terence McKenna’s version:

"The truth does not require your participation in order to exist. Bullshit does."

Conclusion: Choose wisely what you feed your mind with. What comes from God, what aligns with truth, brings peace and elevates our consciousness. What is not aligned with truth brings confusion, suffering, fear, or conflict.

A powerful guide is the Socratic filter. Before exposing yourself to any type of information, run it through this filter:

Is it true?

Analyze if it comes from a reliable source.

Is it good or useful?

What is the purpose of this information? Is it constructive, or does it foster confusion, suffering, or conflict? Does it have a positive impact?

Is it necessary?

We live in the information age, where information floods us from all directions. Even if something passes the first two filters, ask yourself if it’s relevant at this moment, or if it will only add noise. Another way to look at it is: Will this help me solve a problem, make a decision, or clarify an issue? This prevents information overload and distraction.

For this reason, I generally try to avoid any source of information where someone else chooses what I consume—it rarely passes the filter. TV news? Out. Instagram reels? Out. I’ve trained my YouTube algorithm quite well, but it rarely passes the last filter.

There’s only one thought that keeps us chained to these questionable sources of information: “You have to stay informed!”—and I’m not even sure it passes the first filter. I believe we have to stay educated instead. We should seek knowledge—something that changes us for the better—not information that goes in one ear and out the other, leaving chaos behind.

So, I’ll leave you with these questions again, hoping they bring some light and guidance in this strange and overwhelming information age. It’s only up to you to ensure peace in your inner world, and the outer world will merely reflect it.

THE MIND

1. What new knowledge or skills have I acquired this year that have added value to my life?

2. What topics or problems have occupied my mind repeatedly? What have I learned from them?

3. What have I done to exercise my mind or help it become more balanced?

4. What do I feed my mind with?

5. What things interest me and make me feel enthusiastic right now?

With all my love,

A.
