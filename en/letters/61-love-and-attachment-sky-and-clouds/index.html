61. Love and Attachment. Sky and Clouds

Everything is a gift

"For the person who has learned to let go and let be, nothing can ever get in the way again." – Meister Eckhart

A few weeks ago, silence came back into my life like thunder. Betül, my partner for the past year and a half, finished her studies in Boston and returned to Istanbul.

When we live with someone, our mind starts getting used to the good company, to receiving affection and constant support. We start to cling, to generate attachments. It’s enough for one of us to feel a bit down one day, and for the other person to show up offering comfort. That’s when the ego says: “Ah, when I’m not well, this person can help me.” That’s what the ego does: it tries to fill a spiritual void with material things from this world—substances, possessions, experiences, and in the case of this letter, relationships.

Knowing that Betül was going to leave, my mind began to fill with fearful thoughts, imagining I was going to lose something important. But I know that, in truth, God only gives and never takes away, so I began to pray to understand what that meant in this case.

The answer I received was the following: when Betül came into my life, she came as a gift from God. At that time, Love and Wisdom decided that a wonderful girlfriend was the best thing for my growth—and so it was. But now that Betül is leaving, what then—does God love me less than He did back then?

Faced with that truth, I understood that even if I couldn’t yet see or comprehend it, whatever was going to come after Betül’s departure would be the most loving and wise thing that could happen to me. My faith grew strong enough to hold on to that branch, and from that day on, the river of emotions could no longer sweep me away because I was firmly anchored.

The day Betül left was beautiful. I helped her with everything I could and accompanied her to the airport. The painful moment came—of course—at the security gate, when, after giving her a hug, I saw her walk away. Something in my chest began to hurt intensely. A few years ago, I would have interpreted that feeling as: “this is breaking my heart.” Now I know the heart cannot break. The only things that can break are the walls we build around it to hide the truth. It’s as if the heart were covered by a crust of attachments. That’s what breaks. Knowing that my attachment to Betül was cracking, I welcomed the pain with joy.

Attachment disguises itself as love, but in reality, it is what blocks love.

A few minutes later, the storm passed, and I returned home feeling strange, but at peace. That night, I resumed my meditation practice, which I had abandoned three years ago. It seems I needed to sharpen my attention to cut through the illusions that were about to arise in those days.

The next morning, there was a palpable emptiness in my apartment. I contemplated it, knowing that emptiness is not absence, but presence. I meditated again for an hour, did some yoga, read my ACIM lesson, had breakfast, and went out for a walk.

Walking alongside the aptly named Muddy River, new fears began to cross my mind. I don’t remember exactly what they were—because they’re no longer in me—but they probably had to do with the fear of loneliness, of feeling isolated and abandoned. What I’m doing in this period of my life is literally filling myself with light, going deeper into my subconscious, illuminating and dissolving in the light all the wounds I didn’t know how to process in the past. The thought of continuing that path into the darkness all by myself terrified me. But the truth behind that fear was that I am not walking alone. So, recognizing the falsehood of those thoughts, I began to surrender to reality, without resistance, saying yes with as much depth as I could, and letting go of all the ego’s complaints.

A few minutes and earthquakes later, stillness returned. What I saw then scared me again, but for very different reasons. I had let go of my attachments to Betül so deeply that I felt I had dissolved the relationship. “Wait—did we break up?” I felt total uncertainty, an overwhelming sense of freedom and vulnerability. Like a baby in his father’s arms, with no idea of what’s coming, but trusting he is fully held. Again, I didn’t resist; I simply contemplated and surrendered to that new emptiness. “If it is Your will that this relationship continue, it will. If not, there is nothing I can do to prevent it. Therefore, I release any attempt to control the situation and simply try to listen for Your guidance.”

And then a light came on—or rather, I rediscovered it. As I said before, attachment disguises itself as love, but in truth it hides it. Attachment is a chain, and love, if it is not completely free, is not love. I had just cut all the chains that bound me to Betül, including the belief “she is my girlfriend,” which is why I doubted whether we had broken up. What I rediscovered then is that I am not with her because of chains binding us—I’m come to her from the freedom of love. A relationship like that renews itself in every instant. It is the opposite of taking something for granted. When we take something for granted, it becomes part of the furniture of our life. When we don’t take it for granted, it reveals itself for what it truly is: a gift.

And so I came to perceive Betül’s presence in my life once more—as a gift that renews itself each day. My attachments would have compared the new (long-distance) situation to the previous one and mourned the loss. But without attachment, the past doesn’t exist, and the reality is that there’s a girl I love who lives in Istanbul and who calls me every day with a smile. A gift.

It was a beautiful beginning to this new chapter, fine-tuning my mind to reality the moment it changed, thus avoiding even the slightest resistance, the slightest suffering. The consequences of letting go of that attachment have been incredible.

In leaning on Betül, I had externalized the source of love. But that’s not real. The source of love is within, and if I’m not able to love myself, it doesn’t matter how loving someone else is—I’ll only be able to receive the love I’m capable of accepting.

Letting go of my attachment was a declaration of truth: the source of love is within, nothing external is essential, and I reclaim my capacity to love myself. And so it was—the crust of attachments covering my heart cracked a little more, revealing a new depth of my seemingly infinite heart. What I found there had tremendous and immediate effects in many areas of my life. The following two weeks were a symphony of healing revelations. One of them in particular I hope to share, because of how beautiful it was. The ways in which my relationship with Betül has expanded are also worth sharing, but I don’t want this letter to go on much longer.

Now I see that the answer to my prayer was absolutely true. It’s no longer faith—it’s certainty: the fact that my wonderful girlfriend left Boston and I was left “alone,” face to face with my darkness, was truly the most loving and wise thing that could have happened to me. It may sound forced, even false, but it’s not. I’m writing from the light.

What is false is separation—and this was a wonderful opportunity to deepen into that truth. The truth is that God is, and God is One, and nothing but illusion can separate me from Betül or from any other facet of Creation. To recognize this is to heal. That is the treasure this new dragon was hiding behind its fearsome face. But again—fear is just another illusion. God is, and God is Love, and nothing else is true.

With all my love,

A.

P.S. The photo that opens this letter was taken a year ago in Boston. This year, when the magnolias came back into bloom, I decided to take a walk to the same place and send a selfie to Betül. A few minutes before arriving, she called me on video and asked, “What are you doing?”

If our minds aren’t connected… then flowers don’t exist and I’m a banana.
