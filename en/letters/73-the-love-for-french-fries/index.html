73. The Love for French Fries

What are we really longing for?

“Why do I love French fries?” she asked me as she waited, somewhat anxiously, for the fries she had ordered.

“Do you think ‘love’ is the right word? Do you really love French fries the way you love me, for example? Maybe you don’t love French fries. Maybe you don’t even desire them. I believe that what we truly love and long for is love itself, but we confuse it with the things of the world, in this case some fries. That’s why we think we love them. But to love something, in its deepest sense, is to be one with it, to recognize ourselves in it.

Maybe we are made to love love itself, call it God, the Self, or the Universe. Maybe we are made to identify with that unique and eternal Reality, and not with the multiple and fleeting things of the world.

But finding God seems difficult, so instead we love the things of the world.

But it is that very love for the things of the world that makes it difficult to find God.

We love these things, but they never satisfy our deepest longing. Maybe because, deep down, they are not what we are truly seeking. Only love can fulfill that longing, because only love is real.

French fries, for example, release neurotransmitters similar to those produced when we love or feel loved. That can create a pleasurable illusion of love. But the fries come to an end and, like all pleasures we confuse with the fullness of love, they often leave behind a bitter aftertaste. Not because they are bad in themselves, but because they weren’t what our heart was truly yearning for.

Only the love we accept remains. Only it can heal this search for water in mirages, and finally offer rest.

Once that confusion, that misdirected love, that mistaken identification is healed, one can enjoy eating whatever one wants, without guilt. “Love, and do what you will,” as Saint Augustine said.

As for me, nothing you do or don’t do can change the love I feel for you.”

And there, in that physical and metaphysical embrace, desire and guilt vanished, and she discovered that, in truth, she didn’t want any more fries.

With all my love,

A.
