86. You Are Mine

And an all-in wager against rage

Everything in nature works like this:

The fruit falls when it’s ripe.

The branch, eaten away by termites, falls under its own weight when the force of gravity surpasses the strength of its bond to the trunk.

The leaves fall when the cold arrives, and the birds fly away in search of warmth.

In nature there is no doubt, no confusion, no conflict. Only the harmony of natural law. Humans would call it certainty, but even that is a human concept, and reality is beyond concepts.

We are part of that same nature. Nothing has changed.

Yesterday, in qigong class, we did a 50-minute meditation holding various postures. I decided to do it standing, to increase the effect and go deeper.

As soon as we began, the classmate sitting to my left started moving. He was wearing a vest made of some synthetic fabric that made noise whenever he moved. The meditation implicitly required an effort to remain still, but this classmate began moving continuously and didn’t stop.

The sound of his vest began to irritate me, and judgments and thoughts of attack started running through my mind:

“Why can’t you stay still? Can’t you make an effort to be quiet like the rest of us?”

But I was beyond my mind, watching all of it, and this is what I saw.

After a while of indulging in anger, I slowly began to realize that I was letting myself be deceived by an illusion. That anger, which supposedly attacked my companion, was only hurting me.

“Could I stop attacking myself?”

I then looked for the source of the anger, the energy from which those thoughts were arising. I looked into my abdomen, and there it was, in the region of the liver. “Okay, I’m generating this energy in response to the sound of my classmate’s jacket. Do I really want to give an ordinary sound the power to hurt me in this way? Clearly not. I reclaim the power of my mind and choose to stop attacking myself.”

But instead of disappearing, the anger seemed to intensify. I had been observing it for about half an hour, struggling with it without much success. Then I felt I had to call in the heavyweights:

“Holy Spirit, help me to see this with love.”

Rage.

“I’m seeing an illusion. I only want to see through Christ’s vision.”

Rage.

Finally, something within me said, “I would rather die than go on attacking my brother in this way.”

And that’s when the change occurred. It wasn’t that the Holy Spirit or Christ lacked power to help me; it was that my will wasn’t unified in the decision to let go. I was delegating while another part of me was still clinging. But choosing death over continuing that way meant putting everything I had on the table.

At that point, my will became one; unity is love, and to reach love is to reach healing. The energy burst out like a flame and left my body like a demon, seeing that it was no longer welcome in that house.

And at that moment, after about forty minutes of constant noise, my classmate finally became still.

Silence.

Once again, I saw that my mind is not separate. Following laws beyond my comprehension, my anger and my companion’s restlessness must have been dancing together. As my anger left, he found stillness.

A few minutes later he began moving again, but this time I saw something radically different.

Where before I saw my classmate, now I felt the presence of Christ sitting beside me. I saw that, with perfect wisdom, he had generated a soft, steady noise to trigger my ego. That anger was within me, and sooner or later it needed to come up to be resolved. What better way to do so than in the middle of meditation and in the presence of the teacher?

The illusion was that my classmate, in his immaturity, was preventing me from accessing peace.

The truth was that my mind, in its immaturity, was choosing to attack rather than to love. The only obstacle was within me.

But fruits fall by their own weight once they’ve received enough sun, and minds become unified and healed once they’ve received enough presence.

When that obstacle dissolved, what had once caused me suffering now appeared as a blessing. What had once been a clumsy guy was now Christ in His perfect innocence.

When the meditation ended, I opened my eyes, and there was a great leap. Not only my classmate is Christ. The entire universe is Christ. Nothing else is real. Everything else is a projection of my ego. But in that moment, the projections were suspended just long enough for me to see: reality is only Christ, evolving through the form of the universe to realise Itself.

Everything is in perfect harmony.

Everything is perfectly innocent.

At the end of class, I told all this to another friend of mine. He said the guy kept moving in his chair because he had fallen asleep and was nodding off. Who could get angry at someone who’s asleep?

There is only one universal will, and everything else is the optical illusion of being a separate individual. My consciousness keeps opening that illusion, petal by petal, and I begin to perceive a radiance emerging from the center of the flower.

The jewel in the center of the lotus.

Om mani padme hum.

As I learn to trust the light that I’m beginning to glimpse, the words of Ramana Maharshi start to make sense:

“What is destined not to happen will not happen, no matter how hard you try.
And what is destined to happen will happen, even if you try to prevent it.
This is certain.
Therefore, the best course is to remain still.”

At the end of the day, I went for a walk, in a tender conversation with that innocent pPresence. A fear arose in my mind:

“Now I see you because I spend fourteen hours a week in the master’s presence. When I return to Spain and lose access to this light, my mind will descend again into the dark valley and fall once more into the river. When I lose the ability to hear you, what will become of me?”

And the answer came:

“Even if you stop hearing me, I will still be here, always, guiding your every action. You are mine.”

With all my love,

A.
