Nº 43

Tuning Your Instrument

Life is a symphony, and it is good to be in harmony.

Before giving a concert, the first thing a musician does is tune their instrument. This is especially beautiful in orchestras: the first violin plays an "A," and then all the musicians tune their instruments simultaneously, creating a bouquet of sounds where each part seeks to be in perfect harmony with the others. Once done, the orchestra sounds like a single instrument. It is, in fact, a single instrument, played by the conductor through the power of intention.

Now imagine an orchestra again, but no one has explained to the musicians that they can (and should) tune their instruments. They don’t even know it’s an option. How would that sound? How would the musicians treat each other when hearing the resulting chaos? They’d likely look at each other bewildered, annoyed, saying, “What are you doing? That’s not the right note!” Each convinced their instrument is fine as it is, and it’s everyone else who is playing out of tune.

This, in many ways, is our beloved humanity. But I’m not here to complain. I’m here to propose something: let’s tune before we play. Let’s dedicate the first minutes of the day to tuning the instrument that we are.

The human being is an instrument. You, the mysterious you, play it with the power of your intention. The universe you live in is the resulting symphony.

Now, the musicians in the orchestra tune themselves to the "A" given by the first violin. But what should we tune ourselves to?

Each religion provides its own answer to this question. My current understanding is that what we are in essence is of the same nature as God, to the point that they are one and the same. By this, I don’t mean that God has a human form or attributes. What I mean was eloquently expressed by C.S. Lewis, author of The Chronicles of Narnia and a great theologian:

"You do not have a soul. You are a soul. You have a body."

What you are is a soul, and that is what is of the same nature as God. The human being is the instrument through which your soul perceives.

Therefore, we can discover what we should tune ourselves to by studying and imitating the traditional attributes of our understanding of God, which are common to all religions:

SILENCE AND STILLNESS

Cultivate inner silence, a calm mind free of chaotic thoughts, capable of listening deeply and acting with wisdom.

Practice: Meditate. The goal isn’t to silence your mind but to know it; to know yourself. The only failed meditation is the one that you don’t do.

CONSTANT MOVEMENT

The universe is in constant motion. Cultivate a body that is agile and adaptable to change, capable of moving with grace and staying in tune with the rhythms of life.

Practices: Dance, walk, run, practice yoga or calisthenics.

UNCONDITIONAL LOVE

God is love. We exist in God. We are love. Cultivate a pure heart through forgiveness, compassion, gratitude, or selfless service.

Practices: Search your heart for the resentments that darken it and pray for the ability to forgive. Do the workbook in A Course in Miracles. Say "I love you" more often.

UNITY

Everything is so deeply interconnected that, in truth, it is a single "thing." Cultivate kindness towards all forms of life, recognizing their dignity and acting with respect.

Practice: Care for and connect with the living beings around you, whether they are humans, animals, or plants.

PERFECT ORDER

Nothing is missing, nothing is superfluous; everything is exactly where it needs to be, flowing in perfect harmony with everything else.

Practice: Clean your space, simplify your life, know your priorities, plan your day accordingly.

INFINITE CREATIVITY

God is the Creator, and we are His instruments of creation.

Practice: Write, dance, sing, paint, or learn to play an instrument like the piano.

TRUTH AND WISDOM

God is truth, and ignorance is the sole cause of suffering.

Practice: Seek books written by those deeply attuned to truth. Read about Jesus, Buddha, Krishna, or the lives of saints from various traditions. Some books can transform your life profoundly with a single reading. My favorite is “Letting Go” by Dr. David R. Hawkins. Read daily.

LIGHT

God is the source of light. Darkness does not exist; it is merely the absence of light projected by your ego.

Practice: Bring the light of your awareness to areas in your life where you perceive darkness.

ETERNAL PATIENCE

God is never in a hurry. Nature never rushes, yet everything is accomplished.

Practice: Move through life unhurried, trusting the process. Love your rhythm.

FULFILLMENT

In God, there is no lack. In our true being, there isn’t either.

Practice: Keep a gratitude journal to train yourself to recognize that you have enough. The universe always provides what we need—be it a promotion or a painful breakup. Everything in our lives comes from infinite love and wisdom.

PEACE

There is a supreme peace that transcends all conflict. It is here and now, beyond the clamor of our minds. What comes from God brings peace and love.

Practice: Seek what comes from God and move away from what doesn’t. Observe how your mind seeks conflict and instead choose peace.

HUMILITY

God is omnipotent yet does not impose His presence.

Practice: Serve others in subtle ways. Prepare breakfast or clean your space out of love for those you live with.

BEAUTY

Beauty is the face of God, and it reveals itself in intimacy.

Practice: Observe with mindfulness how milk swirls into your coffee, the shapes it creates. Look at the clouds outside your window and appreciate them.

JOY

God is the source of joy. That source is only here and now, and our attention is the key that unlocks it. Nothing else is real.

Practice: Engage in any of the above categories, and joy will follow naturally.

The more complex the instrument, the longer it takes to tune. Our instrument—the human being—is the most complex known in the universe, and it will take us eons to perfect its tuning. Therefore, the goal should not be to be perfectly tuned but to learn to love the act of tuning itself.

I’ll end with an anecdote about Hazrat Inayat Khan, a Sufi musician and writer. He played the sitar, an incredibly complex instrument. As a true mystic, his relationship with music was so profound that, for him, tuning the sitar was not just a technical preparation but an essential part of the musical act—a ritual of connection between the musician and divine harmony.

It is said that during one of his tours in the United States, he became so absorbed in tuning his sitar, he entered such a state of devotion while adjusting the strings, it took him so long to do it, that when he finally finished tuning, the audience exploded in applause and the organizers closed the curtain. Everyone thought that was the concert.

The first step is realizing that you can tune your instrument and that your life will improve immensely as you do. The second step is understanding your instrument and learning how to tune it. But perhaps the true work lies in doing so as Hazrat tuned his sitar: with so much love that it could move an audience.

And here is where the analogy with music breaks: a musician tunes their instrument before playing the piece. But tuning ourselves is not just the prelude; it is the concert itself.

With all my love,

A.
