Rage

Rage against the dying of the light

Terence McKenna used to say that "culture is not your friend." The concepts through which the world operates are too small to guide what you truly are, and they only bring the confusion of someone who listens to too many voices.

An agitated surface hides the pearl at the bottom.

If you let yourself be guided by inherited and limited conceptions, you will receive an inherited and limited life. Meanwhile, your authentic life waits, glowing luminous beneath the opacity of ordinary existence.

What to do with the inner unease that stirs when one senses this?

There is a Sanskrit term, "samvega," which names a kind of inner roar that ignites when one realizes they have been living in a superficial way, out of alignment with the truth they sense at a deeper level. In that moment, the option of continuing as before becomes unbearable. The only way forward is not to change anything external, but to say "enough," to cross the inner wall of fear that rises when you abandon a prefabricated future, and to leap into the unknown guided only by love of the truth.

That is the spiritual zeal that gave the Zealots their name, except they directed that inner roar outward into violence. That is what the world does; that is what the spiritual ego does: feeling attacked, invaded, and subjected to a lie, it attacks the external forms that lie takes. That is Don Quixote charging at windmills.

Jesus felt a zeal like this when he entered the temple and found it full of thieves, but he did not turn toward violence. Against whom could he be violent, if not himself? Violence has no place in Christ, who is One. The only possible response is a deeper surrender: to let love lead you toward the death of what you’ve been, so that a wider and truer life can open. In his zeal to see God's will done on earth, Jesus chose the cross.

Do not go gentle into that good night. Rage, rage against the dying of the light. That is how Dylan Thomas expressed this same zeal, speaking not only of the death of the body, but of all the ways we settle for noise instead of the clarity silence, judgment instead of forgiveness, the slavery of the senses instead of the freedom of the soul.

We fall slowly, without wanting to notice it, into a conformism that is death in life. A comfortable and routine way of being that we sense is not the truth. Truth makes all things new, and that way of being is covered in dust. A mediocre novel written over the face of the living God. For a time we choose to stay there out of fear of discomfort, fear of what others will say, fear of the unknown, fear of what Love says we are.

But not today.

The light has awakened to itself, nearly extinguished beneath the grey crust of the world.

Rage, rage against the dying of the light.

With all my love,

Antonio
