31. Clarity

Last Sunday, I turned 30. The number itself doesn’t matter to me, and I move toward maturity as happily as a child heading to the park. But it’s true that I’m noticing a change, like the one seen in nature when one season gives way to the next.

The forest is in full metamorphosis. Some green buds are starting to appear, and flowers I’ve never seen before are blooming. At the same time, the old, now useless leaves are beginning to turn yellow, orange, red, and eventually fall.

Last Thursday, I saw all the leaves on one wing of the forest fall, all at once. It was a terrifying and awe-inspiring spectacle. In a matter of minutes, part of my ego, my identity, my way of understanding the world, my way of functioning and treating myself, crumbled. Since then, I feel serenity and joy. Also, a very pleasant confusion that is not really confusion but clarity—a clarity of knowing that I know nothing. As a result, my mind is quieter, and my energy lighter.

Before that change happened, just before that branch of my ego cracked and fell, my emotional state was one of terror. My ego tried to show me how much I needed it. “Without me, your life will be a disaster,” it told me in a thousand ways. But deep down, I knew what was happening. The leap into the unknown was inevitable, and I had two options: resist and suffer, or trust and allow myself to be taken to a new place.

The ego resisting change is like someone waist-deep in the ocean trying to stop the waves from reaching the shore. That’s the ego trying to stop the evolution of the entire universe. It’s not a viable strategy, though one can insist on it to the point of exhaustion—and that’s suffering.

This time, I didn’t resist much, and truth stormed in as the phrase “Let all things be exactly as they are” passed through my mind. With that sign of radical acceptance, the demolition began. The fearful thoughts had nothing to cling to, meeting the luminous wall that this phrase represents. A part of my ego began to collapse. The change was so quick and deep that, within minutes, my posture transformed remarkably. The tension in my jaw, present since summer, completely dissolved, my chest opened up, and my shoulders relaxed. Even my scoliosis seemed to straighten out. By the end, I felt as if I had been reset to factory settings. I walked out of class like new. Yes, all of this happened during my counterpoint class at 9am. Evidently, I didn't catch a thing from the lecture.

Since then, my perspective has changed qualitatively. There are moments of such beauty that I am left speechless. The best thing is to look at other people. It doesn’t happen continuously yet, but I’ve had several glimpses where, when looking at someone, I saw their body, as always, but I saw something more, something indescribable and overwhelmingly beautiful, something timeless, something noble, something divine. The body is just an expression of that, an avatar that manifests what happens at that more subtle level. Looking at someone in that way is like seeing the light of heaven shine upon the world.

Despite the fascination I’m experiencing with this new shift in perspective, I feel I don’t have much more to say about it. At least not for now. I’ve made leaps like this in the past, and I know that over time a new understanding begins to crystallize into language. I’m not there yet, so for now, I simply sit and contemplate the beautiful views.

With all my love,

A.
