35. Speech for the Bride

A week ago, thinking about what to give my sister Cris for her wedding, I asked my heart, and the answer was: “don’t buy something, create something”. “A letter,” I thought, and I began to write. But in the first paragraph, I realized it wasn’t a letter; it was a speech. A few days later, I asked again: “Should I read it, or should I forget the music sheet and simply play?” “Just play,” my heart answered. The thought terrified me, but the heart knows what’s best, and I trusted that it would not only give me the courage to speak in front of 500 people but also the right words.

At the wedding, during the second course, I was feeling unwell. The jet lag, the long day, and especially greeting 200 people in two hours had left me noticeably exhausted. But my heart, inflamed by so much beauty, kept me on my feet and with the spirit to speak. But I asked again: “Is it better if I do it?” and it said no. The heart doesn’t give reasons; it dictates. After asking several times and seeing that the answer was clear, I understood that giving my speech simply wasn’t in harmony with the moment, so I let it pass.

Luckily, I have an audience here that I know will appreciate it. In fact, many of those present were at the wedding too. So I stand up, tap my glass like in the movies, and begin to speak:

I would like to invite you to imagine something with me. Bring your attention to your heart and picture it as a grand hall, perhaps in a museum or a mansion, with walls covered in photos and portraits of the people you hold dear and the memories you cherish there. What do you see? Whose faces appear?

[A few seconds of pause. I invite you to close your eyes and take a moment to look within.]

It’s good to pause for a moment and appreciate the treasure we all carry in our hearts.

This morning, while talking and praying with my sister Cris, we ended up doing something similar. What she saw were our grandparents: Abel, Lito, and Kiko. Kiko, too old for such an event, wasn’t going to make it to the wedding (he turned 97 yesterday). Abel and Lito are no longer physically with us, but they are in spirit. The three of them used that moment of stillness as an opportunity to come and give Cris a kiss.

This same process is what I’ve followed over the past few days to develop this speech. I took a long, quiet walk through that hall, exploring what I hold in my heart in relation with Cris.

There are things that the eyes of the body cannot show but that the heart can. When I look at you through the eyes of the body, I see you here, seated, radiant, almost floating, dressed like a lily. It’s a sight so beautiful it could bring anyone to tears. But it’s when I look at you through my heart that the true beauty is revealed.

When I see you through that love, I see all that you were, all that you are, and all that you will be. I see the essence, like an invisible thread, connecting all your versions—the eternal Cris that God created and that all of us who know and love you see and treasure. This is the Cris that Quique saw when one day he looked at you and saw in you his future wife, a vision so powerful it changed his path forever. And here we are today, all gathered, bearing witness.

I’ve been fortunate to follow the miracle of your life through all its stages, from the morning we went to meet you at the hospital as a newborn until today, and I’ve seen many things through my walk.

The first thing I saw is that you’re still a baby, Cris, and no matter how many branches your family tree may grow, in my heart, you still wear that pink bubble-shaped float strapped around your baby belly.

What I didn’t find there was a single conflict with you during our childhood. María and I chased each other around daily, like Tom and Jerry, but you had something untouchable about you. There was a purity, a grace in you, that made it impossible to get angry with you.

There was an angelic goodness in you, and in this world, which is a veil hiding heaven, that goodness cost you more than a few tears. "Too good" became one of the phrases we’d use to describe you. But that purity of heart, though sometimes difficult to navigate in a world ruled by other forces, also brought with it an immense gift that more than made up for any hardship. From a very young age, you had the gift of faith. You were the only one in the house to create a little altar, your coin de prière (prayer corner in French), as an expression of your innate devotion.

I remember the miracle of that time at a nun’s camp, when you lost an earring swimming in the sea. Days later, after the camp had ended, one of the nuns found it in the sand and sent it to you in a little matchbox. We are all beloved children of God; some of us know it, and some of us don’t. But you knew it; in you it was tangible.

That little girl is still present in all her grace and purity; she’s the one listening to me now, and she still takes the reins from time to time, like when you suddenly exclaim “Tono!” and out of nowhere jump into my arms to give me a hug. But it’s not just that little girl listening to me now; you’re also a young woman, taking the first steps of your own path. You start well-equipped, with a well-furnished mind, a heart courageous enough to be tender, a work ethic as solid as diamond, and a great partner who is your equal. Even with just a few steps, your path has already shone with the integrity with which you walk. There is something incorruptible in you—a Voice that speaks to you silently yet clearly, saying, “This is the way,” or “This isn’t right,” and you’ve already shown a heroic capacity to act accordingly.

I believe every human life has the potential to be a glorious adventure, but it only becomes one if you insist and remain steadfast in what you know to be true. That path begins with an intuition, a lily in the distance, as the poet Machado said—a dream of purity that you never quite reach but that guides you. It’s something only you can see, something that calls to you, even if to others your steps may sometimes seem those of a fool. But Machado’s fool doesn’t wander aimlessly; Machado’s fool moves forward through the fields of God. You know how to listen to that Voice that guides you. Nurture that patient listening, Cris. If you do, everything you need to know will arrive in your open heart when you need to know it. Until then, live in peace, feed the uncertainty that might come the fire of your faith, and wield the word “no” as often as necessary to keep your path clear of what you know is not true—of what doesn’t serve you, your family, and your shared vision.

In your essence is written not only who you were and who you are now, but also who you will be. This is not something mystical or esoteric; those of us who know you can see it clearly. I’m talking about the immense potential in you, Cris. I’m referring to the gifts you were created with, and your highest path is to cultivate them and offer them as gifts to the world. You are a gift to the world, Cris. Never fear sharing yourself as you are. The world around you will bloom whenever you do, and the faces that look at you will light up.

Once, in a moment of crisis, I asked you what your gifts were. I could see them, but I wanted to know if you could too, because staying in touch with them would serve as your guide. You gave a very valid answer—you spoke, with a bit of shyness, about your seemingly infinite capacity for effort, your quick ability to learn, or your knack for doing things well. These are undoubtedly great gifts, and they will take you wherever you want to go. But I was looking at a different kind of gifts in you, subtler, perhaps less valued by the society we live in, but ones I consider the most powerful because they make you a source of light in the world.

I’m talking about your immense empathy, which has been challenging to navigate, as you tend to take on the pain of others as your own. Don’t close it off; cultivate it—learn to navigate. That’s where the gift lies that allows everyone who spends time with you to leave feeling seen, embraced, accepted, and loved. I’m talking about the gift of looking at others with tenderness and awakening it in them. I’m talking about the ability to harmonize and elevate any space you’re in just with your presence. Today, seeing you walk into the church, we all had to hold onto our sits to keep from floating away, weightless. I’m talking about your ability to reconcile, to offer a hug in response to conflict, to forgive quickly and love unreservedly. That’s what I mean when I say you’re a gift to the world. These are the gifts that make you a great sister, a great partner, and will make you a great leader if you choose to be one. They are the gifts that will make your house a home for everyone. They are the gifts that will make you a truly great, great mother.

What one also sees when looking through the heart—and with this, I’ll finish—is the perfection of all things, moment by moment. We humans struggle to see this because we have too many ideas about what life "should" be. When you focus on what "should" be, you lose the ability to see "what is." But this is easier to perceive when looking at nature. So, indirectly, I’ll speak about you, about everyone, and especially about women, through the life of a lily.

It begins with planting a bulb in fertile soil, where it hydrates and absorbs nutrients. From there, the first shoots emerge, gathering sunlight through their leaves. That energy allows the stem to rise and eventually develop a magnificent white flower. Then, the petals begin to wither, revealing the seed capsule, ensuring reproduction. Finally, the plant enters a period of rest, storing its energy back in the bulb, where it waits to begin a new cycle.

What the world desires and values is the white, open lily, seen as a symbol of elegance and purity. Everything else seems like waiting, an investment, or a sad memory, something that already passed. But the eyes of the heart see things differently. Yes, the open lily is magnificent. But so is the bulb, which already contains the essence; the entire journey is already there. And so is the fertile soil and the sun that nourish it, giving and giving without expecting anything in return. And so is the stem and the invisible force that makes it rise from within. And so is the open, white flower and the sweet fragrance it fills the house with. And so are the withering petals, opening the way for new flowers to come into the world. And so is the final phase of retraction, back to the bulb, to the essence, where it doesn’t die but waits for a new life. Every part of that process is a perfect sculpture of what it is in each moment. It is the miracle of life, and in every stage, it shines with a dignity, a selfless devotion, a mystery so profound that when one learns to see it this way, the mind falls silent, and all that’s left to do is to give thanks and bless it—which is the same as looking at it with love. And with that, I finish.

Thank you, Cris and Quique, for being the way you are and for living so authentically. Thank you for getting married and placing such a radical love at the center of your lives as your guide and path. Thank you to everyone here, on behalf of both families, for your affection and for sharing with us the joy of such an extraordinarily beautiful day. I wish you all a Merry Christmas and hope you visit often the treasure you carry in your chests. God bless you all. And cheers to the bride and groom!

With all my love,

A.
