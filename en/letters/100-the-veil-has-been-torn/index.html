The Veil Has Been Torn

On abundance and the illusion of guilt that blocks it

100 letters with Esferas!

Thank you from the bottom of my heart for being here with me.

Today I bring a powerful letter, an expression of a catharsis I experienced last weekend at an event led by a wonderful young man named Nacho Sala.
For some time, I had been hitting my head into two walls: on the one hand, a strong resistance to doing my spiritual practices; on the other, difficulties in my relationship with money, which threatened to hinder my embryonic professional path.

This weekend I saw that, in truth, those two walls were actually the same one, manifesting in two different dimensions. Once I saw this clearly, I took a leap of faith and discovered what lay on the other side: the acceptance of abundance.

In this letter I share my inner process, in the hope that it may be of help to you if you also struggle with that.

Once again, thank you for being here.

With all my love,

Antonio

Ten years ago, just before the epiphany that revealed to me that the Love of God is the essence of reality, if you had asked me about the meaning of life I would have answered: “nothing, we are nothing.”

Even after that first, overwhelming encounter with God, I continued preaching nothingness. I remember ending a radio interview that same year by saying, “…because we are nothing.” Clearly, I didn’t get what God had wanted to show me in that experience!

My soul had awakened to the truth, but my mind still had a long journey ahead to integrate that radically opposite vision: from nothing to all that is.

Ten years later, my mind has been transformed and convinced that we do not exist in the world, but in God. There is no earthly law that the Love of God cannot override. There is no limitation, nothing impossible in God. Miracles have become the order of the day. It is increasingly evident that my life is guided not by my limited, linear mind, but by a non-linear intelligence that includes everyone and everything.

And yet, something didn’t add up. If a few years ago I deeply enjoyed my sadhana (my morning spiritual practice), it had now become an increasingly heavy burden. I had reached a wall so high and so wide that I didn’t know how to cross it. The Voice that speaks for God told me again and again, “Rise,” but I didn’t know how to do it.

Last week, my soul expressed itself at the piano with these verses:

When will it stop weighing so much?
Is my soul just a lily crushed beneath the mud?
There is life in me I do not yet know
Like a veil against the wind
Waiting to be torn
Rise in Me
Open yourself to Me

And then something unexpected happened, a true deus ex machina. My friend Nico, a Berklee friend and qigong companion, wrote to me last week to tell me he was coming to Madrid for an event led by a guy named Nacho Sala, a student of Dr. Hawkins, speaking about entrepreneurship. I told him he was more than welcome to stay at my place, but I declined his invitation to join the plan. Arrogance spoke: “I’ve studied enough; now it’s time to work.”

I picked him up at the airport on Saturday at midday, and he renewed his invitation. This time I asked my heart, “Lord, do You want me to go?”, and my heart expanded and burst into laughter.

About 30 hours later, I walked out of Nacho’s event transformed. The veil had finally been torn, and now that I was on the other side of the wall, I could see what it was: the fear of abundance, the guilt around wanting things of this world, and, behind it all, the fear of God.

Let me explain.

(But first, a cat.)

In this material world, to give implies to lose. If I give you €10, I now have €10 less. In the same way, receiving something implies that someone else now has less. But in our subconscious, it is not only another human who ends up with less, it is God Himself. To receive something is equivalent to taking something away from God. And now, within the thought system of separation, God is going to come and take back what is His and destroy me. This unconscious belief is foundational to the ego, yet it lies buried deep in our mind. I discovered it two months ago, but this past weekend I finally dared to let it go and see the truth it was hiding.

The ego is the belief shared by all our human minds that we are separate from God. That is the original sin, but instead of interpreting it as an absurd mistake, we interpret it as something guilty, something that deserves punishment. That guilt is so immense and so uncomfortable that the mind denies it, pushes it into the subconscious, and from there projects it onto the world outside. Now the guilty ones are others. Then the ego reacts with anger, but again it does not take responsibility for it; it represses it and projects it. Now the angry and vengeful ones are others, and they want to attack us. Thus the idea of a Punishing God is born, a God who supposedly wants to punish us for our sins. This is obviously a projection of the subconscious and has nothing to do with the reality of God, who is only Love. But the ego reacts to its own projection, feels fear, raises its shield, and hides in a world of shadows ruled by the unholy trinity: guilt, anger, and fear.

From that thought system, how can one accept abundance in their life? To receive a lot would be equivalent to increasing God’s guilt and anger. Obviously, the ego (which is a denial of God) will not interpret it this way; instead it will say things like: “I have enough with little; wanting more is selfish,” “If I do well, I will betray those I love,” “If I am abundant, I will lose my humility,” “Others need it more than I do,” “You can’t become rich with integrity,” “If I receive something good, something bad will come afterward,” “I am not enough; others deserve abundance, but I don’t,” and a long list of justifications. The truth is that behind all those masks of prudence, humility, spirituality, or morality, the ego is simply protecting its own existence: it is better not to receive, because receiving and seeing that life is abundant and prosperous would question the basic belief in scarcity, guilt and separation.

By following the voice of the ego, we choose scarcity and self-sabotage. That chronic lack becomes our comfort zone and manifests in every area of our lives. Financially, we choose struggle. In intimate relationships, we settle for mediocre love. In health, we do whatever is necessary to maintain the undesirable conditions that make us suffer. But above all, it represents an enormous spiritual blockage, because lack and the Love of God are radically incompatible.

So what happened this weekend? What shift took place in my mind that unlocked and dismantled this entire paradigm? It all came from opening myself, with total honesty, to explore the question: Why do I feel guilt when I desire money?

By saying to God, “Your love is not enough; let me look in the world for what I lack,” we have made money into an idol. In the world, money is the god of abundance, security, power, and possibility.

As long as we desire that money-idol, guilt will be inevitable, because unconsciously we will know that we are saying to God, “No thanks, I prefer this.”

The problem is not money itself, but the meaning we have given it. It is crucial to stop seeing money as the source of security, peace of mind, abundance, and all those qualities that can truly only be found in God.

If we survive and if we do well, it is thanks to God, not to money.

How can we identify, then, whether we are seeing money through the ego? Ask yourself: What do I want this money for? What am I seeking in it?

If the answer is to have peace of mind, or to feel safe, or to have power in the world, or simply for personal purposes that have nothing to do with your inner growth, then you will feel guilt when you desire it, you will block it, and it will not arrive.

The key is that you are not the ego and, in truth, you are not seeking those qualities in money because you already have them. They are your divine inheritance, they are what you are, and no amount of money could substitute that. In reality, you have no personal purposes, because you are not the individual person your ego tells you that you are. To desire something for illusory reasons is as if you did not desire it at all. Do your dreams at night have any consequences in your waking life?

What happened this weekend is that I recognized this entire egoic structure and rejected it. I stopped projecting false meanings onto money. I took away all its power to give me those essential qualities and returned them to God. Then I looked at money, now completely neutral and insignificant, and prayed, “Holy Spirit, show me the true meaning and purpose of money.”

And what I saw was what I have seen with everything we hand over to the Holy Spirit: “I am here to serve you.” What we once used to move away from God now becomes the path toward Him. What was once a symbol of guilt and fear now becomes a symbol of love.

The purpose of money is to use it with love, as if it were water we use to cultivate our own life and the lives of others. You are a gardener of yourself and of the beings around you, and money, used in this way, can help them flourish.

The way to transcend fear and guilt around abundance, then, is to place it at the service of God, helping others or ourselves grow in that direction. And when guilt appears, instead of repressing it and projecting it, we choose to forgive ourselves. Guilt does not come from God, and it is an illusion that, if we ask, will joyfully be removed from our mind.

From this perspective, money no longer has anything to do with personal survival. That is in God’s hands. Now it is simply the rain that comes from the sky to water all the flowers in the garden.

Abundance, then, has nothing to do with money or with anything external, but with an inner recognition. God created us by extending His love and His Being. That is our essential nature. That is what was revealed to me in the epiphany of my twenty-first year, and today I am finally beginning to understand how that is lived in the world.

God’s will for us is to accept that total fullness, to recognize, each one of us, that we are the Kingdom of Heaven in its entirety. In the world, giving and having are different things. In Heaven, they are the same thing: you have everything because you are everything.

This world was created by the ego to escape from the supposed wrath of God. Its deceptions (problems, illnesses, lack, fear, guilt, anger…) are the chains that keep our will and our being bound to the world. “There is no time for God, look at how many problems there are to solve!”

My chains have been breaking for a while, and I’ve begun to look beyond and recognize the extension of my Being, which reaches beyond the physical universe. The universe appears within me, not the other way around.

And at the same time, here was this young human being, standing on planet Earth, washing dishes while worrying about how to make a living. That is inevitable for a mind identified with the ego.

Now I see the madness of that belief, the terrible falseness of that guilt, which I now categorically deny and banish from my mind forever.

Now I see that the resistance to my spiritual practices came from my resistance to abundance. One cannot open to the infinite abundance that God is if one believes that, in doing so, one is risking divine punishment. That is why I did not want to practice qigong, not out of laziness, not out of weakness, but out of fear of the inner abundance that this path inevitably brings.

God does not want me to renounce the riches of the world, but the false meaning the ego has given them. This week I toppled the statue of the supreme idol of this world, money, and in doing so I opened my mind to abundance. I deny that the outer world has power over my inner peace, over my inherent state of abundance, joy, and security. Those are attributes of God, and I return them to where they belong. The dark wall of guilt and fear that separated my spiritual life from my earthly life has fallen.

The veil has been torn.

I no longer allow false identifications in my mind, ideas of smallness, lack, guilt, illness, attack, and fear, to cloud what I know I am. I have seen the error, and I ask that my mind be healed and aligned with the truth. I am a holy child of God, and denying that is not humility but the pride of the ego. It is to fall into the absurd illusion that I am outside the totality, separated from God. It is to remove myself from the Holy Trinity, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and say that, in addition to that, there is Antonio. No: to affirm Antonio as my ultimate identity is to deny that I come from God, that He is the source, essence, and only reality of my Being. Antonio is part of me. But so are you, and to the same degree. There is nothing that separates us.

The veil has been torn.

My spiritual path now is about aligning my earthly life with the Kingdom of God that I am within. To live in such a way that my life is a demonstration that the law of the world (“to give is to lose”) is false. The law I choose to embody is that “to give is to receive.” That is the law of love. That is the law of what I am. If anything within me resists that truth, trying to hide it once again, I hand it over to God now so that He may dissolve it in His love.

The veil has been torn.

Today, at last, I rise and wash the dishes in peace, knowing who I am and Who is with me.

With all my love,

A.
