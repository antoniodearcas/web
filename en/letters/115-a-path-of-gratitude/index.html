115. A Path of Gratitude

Always and everywhere

The good thing about not going to Mass very often is that when I do, I hear everything with fresh ears. Things I had been hearing my whole life in a semi-unconscious way suddenly surprise me. That was the case the other day, when I heard some words about gratitude.

At 21 I had already been contemplating the nature of reality for some time, and had developed some deep intuitions: that my mind was not contained inside my head, but was more like an antenna that could tune into different frequencies of thought; or that perception was in fact projection. But my first intuition of God, of the Father Creator, came to me one day while eating a steak.

I was in my last year of industrial engineering in Chicago. Alone in my apartment, watching the snow fall in slow motion on the other side of the window, with some pleasant music playing and eating a good steak. At that time I considered myself an atheist, but suddenly I felt a deep gratitude for the beauty of that moment, and it confronted me with the inevitable question: who am I grateful to? If I am appreciating this moment as though it were a gift, who gave it to me?

It is true that, if I was there at all, it was thanks to my father's financial support, so in part it was to him. But who could I thank for the beauty of that moment, the snow, the solitude, the music?

Without yet understanding it, my heart was opening and orienting itself again toward its origin, toward Home.

[10 YEARS LATER]

The other day I attended the wedding of a good friend. My joy and love for him, together with the beauty of the music and the church, made my heart open more than usual. The antenna of my mind tuned into the Spirit, and I began to listen with greater depth.

I then heard something I had heard hundreds of times before, but had never truly listened to until that moment:

It is truly right and just,

our duty and our salvation,

always and everywhere to give you thanks,

Lord, holy Father,

almighty and eternal God...

This is what I heard:

It is right.

It is right because God is the only source of all that is good. Being grateful to something created (like my earthly father) is already very good in itself, but in absolute terms it is stopping halfway: like thanking a reflection for the light of the Sun.

It is necessary.

God does not need our gratitude, but we do. Without it, we cannot appreciate what is good, and our perception gradually grows dark. Gratitude orients our mind toward God, who is the source of all good. Without it, we are denying that source, and therefore rejecting the good itself. Without gratitude we can accumulate treasures in this world, but not treasures of the heart.

It is our duty.

In this world we have many duties, all pulling at one another in an attempt to capture our attention. But in the true context of our existence, the Spirit, we have only one duty: to remember that we are one with God, our Self, and with all of Creation. A Course in Miracles calls this unique function "forgiveness." Dr. Hawkins called it "letting go." Here it is called "giving thanks." No matter the tradition, at heart it is all the same: releasing the grey interpretation the ego gives to this moment and choosing to contemplate it in the light of God. Everything is a gift. Do that always, and you will have fulfilled your destiny perfectly, regardless of the outward shape your life takes in this world.

It is our salvation.

Salvation from what? From the nightmare that results from believing we are separate from God. Gratitude reveals that, in reality, there is no separation at all. The union between what we give thanks for and God is more intimate than the union between light and the Sun. To be grateful to God is to be in Him.

Give thanks ALWAYS, and in ALL places.

Being grateful for pleasant things is good. But in my experience, gratitude has been most powerful when the situation has been hardest: when the pain seemed unbearable and hope was fading completely. In those moments, it is the ego that suffers and fights for its existence; but beyond it, your soul, your Spirit, crosses the threshold of a new birth. The most powerful thing you can do to pass through that ring of fire and completely dissolve resistance to transformation is to give thanks to God.

Then the happiness of the Spirit illuminates the mind and embraces the supposed misfortune. Pain and joy coexist like clouds and sky. Under that light, one sees that the situation is like the bitter but necessary medicine that an infinitely loving doctor brings to heal us.

A thank-you at the right moment has the power to transform catastrophe into blessing; it has the power to reveal that every instant is God wanting to offer us his gifts. A true thank-you opens the doors of our mind to His Presence, and lets the heart be filled with treasures.

A final note: what is worth giving thanks for?

Although it is good to be grateful for the things we have, we must observe whether our gratitude comes at the expense of others. Some examples might be feeling grateful for being healthy when someone falls ill, or for financial comfort when thinking of others living in poverty. That kind of gratitude, at the expense of what others lack, is in reality a form of attack. It is believing that God has favorites and treats some people more favorably than others. That is not the real God. The sun shines on everyone equally.

To be grateful to that partial god is to give thanks to something unreal, and therefore it is as though one were not grateful at all. The happiness derived from that form of gratitude comes from the ego, not the Spirit. It feels like temporary relief, but not like fullness.

If we want to experience true gratitude, the kind that opens the heart and unites our mind with its source, we must give thanks for the things we all receive equally.

We must give thanks that, although we tend to forget God, He never forgets us.

That his infinite embrace is always ready to heal our mind of its errors.

We must give thanks for the ways in which he guides us, out of darkness and toward the light, along the path most suited to each of us.

For how he gives us strength to face the difficulties of this world.

We must give thanks for the ways in which his light manifests in our lives, whatever form it takes.

We must give thanks even when we understand nothing, and especially then, when we understand nothing and are suffering. That thank-you will be like a prayer asking God: "show me what is real, for I have lost my way."

And we can also give thanks for the key itself: the word "thank you." One of the first words we ever learned, and probably the last we will say when everything is finally resolved at the end of time.

With all my love,

Antonio
