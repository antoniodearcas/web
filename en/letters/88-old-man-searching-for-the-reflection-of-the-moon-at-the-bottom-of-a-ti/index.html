88. Old Man Searching for the Reflection of the Moon at the Bottom of a Tide Pool

Finding The Middle Way

“The only devils in the world are those running around in our own hearts, and that is where all our battles should be fought.”

- Mahatma Gandhi

I’m doing a qigong practice called Old Man Searching for the Reflection of the Moon at the Bottom of a Tide Pool. “Old Man Tide Pool” for short. It’s a powerful practice designed to voluntarily trigger all the trapped emotions in our system in order to purge them. Traditionally, it’s done every night for 40 days, during which one must avoid alcohol and conserve sexual energy.

A year ago, I tried it, and by the ninth day I had to stop because things came up that were so difficult to process that I got scared and quit.

This time, I’m on day 37, and it has been quite a purgatory.

Why do I put myself through something like this?

Good question. Emotions that we don’t consciously process accumulate in our energy system. From there, they cause all kinds of problems. They become hidden determinants of our behavior, demons that haunt and manipulate us from the shadows, physical illnesses, addictions, interpersonal conflicts, neuroses… They are also the veils that cloud our vision of the world. When we look outside and see things that anger us, sadden us, or make us anxious… all we are seeing are our own filters.

Personally, I didn’t want to do the practice and face all of that. But there is an inner Voice that fears nothing and guided me to do it, promising to stay with me throughout the entire process.

Yesterday, after 36 days of practice, I started “doing yoga” at 7 a.m. All I did was stay still on my mat, feeling too tired to do anything other than Child’s Pose. I was indulging in my complaints about how long and hard the path is when the Voice returned and said:

"If you knew how close you are… the path to Heaven is certain. The only thing within your power is to delay it more or less. The way one delays is by resisting cooperation, and that is the cause of suffering.

You feel tired because you believe the path is long. But the truth is that only this moment exists. You are only asked to take care of one instant at a time. Moreover, exhaustion comes exclusively from resistance, so resisting more does not bring rest but more tension.

Let me carry you. You just breathe and observe. Do not force anything, nor despair. Only turn to Me when you feel lost, and I will take the reins."

I leapt out of my defeated yogi posture and wrote all of this down. I sat and contemplated those words, letting them sink into my mistaken mind. I realized then that I had been hearing similar ideas for days.

After class, a friend said to me a phrase that Siddhartha heard from a musician about 2,600 years ago:

"If you tighten the string too much, it breaks.
If you loosen it too much, it won’t sound."

Those words made him understand that asceticism was not the way. Through this, he discovered his great teaching, the Middle Way. Soon after, having found the true path, he “became” the Buddha.

The words of Nisargadatta Maharaj have also come to mind these days:

"First you must make an effort to realize that effort is useless. Only then can you rest in what has always been."

And the closest, though not necessarily easiest to apply, from Dr. Hawkins:

"You can just be what you are and relax into it."

Okay, I think I get it!

Paradoxically, last night I did my practice, and it was the first day I did not only Old Man Tide Pool but also other exercises, spontaneously and effortlessly. Am I reaching the place where one understands that to attain truth, all effort must be abandoned? Is that what the Voice meant by “If you knew how close you are…”?

I don’t know.

What I do know is that, after the effort of writing this letter, I’m going to take a nap.

Happy Sunday.

With all my love,

A.
