139. An Unsexy Spirituality

A single true sentence gave me the strength I needed.

The rock, or the tide of emotions?
The rock, or the flames of passion?
The rock, or the lightning of inspiration?
If I must walk every day, let it be on rock.

In my letter "132. To Row, or Not to Row," I told how a spectacular unblocking had taken place in my solar plexus, the center related to doing and to "I can." I uncovered a belief I had been holding for several years: that effort comes from the ego; in God, everything unfolds with ease and inspiration.

I'm not saying it is false or true; it is limited, and that limitation was like a rope binding my wings. What beliefs do is tell God how reality should be. That is why, if you want to know reality, you have to let go of all beliefs.

When I let go of that one, enormous changes happened in my life. I now get done in a day what used to take me a week. Now the problem is that I find it hard to stop!

I have also discovered the supreme importance of developing character. I used to confuse it with the ego; why put so much effort into improving what is fleeting? Now I see more clearly what it is: virtues do not live in the same dimension as the vices of character, just as the sky does not live in the same dimension as the clouds, or light in the same dimension as shadows. It is not about improving what is fleeting, but about letting it go so that what is eternal can shine. To truly integrate spirituality and be able to do God's will on Earth as it is in Heaven, the development of character is central.

What I love about this shift in perspective is, first, how unsexy it is. I used to think liberation lay in seeking something ideal: always working inspired, always letting a voice beyond the clouds tell me what the next thing to do was. Now I simply try to do what I have to do. And that, which I once would have felt as oppressive, is now tremendously freeing, because my action no longer depends on my fluctuating, capricious moods, or on my personal likes and dislikes. It now depends on truth, and it seeks the good simply because it is good.

A few days after this awakening, this wonderful triptych came to me, mentioned almost in passing in a book by Romano Guardini. When I heard it, I knew it was my new true north:

What is, is.
What is right must be done.
What has been entrusted to me, I uphold.

I felt the power of truth in those words when, the next day, I spent several hours cleaning the terrace, something I had been avoiding for weeks. A single true sentence gave me the strength I needed: “What is right must be done.”

What I love about this shift in perspective is, second, that it is filling me with hope. And I recognize it for what it is, a theological virtue, a light I cannot make on my own: a gift from God. The gift is looking to the future and no longer seeing my past mistakes repeating themselves, my projected guilt coming back at me like rabid dogs, my limitations hurting me and those I love: now I look to the future and see, behind the opaque curtain of the unknown, a tender smile.

With all my love,

Antonio
