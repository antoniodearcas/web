87. The "I Am" that we share

A meditation in word, piano, and painting.

“Sometimes I came across a tree which seemed like a Buddha or a Jesus: loving, compassionate, still, unambitious, enlightened… the tree is my church, the tree is my temple, the tree is my mantra, the tree is my poem and my prayer.”

- Satish Kumar

Almost a year ago, on Thanksgiving night, I went out for a walk before dinner. I had been cooking all afternoon and was feeling stressed. I took a walk through the park to rebalance my energy, but seeing that I still couldn’t let go of the tension, I thought of touching a tree.

Let me explain. Shoes electrically insulate us from the ground, which cuts off our grounding. Touching the earth, a rock, or a tree has this neutralizing effect. So whenever I was stressed on my way to a class, if I could, I would touch a tree to release the excess energy. But that day was the last time I did it, because my understanding changed profoundly.

Let’s return to my walk.

I approached an oak nearby, placed my hands, one on each side of the trunk, closed my eyes, and directed my awareness to the energetic plane. What I felt then, completely unexpectedly, was the fusion of my consciousness with that of the tree. I felt its branches and roots as clearly as I felt my arms and legs. Its life was exactly the same as mine. In that state of communion, I saw that we were, in fact, one Life, without any boundary separating us.

After the initial surprise, a brief wordless conversation took place, but with a clear meaning that I translate here.

It began with an unusual greeting, yet one that perfectly expressed our communion:

“I love you.”

Then the tree showed me what it was: I felt what it was like to perform photosynthesis through thousands of leaves.

I responded by showing it what I was: this is what singing is like.

It replied, “Ah, like the birds!”

“Yes, exactly! Like the birds.”

And no more was said; we simply remained in silence, enjoying our time together.

After that experience, out of respect, I never again discharged energy I didn’t want onto any tree, just as I try not to do onto any other living being.

Today I bring a video I recorded this week with Oceun, a brilliant pianist and fellow student from Berklee. It was a project for the class Music, Spirit & Transformation. The book we’ve been reading is called Spiritual Ecology: The Cry of the Earth, a collection of essays on the need for a paradigm shift in our relationship with nature.

The conventional understanding, called materialism, conceives of nature as an object: a collection of resources more or less useful to human beings.

Spiritual ecology, on the other hand, sees nature as a subject. The same Self that manifests in us also expresses itself in every plant, every tree, every animal, every river, and in everything that exists on Earth.

Although my path has unfolded almost entirely in the city, when one deepens in the knowledge of the Self, one begins to recognise this Being everywhere. Fortunately, there are also trees in the city, and I have been given the gift to experience what Satish Kumar describes in the quote at the beginning. But my experiences of communion have not been limited to what we usually consider “living beings.” If something “is”, there is the Self, expressing its essence through a form: a carpet, a bench, a cloud, a squirrel, a tree, a human being.

For the project, I felt called to express the essence of this radical subjectivity: the “I Am” consciousness that permeates and is the essence of the entire universe.

The result is this video, which begins with an invocation to that “I Am,” offering our gifts so that it might express itself through us. Then, Oceun improvised on the piano while I painted a piece from beginning to end.

I hope you enjoy it and that it brings you a moment of contemplation and presence. The entire video is an invocation to the “I Am,” which is your true Self, before the mind clothes itself in finite and temporary qualities.

With all my love,

A.
