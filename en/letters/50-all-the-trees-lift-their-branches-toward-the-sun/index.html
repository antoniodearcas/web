50. All the trees lift their branches toward the Sun.

On how we bend ourselves to find light where it is not

“If you put God second, you put Him nowhere.”

All the trees in the world lift their branches toward the Sun. They naturally tend toward that which gives them life.

It is endearing to see city trees adapting to the circumstances they’ve been given: living in the shadow of towering buildings. These trees bend, turning away from the buildings, seeking that ray of sunlight that passes through their street every day at noon.

Trees are simple: they follow their own law, one that is not subject to distortion. But imagine for a moment that it were. Imagine that I went up to Serefé, the oak tree that lives outside my window, and said:

“Hey, Serefé, I’ve heard that the best light isn’t the one that comes from the Sun, but the one that emanates from the TVs in my building. If you turn toward the windows, you might catch a bit of that light at night when my neighbors watch their shows.” Then Serefé, being completely innocent, says, “Oh! Thank you!” And from then on, she begins to bend her trunk and branches toward the building, seeking the glow of mere pixels. Little by little, she hides herself from the light of the Sun, which now, in her mind, doesn’t seem so important. Over time, after looking in another direction for so long, she eventually forgets about it.

I could walk through the park and keep deceiving trees:

- Don’t drink the water from the river! It’s better to finish the cans that the kids left scattered around your roots. That’ll give you much more energy.

- Oh, thank you! Sun bless you!

And to another, I could say:

- Hey, the tree next to you is stealing all your light. It’s an invasive species. If instead of using your energy to grow, you use it to strangle its roots, you’ll stop its growth, and then there will be more light for you.

- Oh, I hadn’t thought of that! Thanks!

- You’re welcome, tree!

And so, by distorting the truth, I would lead these innocent trees toward alternative, novel, supposedly more valuable paths—pulling them away from what is simple, true, and good, from that which truly nourishes and sustains them.

This is what Lucifer represents. The word Lucifer means “the one who brings light.” It is that which brings false promises and half-truths, leading you away from what is real, from the true source of life.

The Sun does not argue or seek to convince the trees of the quality of its light. The Sun simply shines. Truth does not shout. Truth is not anti-anything. Truth simply is, without opposition, and it is always available for those who wish to drink from it.

Fortunately for life on this planet, trees follow their own law, and it guides them infallibly toward their own good and the good of all.

We, however, play a much more complex game. Our mind, which determines our behavior, is subject to tremendous distortions of truth. The only problem of man is ignorance. That is the only "sin"—not something deserving of punishment, but something in need of correction.

Let’s return to Serefé. Two years have passed since my deception, and her body has twisted and sunk into the shadow of my building. She is languishing, falling ill, growing sad. Her mind searches for solutions to her suffering, first looking outward:

"Why hasn’t apartment 42 watched their show today? Don’t they know I need that light?"

Desperate, she starts to realize she has no control over what my neighbors watch or don’t watch. Finally, one day, bent over in pain, she surrenders.

Having lost hope, she stops looking outward for solutions.

Her mind turns inward.

For the first time in years, she feels her body.

And then she notices something she had forgotten—something extraordinary. On one of her branches, the one furthest from the building, a ray of sunlight is touching her leaves. She realizes that must have happened every day, but in her desperate search, she had stopped noticing. She had been hypnotized, her attention anchored in the wrong place.

Astonished, with a tremendous lower-trunk pain but an even greater joy, she begins to straighten herself, seeking to expose a little more of herself each day to that delightful beam of light.

"Ah! This was it! But how could I have lost sight of it when it was here all along?"

Perhaps she will remember the conversation in which I deceived her, or perhaps she won’t. But that doesn’t matter. She has found the true light again, and nothing else matters—nothing is lacking.

With all my love,

A.
