137. Constant Prayer

Consciously or unconsciously, ask and it will be given to you.

The other day, a member of the community asked me about how to spend more time praying or being aware of the Presence.

The answer was that, in reality, we are always praying.

Sometimes we pray explicitly, through thoughts or out loud, but we are always doing it implicitly. The mechanism is always on. At every moment, there is some desire or longing in our heart, and that is the constant prayer.

When we pray well, that is, redirecting the longing of the heart toward the true God, we receive what we have asked for: his peace and the memory of his love. However, when we stop praying, our heart returns to its habitual prayers, which tend to be oriented toward the things of this world, things that must be in the future because we do not feel them now. Our prayer then moves from the true God to all kinds of false gods: in our heart we pray to the god of productivity and success, because only he can save us from scarcity and insufficiency. We pray to the god of pleasure, because he will give us the joy we lack right now. We pray to the god of approval, dreaming that he will confirm to us “you are enough, you are good.” We pray to the god of control, so that nothing bad will happen to us.

There are many false gods, and none of them are real. Which means our prayers receive emptiness as a response. External things arrive and soon pass; but the missing puzzle piece in our heart remains not-there.

But the maxim always holds: ask, and it will be given to you.

In every instant, you are receiving the answer to the prayer of your heart. It never fails, it cannot fail. You are seeing what you have asked for.

Do you understand now why all religions place such emphasis on opening the heart? If we never go in there to put things in order, it will be the disorder that raises its prayer, and disorder is what we will receive in return.

If you put God second, you put him nowhere.

The ultimate goal of prayer is to learn to ask only for the peace of God.

Saint Augustine said that happiness consists in desiring only what is good, and having all our desires fulfilled.

One could debate endlessly about what is good, but the second part of the Doctor’s phrase gives us a clue: how can we always have all our desires fulfilled? It can only be if what we desire is always present, always given, and merely waiting to be accepted.

That is why the conclusion I reach is this: there is nothing outside this instant that I truly want to desire.

With all my love,

Antonio
