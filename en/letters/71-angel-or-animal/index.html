71. Angel or Animal

Where do we come from?

One of Dr. Hawkins' most wonderful contributions is his explanation of the evolution of consciousness on this planet.

The first form of life was the bacteria, which is guided by three basic impulses: seeking nutrients, avoiding threats, and reproducing.

There lies the heart of the ego.

From that seed, the tree of life on this planet began to unfold: invertebrates, fish, amphibians, reptiles, dinosaurs, birds, mammals… until it reached us. Each new species didn’t start from scratch, but rather reorganized and added improvements to the structure it had inherited.

There is a simplified model for understanding our brain called the “triune brain,” which serves as a helpful metaphor for understanding ourselves. According to this model, our brain has at its core the reptilian brain, surrounded by the limbic system (associated with mammals), and finally the neocortex, associated with humans.

In other words, at the center of our brain, there is a dinosaur. That part of you only cares about dominating its territory, hunting, defending itself, and mating. All of it in an instinctive, automatic way.

The mammalian brain has more to do with emotions and relationships. It is here that maternal love and emotional memory appear for the first time.

Lastly, the neocortex is responsible for logical thinking, language, planning, imagination, reflection, spirituality, and the ability to play with symbols.

The funny thing about the human experience is that all of these brains are active at the same time. The conflict is built-in.

Put even more simply, we could say that the conflict is between the animal and the angel within us. Both come from radically different origins.

The animal comes from a harsh evolutionary process, on a planet where the energy needed for survival must be obtained from the outside. Animal life is marked by lack, fear, attack, and constant struggle. In that world, everything is scarce; the search for food, space, protection, or mates never ends. It is a world of mere survival, where only the strongest prevail. There is no time to pause or contemplate; here, one must act fast, driven by instincts and emotions. From this comes our almost essential conditioning: the impulsive tendency to seek outside what we feel is missing inside. This is the world of fear.

The angel, however, comes from a place that is the complete opposite: fullness. The source of life is not outside, waiting to be conquered day after day, but within, eternally. The natural environment of the angel is unity, abundance, clarity, peace... Attack has no place where there is only oneness. If there is only one, who would there be to attack? Part of this spiritual nature is also the power of intention, which is the essence of freedom. This is the world of love.

This duality is also known as the battle between ego and Spirit. The ego is a product of animal evolution on this planet. It is neither good nor bad. It is simply what it is; what it had to be in order to adapt and survive. The problem is that once we access the Spirit, we see that there are more peaceful and harmonious ways to live, but the ego still wants to keep its "good old habits."

And so here we are, humans, with a consciousness that has access to both worlds.

Where do we come from?

In general, this world is ruled by the ego; we all begin there, as innocent babies concerned only with the most basic needs of the body.

But sometimes consciousness awakens and, superimposed upon the animal nature, recognizes its spiritual nature. A conversation (or perhaps a battle) then begins for the sovereignty of the mind.

The animal uses attack (and sometimes urine) to conquer and maintain its territory. The angel does not attack or impose; it simply offers a loving alternative that can be freely chosen.

The animal screams, complains, and laments. It feels threatened by the Spirit. It manipulates and distorts both truth (ignorance) and energy (negative emotions) to maintain its dominance.

The angel is silent and patient. It does not defend itself, truth has no need for defense. It truly does nothing. It simply is.

The path begins when we stop obeying the voice of the animal exclusively. When we hear the voice of fear and say: no, thank you, this time I choose trust and love.

The path advances every time we let go of our identification with the animal and choose to recognize our true self (and that of others) in the angel. In other words, the path advances when we forgive or let go.

The conflict ends when we give our mind completely to one of the two sides. At every moment, we are serving one or the other; there is no middle ground.

To whom do we bow?

The animal or the angel?

The ego or the Spirit?

Fear or love?

With all my love,

A.
