Judgment and the Light It Hides

Or how we unknowingly darken our own mind

"Do not judge, and you will not be judged; do not condemn, and you will not be condemned; forgive, and you will be forgiven." — Luke 6:37

This week, I received the gift of seeing the meaning of these words on a deeper level—the gift of clearly perceiving a mechanism that had remained hidden until now.

When one reads these words of Jesus, it seems as if He is saying that if you judge someone, sooner or later, the universe will return the harm you have given. "You reap what you sow." But what I saw this week could be explained more precisely like this:

  • Do not judge, because you will inevitably judge yourself in the same way.

  • Do not condemn, because without realizing it, you will be calling upon the same condemnation for yourself.

  • Forgive others, and you will have forgiven yourself.

Let me explain. Since you and I share the same nature and, ultimately, are one Being, when I judge you, I am also judging myself—exactly to the same degree.

If I call you "lazy," I am assuming that our nature includes the possibility of being lazy. So, even if I do not generally see myself as a lazy person, sooner or later, I will find myself tired or facing something I do not want to do. In that moment, the judgment I once projected outward will turn back on me and say: "You are lazy."

The reality is that no one is truly lazy. Would you say that a stagnant river is lazy? Or that a plant that has not received enough sunlight is lazier than another that has and, therefore, has grown more?

With humans, however, it is incredibly easy to attach these kinds of labels. "Lazy!" "A bad person!" As Carl Jung said, "Thinking is difficult, that’s why most people judge." And indeed, judging is easy. It gives us a false sense of control and knowledge, and it also grants us a subtle but pleasant feeling of superiority.

What we fail to see is the effect that each of our judgments has on our own mind. Even though we may not have claimed it yet, we have full authority over our mind due to our divine origin. Every judgment we cast upon another is a decree in our own inner kingdom, dictating under what illusory conditions we do not deserve to be loved and, in fact, deserve to be punished. Every judgment we cast upon another is like a cloud that appears in our own mind, obscuring the light of our innocence. It hides the light of the world that we truly are. With each judgment, we lose sight of it a little more, and our world becomes a little darker.

The ego will interpret this diminishing light as being caused by whatever so-and-so has done to you, which will only reinforce your judgment. The truth is that nothing and no one outside of you has the slightest power to take away your peace, joy, or happiness. Only you have the power to either conceal or reveal the light within you.

The ego will resist this truth because its existence depends on it. Now, watch out, the ego is not “bad”. Only the ego would judge itself as bad! The ego is our animal inheritance and, as such, represents earlier stages of evolution on this planet. It is not bad; it is limited, primitive. Opening to the Spirit means opening to truths that are absolutely shocking to the ego, and it will feel threatened and resist. It will try to provide all sorts of reasons why so-and-so is really an X or a Y. At that moment, you will have two options:

Option One

Trust the ego and its reasoning, which is based on a limited and distorted perception of the situation, giving the false sense that the judgment your mind has made is grounded in truth. By judging someone as bad, you will inevitably believe—whether consciously or not—that you too are bad, and in the depths of your being, you will fear the punishment that, in your imagination, the bad deserve. Choosing this option will darken your mind, filling it with fear, sadness, anger, resentment, anxiety, worry…

Option Two

Accept the fact that, regardless of what you think or perceive, if your conclusion takes away your peace, then it is not based on truth. Suffering arises from believing what is not true. "The truth will set you free" and "All I know is that I know nothing" are two truths that point toward peace. Hold onto them and move through the ego's clouds of complaints as you restore awareness of your own light. By letting go of judgment against another, you will let go of the judgment you had unknowingly imposed on yourself. By accepting the innocence of another, you will regain awareness of your own innocence. Trust that the peace you are choosing and the truth of this decision go hand in hand.

This weekend, I had the opportunity to see this entire mechanism in action. On Friday, I had a difficult situation with someone, and I judged them without even realizing I was doing it. Immediately, negativity flooded my mind, and the rest of Friday and Saturday were difficult—dark, lacking in joy and energy. I even thought I was getting sick. On Sunday morning, I slammed my spiritual fist on the table and started doing qigong—in other words, dissolving clouds. And the light returned, with such force that I saw the entire mechanism of what had happened, and I felt grateful for the clarity.

My mind did not understand, and at first, it could not follow my heart when it said, "your brother is innocent." But that’s okay—I don’t need to know. I prefer the tangible peace that truth brings, even if it is incomprehensible, over the satisfaction of believing I understand and the darkness that comes with that illusion. Clarity will come once the clouds have dissolved, not before.

And so, little by little, it is revealed that what we truly are is innocent.

Interestingly, we recognize this in children, and it is socially accepted that they make mistakes because we understand they are innocent. But as we grow into adulthood, we deny our innocence under the assumption that "we already know what we are doing, or at least, we should." The truth is, we are still growing, still learning, and most of us have a long way to go before we become fully aware of what we are doing.

I’ll end with a truth that is always worth remembering: human beings always act according to what they perceive as good or beneficial. The degree of truth or love in the options we see available at any given moment depends on our level of consciousness, which is in constant evolution. If we see someone making a harmful decision—or if we ourselves do—it is because we could not see a better alternative; we simply did it the best we could.

With all my love,

A.
