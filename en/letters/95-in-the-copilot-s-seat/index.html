95. In the Copilot's Seat

What one sees after abandoning all control

This final semester at Berklee, I was fortunate enough to access very deep perspectives on Reality. Today I recovered this text, written after one of my most shocking experiences: a glimpse into reality beyond concepts, abandoning all desire to control and all limited identification. It is a letter about total joy and trust.

I am always talking about the same thing, in one way or another: that which I call “I” is not a body, nor is it confined to a body, but is instead something much more mysterious and vast.

The reason I talk about this so much is because that idea, explored rigorously, has the capacity to dissolve all problems and forms of suffering. But this letter is not about the wonderful consequences of that shift in perspective. What I want to explore today is my direct experience of this profound truth: I am much more than a physical body.

The experience is never the same, because one can never step in the same river twice. But one common element is seeing my body from outside while I meditate, just like I would see any other object in the room. This is not so difficult to achieve. The other day I guided a meditation for my sister and her husband, who are totally new to this field, and when we finished we realized that Q., in order to follow my instructions, had transcended his body effortlessly, seeing it from the outside. One experience like that is enough to see that what we are is not confined to the physical body.

But I am writing about this now because today I had one of my deepest experiences in this regard. It was at the end of my sixth qigong class this week, after a standing meditation of about thirty minutes. My mind at that moment was focused like a laser and still like a mirror-lake. When we finished, we sat down, and Joe began to speak; his voice was an open door to the mystery:

If I had no memory of what I am, nor had any concept or mental conditioning, what would I see when asking myself: “what am I?”

Inquiring in that way, my mind began to slide beyond the perception of the known (my attention was observing my right hand) toward the ineffable reality that waits behind it. Then the thought came: “I am not a concept.” And I felt my supposed ability to control what happens dissolve completely, and with it, my personal identity.

My sensory perceptions did not change: the sensation of my hand was still there. But, at the same time, what I was contemplating was something totally new and radiant; observing my hand in this way led me to ecstasy. I was no longer feeling “a hand,” because “hand” is only a concept superimposed upon ineffable reality. Without the concept, what remains is a total mystery, something that includes the entire universe, something made of the divine Creative Energy with which God manifests in every moment.

Words, by definition, cannot go beyond concepts, so it is not without a certain frustration that I write this. All I can describe is how I felt in that state: pure joy and total trust.

Pure joy is like the water that is the essence of all drinks: wine, tea, and tap water all have pure water in common, even though each liquid then has other substances that give it its particular qualities. In the same way, all the things we perceive as distinct are actually made of the same single substance that gives shape to all of Creation. The closest word I have to describe how that substance tastes is “pure joy.”

The other quality, total trust, began to shine after abandoning all control. My individual self, Antonio, is built upon temporary concepts. Without any concept, what remains is the body being what it is now, the world being what it is now, with no future to fear nor anyone who wants to control it. What remains is the Universe manifesting spontaneously, being what it is, without the need for an agent to guide it.

A calm mind reveals that our mind is not individual, but the Mind of God itself. The shift in perspective is radical and very shocking. That is why our mind does not stay still: its agitation is its constant effort to maintain the illusion of separation. But today I tasted, at a greater depth than ever before, what it is to let go of that effort and rest in total surrender.

What I saw is that the ego is totally wrong in thinking: “If I don't control what I do, I won't do anything!” By surrendering completely, I saw that there is an Intelligence that takes the reins of all my functions, and that it is totally safe to leave my entire life in its hands. Hence, the total trust.

The words of Jesus come to mind: “Truly I tell you, unless you change and become like little children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.” [Matthew 18:3]. Is not what I am describing the state of a newborn? Their senses are already operational, but they do not yet have any concept of who they are or what the world is. Their state must then be a mixture of wonder and total trust. That is the condition for un-covering the Kingdom of Heaven: the total surrender of the baby, but deliberately chosen by an adult mind.

What would happen if I remained in that state constantly is a mystery; the future always is. But what I see clearly is that it would be an expression of the Truth.

All the things that are now sustained by the constant effort of my ego would fall away. Perhaps I would stop doing things I now do out of fear, guilt, or a sense of duty. I would certainly stop attacking myself, or what is the same, attacking others. My mind would stop using God’s Creative Energy to maintain the illusion of time and the individual, and it could be used for creative expressions impossible to imagine from my current perspective.

In any case, I would stop worrying about “what to do,” because an infinite Intelligence would take care of that for me. It would be like sitting in the copilot’s seat and leaving the steering wheel to the One who knows the Way. I would become the happy observer, enveloped in imperturbable peace, looking out the window at views that grow more beautiful and mysterious by the moment.

With all my love,

A.
