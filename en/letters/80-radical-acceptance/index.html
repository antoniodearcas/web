Radical Acceptance

One of my last letters, “77. What Would Love Do?”, was perhaps the deepest, most psychedelic, and strangest one I’ve ever sent. I myself was left a bit stunned for a few days. But this morning, it came back to my mind in a very concentrated way. The idea is this:

If there is something in your life that makes you suffer and you want it to change, focus on changing your mind first, to adopt a posture of radical acceptance.

If you always end up in toxic relationships, if you always get cruel bosses, if you always trip, or your car gets scratched, if food upsets your stomach or your wrist hurts, if you have money problems, if you fall ill often, if you have family conflicts… don’t try to change the thing itself. Go within to find the cause. Seek to change your mind.

The ego tries to solve problems in the world by working directly with the forms that bother it. This doesn’t work, because the things of the world are only effects, reflections of a deeper reality to which we rarely pay attention: our mind.

The other day I was on a plane, and behind me there was a boy kicking my seat every two minutes. This made me feel irritated. My mind was debating whether to yell at him now or later. Those are the kinds of solutions the ego suggests: attack, be violent, he attacked you first! But then another idea arose in my mind: it doesn’t matter where I go, there’s always some guy kicking my seat, or playing loud music at night, or tapping his pen on the table. The experience always changes, but my reaction is always the same. The universe is trying to tell me something, it’s helping me let go of my anger, and this was as good an occasion as any to turn inward and put things in order.

So I decided, instead of saying anything, to use each kick as an opportunity to release what was being triggered in me. I spent the rest of the flight meditating, discovering the ways I projected my anger onto others, separating them from me. I chose to love the boy, and when I finally managed to see him with love, he kept kicking, but I no longer interpreted it as “will he ever stop!&*” but rather as “this is Christ giving me a tap on the back so I don’t fall asleep or get distracted while meditating.” It became a source of joy and communion, not of anger and separation.

Wisdom sees things in the world that are not harmonious and understands that their causes lie in our mind. So instead of fighting against the world, go into the mind to bring order. Doing this day after day, automatically and spontaneously our life begins to align, without us having to do anything directly.

Jesus said it this way: “Seek first the Kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all else will be given to you as well.”

In India they say it like this: “Do your practice, and everything else will come on its own.”

A Course in Miracles says it even more briefly: “Forgiveness is your only function here.”

And what is forgiveness?

“Forgiveness is still and quietly does nothing. It merely looks, and waits, and judges not.”

That phrase sums up all the contemplative practices of the world. Seek to cultivate that inner posture of radical acceptance, and everything else will come on its own.

With all my love,

A.
