134. I did it!

On the mysterious authorship of Nimbo

Last Friday the Nimbo music video came out.
You can watch it by clicking above.
To read about its mysterious authorship, keep reading.

Last Friday I published the music video for Nimbo, and I felt the joy of having achieved a great goal. "I did it!" I said to Betül, but immediately something jarred in my mind. If there is one thing I have taken from all these years of spiritual journey, it is that I am not alone, nor do I do anything alone. I corrected myself then, adding:

You did it.

My parents did it.

Ari (the album's producer) did it.

Joe (my teacher) did it.

All my teachers, friends and cousins did it.

The whole universe did it.

Jesus did it.

God did it.

This song represents for me a true fruit of the Spirit, and the Spirit is everything. Antonio is the small branch on which that fruit grew, but it is inseparable from the tree.

It is evident in my experience that neither the song nor the video were my invention.

The song arrived easily in a few minutes, almost without my noticing.

The video was a longer process and therefore left more space for my ego to appear saying lovely things like "I have no idea what I am doing," "you don't even know how to use a camera," or "why do I insist on doing everything alone?"

But just as a photon moves through empty space in a straight line, I move straight through God's seemingly crooked lines.

"I don't know what I'm doing" was overcome by an infinite "Trust in Me, I do."

"I don't know how to use the camera" by a "you know enough."

"Why alone" was constantly dissolved by an "I am here" that reverberated through my entire experience.

When the moment came to edit the video, my ego spoke again: "this makes no sense, there is no concept, these are just disconnected images and most of them have nothing special about them."

I turned then to the one who does know and said: "Lord, I don't know how to do this, you edit it."

The anxiety and frustration dissolved and, in a state of calm, the shots began finding their place, at a slow pace, with spacious frames that reflected the absence of hurry with which the editing was happening.

Finally, I understood what the concept of the video was: the light passing through form, just like the album cover with its Christic tree, and just like the song, with its om sounding through the river, the sun, and you.

Everything was perfectly coherent, in a way I had not been able to see from my perspective as a busy little ant.

For things like these it feels dishonest to say "I did it!" unless in that "I" I include my Lord. In that case, it works.

With all my love,

Antonio
