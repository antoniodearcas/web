93. “What would You have me do now?”

Life in the copilot's seat

Today I spent the whole day in the state I’ve been seeking and cultivating all semester: listening, letting myself be guided by an intelligence higher than my own, in a blissful trust. A magnificent closing to my time in this monastery called Berklee, and a good omen for what comes next.

Yesterday I finished my studies at Berklee. After finishing the last qigong class, I stayed for a while talking with my classmates and giving out hugs. Then I went home, lay down on my bed, and after thinking, “I’ve finished Berklee,” I started laughing out loud. What a journey! I came here expecting to learn a few new chords, and it turned into a passage through the desert, a descent into a shadowy valley and a climb up the other side, a plunge into baptismal waters, a leap beyond the moon, the Milky Way, the manifested universe, time and space. My laughter was cosmic.

Now it’s time to prepare my return to Spain: packing, folding clothes, giving away flowers, objects, books, closing my bank and mobile accounts, and all kinds of mini-tasks and mini-decisions (“Should I take this rag? No, Antonio, prioritize.”)

Today I’ve spent most of the day on these tasks, and surprisingly, not only did I not feel overwhelmed, but it was a peaceful and very pleasant day. The key moment happened this morning when, after finishing my morning practice, I saw my mind spring into action:

“Okay, I have to take this package to UPS, but I also need to write to So-and-So, and not forget x and y and z, blah blah blah…”

Ten seconds in that mental state were enough for me to realize that, if I continued that way, the day would be a runaround with no presence anywhere, all spiced with anxiety and the feeling that I was forgetting something.

Instead, I decided to sit, breathe, recenter myself, and surrender my day to the One who knows everything I need to do and knows the most efficient path. So I looked in that direction and asked:

“What would You have me do now?”

The answer appeared in my mind without words: “Take the package to UPS.”

Okay!

And that’s what I did, and the walk was delightful. Trusting that this was all I needed to know for the moment, I strolled to the UPS office with a calm mind, observing the trees, the sky, the people I passed. I was happier than a child in a stroller.

I delivered the package and, after leaving, I paused for a second and asked again:

“What would You have me do now?”

“Whole Foods.”

And, without yet knowing what I had to buy there, I headed to Whole Foods, knowing that at some moment I would be shown what I had to buy. It was a mango and some blueberries.

This state of grace in which I spent the morning is not how I usually live, but it is undoubtedly what I desire most. All my spiritual efforts are directed toward that type of effortlessness, being able to live this way: listening, in harmony. The other day I read a phrase that sums it up well:

Actively calm, calmly active.

It has been a gift to experience it in a situation that would usually have been stressful. To know that I can fully trust, abandon all my plans, and enjoy every moment while acting with complete effectiveness in the world.

To conclude, I’ll share a reflection along these lines from someone who dedicated his life to cultivating this way of being in the world: the wonderful Father Thomas Keating.

“Effortlessness becomes the main practice of your contemplative life. That doesn’t mean you do nothing, but you don’t hink up things to do for yourself. You sort of let it happen.

People could say: “well, if you let God do it, nothing will happen.” On the contrary! If you let God do it, everything will happen.”

With all my love,

A.
