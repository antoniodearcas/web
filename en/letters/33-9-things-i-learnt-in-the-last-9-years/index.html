9 Things I Learnt in the Last 9 Years

For one of my courses at Berklee, we had to write a commencement speech including 9 lessons we had learnt. Considering that my first epiphany was 9 years ago, I thought I would try to condense in 9 points the most valuable things I’ve learnt since then.

I imagine going back to my high school, standing in front of all the old nuns and young kids, taking a deep breath, and then starting to talk about infinite consciousness, qigong and Krishna. Ah, glorious. I hope that moment comes true. For now, I’ll share it with you.

Take one breath as consciously as possible.

1- The life you seek outside is only within you

Would you still want success, or that ideal relationship, if you knew it would make you miserable? The answer to that should be enough to realise that it’s not success or the intimate relationship you’re after, but the inner qualities you think they will provide for you: happiness, sense of fulfilment, freedom, safety, love, communion, peace.

In reality, the source of these qualities is within. Happiness or love are not emotions, passing by like grief or hatred. Happiness or love are aspects of what you really are, they are you, the real you, and you are always here and now. Except that, you are not always here and now, are you? Your mind is busy looking for these things somewhere else, and therefore misses the mark. Then your emotions join the party, telling your mind that there’s something wrong, and that the present moment is not acceptable as it is, pushing you even further from reality. All these things are like the clouds obscuring the sky. But the sky hasn’t gone anywhere, it’s just cloudy. So, our job is to learn to be with ourselves and the world in a way that doesn’t obscure the sky.

Now, take another breath as consciously as possible. There’s a small opening in the clouds.

2- Suffering comes from confusing the spiritual and material levels

One thing we know for sure about this world is that it keeps changing. So, looking for these essential qualities in the changing phenomena of the world is the recipe for misery. It will come sooner or later. Happiness, love, peace, these things belong to the spiritual realm, and yet we search for them in the material world, in experiences, relationships and substances. This error will always create incoherent behaviour, which will cause suffering to ourselves and others. Eating won’t fill that sense of lack, but it will make you sick.

What we are doing here is like following a mirage in the desert. The desert is a desert because there is no water in it. Once this begins to be intuited, our spirit begins to awaken to its own reality, and by going inward, we begin to cultivate this awareness and way of being in the world.

3- There is only one universal shared being

Perhaps the most mind-boggling discovery one can make in this inner exploration is that you are not who you thought you were. You thought you were a body, with a mind and a personality. But if you spend enough time meditating, you will start to understand that what you really are is that which is knowing these things. You are not the little human, you have a little human, and you are that which is knowing it. And this being that knows is universal. We are all that one being. That’s what all religions, spiritual traditions, and mystics and poets, and even Bob Marley, are trying to say: at the most fundamental level, we are all one, one life, one spirit. In Christianity it’s called Christ, and Jesus was a human who had fully realised this. In India they call it Brahman, in Zen they call it Buddha. That’s what we are, right this instant, a divine being that we share with everyone and everything.

4- Perception is not reality

But we perceive things, people and ourselves as separate. Why is this? Our perception doesn’t show us the world as it is in reality, it shows us what we are projecting onto it. You are seeing your beliefs, biases, emotions and traumas projected onto the world. This mismatch between our perception and reality is what causes all our troubles. [Paraphrasing from the book “Reality, Spirituality and Modern Man” by Dr. Hawkins], Socrates said that all men choose only the seeming good (perception) but are unable to differentiate the “real” good (essence) from the false (illusion). The Buddha said a similar thing: the unenlightened human merely lives in a world of illusions (maya), composed of projected perceptions. This causes us to repeat the same mistakes again and again, because we act as we perceive.

5- We are responsible, but also innocent

The tough news is that you are responsible for this, and aligning your perception with reality is a difficult process. Some have called it the hero’s journey, for good reason. All these movies (Star Wars, Lord of the Rings, Harry Potter) are talking about that, sooner or later, you will have to face Darth Vader. The good news is that you’re also innocent. It is a profound truth that, whatever you did, you did it the best you could. If you could have done it better, you would have. You were acting in accordance with your perception, without knowing that your perception was distorted and misaligned with truth. If one understands this, forgiveness becomes inevitable, which is one of the main tools we can use to start healing our mind and therefore our perception of the world. That is why Jesus could look at his executioners and say, “Forgive them, Father, for they know not what they do”. His vision was clear, fully aligned with Truth, and he saw in them their innocence.

6- Spiritual practices correct perception

Restoring this awareness of our innocence is what has been called Salvation, Nirvana, liberation. It’s the path to healing, to being as creative, energetic, alive as you can be. It’s getting to hang out with your inner child all day. There are myriad tools for achieving this. I will divide the ones I have explored in three categories:

At the level of the body: doing yoga, somatic work, Alexander Technique, qigong or even dancing can serve this purpose if done with that intention. The body stores all these stuck energies which distort the way you feel and perceive. These are ways to remove those energies and restore the natural flow of energy.

At the level of mind: meditation, psychoanalysis, studying spiritual teachings, A Course In Miracles, journaling, practicing gratitude, are things that we can use to train our minds to think differently about ourselves and the events of our lives. By looking at things from a greater context, the meaning of things changes. The wider the context, the more freeing. God is just another name for the infinite context. But the mind cannot go there.

That takes us to the last level, the level of spirit: here are decisions of the spiritual will, the setting of intentions at a macro level. What are you aligned with in life? What do you want to be? This is like setting the compass. The waves and the wind might make you lose your balance now and then, but your compass remains constant. By aligning this compass with Truth, Love and Beauty, our whole life starts to move in that direction, and so these qualities start to grow.

7- Reliance upon a Savior

All of this sounds tough and obscure, and it is. We’re living in a house of mirrors and we keep bumping against ourselves again and again. The good news is that we can ask for help. There have been beings who have fully realised their divinity while on Earth, and have offered help to anyone who wants it. A symbol of this I personally connect very much with is when Jesus stretches his hand from above the surface to get Peter, who is drowning, out of the water. But this type of help is not bound by any religion or regulated by any sort of human structure. Krishna, a divine incarnation who lived in India 5000 years ago said, "If you call upon me, by whatever name, you are mine." Religions can be very helpful in maintaining the awareness that this help is always available for us. But if you don’t resonate with religions, it’s not necessary. Just acknowledging the existence of some form of higher power or intelligence, call it God, Nature or the Universe, and asking It for help is enough. The only condition is that we stop clinging to negativity.

8- Make the commitment and help will come to you

Coming back to planet Earth, there are things that we intuit that, if we did them, we would become all that we can be and our lives would be meaningful and fulfilling beyond what we can currently imagine. But the world seems tough, and full of obstacles and difficulties, making fear and doubt enter our minds, distorting our vision and deviating us from our true paths.

But something I’ve put my faith on, and then verified through my own experience again and again, is what Terence McKenna explains beautifully in this quote:

“Nature loves courage. You make the commitment and nature will respond to that commitment by removing impossible obstacles. Dream the impossible dream and the world will not grind you under, it will lift you up. This is the trick. This is what all these teachers and philosophers who really counted, who really touched the alchemical gold, this is what they understood. This is the shamanic dance in the waterfall. This is how magic is done. By hurling yourself into the abyss and discovering it's a feather bed.”

9- All fear is illusion. Love is the law of the universe.

Hurling yourself into the abyss is safe because the abyss is just an illusion. We humans like to give names to things: we see light, and we call it light, and we see its absence, and we call it darkness. But darkness is not really a thing, it doesn’t have unique properties of its own, it’s just the absence of light. Same thing with heat and cold. There is no such thing as cold, there is only heat or its absence. In the same way, fear is just the absence of love. Love is the way we are and perceive when we know we are one with everything else, which is a fact that even modern science acknowledges.

So even if we perceive fear as real, we should not trust it and let it guide our decisions. Love is the ultimate law, based on reality and not on its absence. If we commit ourselves to living guided by love, first we will start running into the walls we’ve built against it, so life might seem paradoxically less loving for a while. This is what happens with commitments, blockages come up to be dissolved. But life is challenging whatever path we chose and the good thing about this one is that it is based in reality. So, basing our lives on the faith that love is the ultimate law, will allow us to bypass our flawed and fearful perceptions, and start moving with life instead of against it, or away from it, and life will respond to this commitment by becoming brighter, more loving, peaceful, beautiful and fulfilling.

I’ll end with another quote by Krishna who, like Jesus, was a divine incarnation, and so his words are really the words of the universe, of life itself, of God. And he said:

“The only way you can conquer me is through love, and there I am gladly conquered.”

With all my love,

A.
