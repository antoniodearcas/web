90. To the Heart

And this time, with deep roots

Before meeting Joe, I was adrift in an ocean of energy. One day, something like a lightning bolt pierced my chest from bottom to top, opened my heart, and my perception of the world changed instantly and permanently. I no longer saw only what my senses showed me; a new kind of vision opened up, revealing an invisible, energetic universe where nothing is separate. From there, I could feel not only the accumulated pain in my own heart, but also in the hearts of everyone I encountered.

Walking down the street, at home with friends, on the subway, or in the homeless man lying on the sidewalk at 30ºF with his head resting on an empty bottle. I could feel inside everyone’s heart. I didn’t see stories or anything I could describe or understand, but I could feel it resonating within my own heart.

My initial response was terror and depression. I seriously considered going to a monastery. How could I continue studying in the middle of Boston in that state? But the answer came, and it told me that I should remain in society. What happened then is that I fell into a very difficult middle ground: I began withdrawing from all social interaction. I stopped going to concerts, bars, gatherings with friends. My fear became pathological.

Without realizing it, I also began avoiding my own heart. It became something strange and incomprehensible. When I brought my attention to other parts of my body, I could clearly feel my physical body: a hand, the nose, the abdomen. But bringing my attention to the center of my chest was like looking through a telescope at a galaxy without stars.

Shortly after that opening, Joe entered my life and, through him, the Presence of Jesus Christ. A hand broke through the surface, took my own, and pulled me out of the water. That was the end of my first lesson with Him. Then I went back out into the street, and I plunged once again beneath the waves. My life now consisted of learning how to swim.

Joe taught me that before trying to ascend, one must cultivate deep roots. It’s an obvious lesson (every tree teaches it) but I was eager to return to the Heaven I had glimpsed at 21. I began my spiritual work looking upward and, when one knocks, the door opens. The opening revealed wonderful things that completely changed the meaning of my life, but psychologically it wreaked havoc.

Over these three years, Joe helped me stabilize and gradually integrate this new way of living. We worked mostly on rooting. In the Chinese tradition, it is called the lower dantien: the energetic center located in the abdomen that connects us to the Earth. It is associated with physical vigor and sexuality.

Until today, I believed that throughout these years I had been ascending, and in some ways I had. But the main focus had actually been on the Earth, on strengthening that lower center.

Today, as I prepare to finish my studies with him and return to Spain, I asked him after a particularly long meditation: “Where should I place my attention when doing this exercise? Sometimes I bring it to the lower dantien, other times to the upper dantien (in the head), and today I’ve been moving all over the place.”

Joe answered, “To the heart. At your level of development, you can begin to center in the heart.”

I then turned my attention to my chest and felt the vertigo again. I realized that, although I listen to my heart constantly (it has become a very explicit guide), I had spent three years avoiding really looking at it, avoiding sinking into that emptiness. I discovered that I can barely keep my attention there for more than a few seconds. What have I been avoiding by not wanting to enter there? The answer has no name, but I know it. And, in turn, it knows me.

Tonight, as I was getting ready to end the day, I was thinking joyfully and in awe about all of this. Then a fear arose: “I only have two weeks left at Berklee. What will this new exploration of the heart be like without Joe’s constant guidance?”

The answer then emerged from the depths of my heart in the form of a prayer I learned as a child. I used to pray it with my mother before going to sleep, about ten years before losing my child’s faith and distancing myself from all of this, as any teenager would. Now the prayer has returned, and in its childlike language contains the whole truth I needed to hear today:

“Little Jesus of my life,
you are a child like me.
That’s why I love you so much
and I give you my heart.
Take it, it is yours, not mine.”

With all my love,

A.
