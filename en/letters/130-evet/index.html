Nº 130 · August 30, 2026

Evet

Story of a calling

This week I said an "Evet" ("yes" in Turkish) that, joined with another "Evet," founded a family.

Almost four years ago, my heart opened, and everything changed radically and permanently. Relationships, purpose, perception, sense of identity... everything was transformed overnight.

Around that time, I was a regular at a yoga studio. One day I met a beautiful monk who would become one of my closest friends from my Boston years, Father Bon. He invited me to his monastery to help him clean out the goat shed. Once there, another friend of his appeared, another beautiful man named Jay. He too quickly became a fundamental friend in my life there.

We spent the morning among rakes, sawdust and wheelbarrows, followed by tea and a pleasant conversation about the religious icons that lined the walls.

When we finished, Jay offered to drive me home, and I accepted gratefully.

I told him I was going through a very hard and confusing time, which I called the "opening of my heart."

He told me he had been through the same thing, which surprised me enormously because I had never heard anyone speak of anything similar, and here I was in a car with someone who understood. It was a relief to know that the psychedelic storm I was living through was in fact an archetypal process.

He described it like this: the chakra system is like a river. A closed heart is like a part of the river where a large log has fallen and is blocking the flow. Behind that log other things get stuck, and gradually something like a dam forms. But if the water in the river begins to push and push harder, it can eventually break the log apart. When that happens, not only does the flow of the river increase enormously, but for a time it also carries along all kinds of things that had been stuck until then.

There was an explanation for my emotional storm, and also for the intense spiritual experiences I had been having.

Afterward, having already arrived at my place, he stopped right in front, made a sudden leap in the conversation, and began telling me how he had met his wife.

I will leave out the story out of respect for his privacy. I will only say that it was a hero's journey that passed through where I was now and ended in marriage. A process I also recognized as archetypal, and which I therefore received as a prophecy, or a map that Jay was handing me for my own adventure.

The doubts about whether I should go to a monastery had been resolved a few days earlier. The Voice that speaks for God answered clearly: "No, your path is in the world."

Now, that same spirit of truth finished the message through Jay: "and therefore, you will start a family."

"Jay, you can't imagine how much everything you've just told me..." and he, looking at me calmly, said with tenderness: "I know, I know." I then stopped explaining myself because I could see he knew perfectly well what I was going through. I threw myself to hug him. I understood in that moment that, after spending several weeks praying ardently for some clarity on this matter of intimate relationships, it had finally arrived. Jay had appeared in my life, and without my asking or even mentioning the subject, he had given me exactly the clarity I was looking for. God had answered my prayers with miraculous force. Jay understood it that way too and said "I suppose this encounter was meant to be."

Still in shock, I got out of his car. He got out too and gave me a proper hug. Hugging someone with such an open heart is a completely different experience from an ordinary hug. As our chests came together, through our clothes and coats, I felt his heart and mine entering into a conversation. In that energetic exchange, my newly opened and frightened heart met a heart that was open and at peace, and that peace communicated itself to mine. At the risk of diminishing the inexpressible depth of that exchange, if it had happened in words it would have gone something like this:

— I am suffering a great deal and I am afraid, but what you have just shared with me and your mere presence comfort me tremendously.
— I know. I know what you are going through. Hold on, you are doing well, I am here with you for whatever you need. There is a deep and luminous peace that you will see when the waters calm.

We said goodbye, I thanked him again and went upstairs. I went straight to my room, collapsed to my knees in front of my altar and wept loudly for several minutes, giving thanks for what had just happened. It was the deep weeping of a man who recognizes himself as safe and loved in God's hands. The weeping of someone who recognizes unequivocally what he truly wants in this world of confusion, conflict and disordered desires. The weeping of a son of divorced parents who recognizes that marriage is not a fallible invention of man, but a vocation that comes from God and goes toward Him. I felt the relief of the sailor who sees a guiding star appear through the storm clouds: my path was marriage.

There was still much journey ahead, a year of darkness that ended, as Jay's story had prophesied, with total surrender: "Lord, if you want me to remain single forever, so be it." Only then did I finally recognize that the love I had spent my whole life searching for out there had always been present at the bottom of my heart. It was the love of Christ, like a lake of infinite depth and eternally unfailing.

Two days later my relationship with Betül began.

This week I married her.

Gloria in excelsis Deo!

With all my love,

Antonio

P.D.: Thank you, Father, for the good friends who help us find our way.
