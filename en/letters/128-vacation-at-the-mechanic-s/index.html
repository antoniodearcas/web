128. Vacation at the Mechanic's

Today's teacher was a flat tire.

Some are traveling in exotic places.

Others are in the city with the AC on.

All equally fortunate, because the Truth is with everyone.

After a couple of months without leaving the concrete maze that is Istanbul, yesterday we got in the car and drove to spend a few days by the sea.

The first thing we did when we arrived was get a flat tire.

We would have preferred to spend the time among pine trees and waves, but God arranged for the first morning to unfold among tow trucks and wheel rims.

While we waited for the mechanic to arrive, I asked Betül how she was feeling. She said frustrated, that this is not what she would choose to be doing with her short vacation. We began to explore that perspective and remembered that every form of suffering comes from a mistaken perception. We prayed together:

"Holy Spirit, help us to see this with love. We let go of whatever interpretation we were holding. Show us the truth of this situation."

A few minutes later, I opened my eyes and saw that Betül was smiling. I asked her what she had received:

"Everything is perfect."

I then shared what came to me, which given the way my mind works, brought the same message but more elaborated (some of us need things explained in more detail):

We believe that vacation should be a time without problems, free from the mundane burdens and tasks that follow us around the city. So when the tire went flat the moment we arrived, that event seemed to go completely against what was supposed to be happening. Hence the frustration.

But when I let go of that human construct of "vacation versus ordinary life," what I saw was that true rest does not come from being near the sea or reading under a parasol. True rest comes from showing up to whatever is going on with love, because then we allow God to carry us. In other words, what exhausts us is resisting what is happening, and rest comes from surrendering lovingly.

The real joy, rest and peace were not at the beach, but in lovingly accompanying each other through the adventure of changing a tire in 90-degree heat.

If Jesus were the third person in the car, how would he hold the situation? How would he treat the mechanic? How would he treat us, and how would he treat himself?

(The truth is I would trade every vacation for the rest of my life for one morning at the mechanic with Jesus.)

The key is that Christ is always present. It is enough to honestly say "Yes, Father," and then go about whatever needs to be done with tenderness, joy and diligence.

And so it was.

A couple of hours later, the whole thing sorted out, Betül and I were in a state of joy that could only have come from the Heaven within us. I said to her: "Do you remember the prayer this morning? Eight words: 'Holy Spirit, help us to see this with love,' and the course of the morning changed radically: from a wasted morning to a beautiful one."

"Wow," she said.

"Wow," I said.

With all my love,

Antonio
