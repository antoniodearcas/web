120. Change of Owner

The previous one knew not what he did.

What does it mean in practice to be "in the world but not of the world"?

Is it really possible to change? To leave the past behind and adopt a new way of being in the world, more aligned with truth, love, and integrity?

These days have been an exercise in exactly that. I see my entire past: the many misaligned actions that led me to develop a poor self-concept, built on guilt and unworthiness. And that past arrives in my present moment like heavy waves trying to drown the new shoots.

A new identity is cultivated not by saying positive affirmations, but by taking actions that bear witness to that change. Every action is like a vote: Who am I? The timid, irresponsible Antonio, prone to laziness and dissociation, or the upright, courageous Antonio, aligned with his deepest understanding of the truth?

When the bag of past votes weighs so much, it is hard to believe it is possible to accumulate enough new votes to tip the balance toward the side I want.

This morning I was contemplating this and it seemed like an impossible battle to win, so I asked for help, and this is what the Holy Spirit showed me:

The past does not exist. It only occurs in the present, in the activity of our imagination, now. There is no scale where on one side sits the near-infinite weight of the past, and on the other the minuscule present trying to win with its infinitesimal weight. Only the present moment exists; there is nothing else. And in every moment we are contemplating a situation that invites us to choose: Who am I now?

The answer to that question is not irrevocably conditioned by the previous instant: this moment is not an effect of the previous one. Each instant is being created anew, and in this instant we are completely free to make a new decision. It makes no difference how many years we have lived from negativity. Only this present situation exists, and it is asking you: Who am I now?

When we accept the ego’s answer, our mind responds based on that supposed past, and our identity then seems to drag chains.

And yet we can also let the Holy Spirit answer for us, and his answer is: I am Spirit, I am free.

From there I see that the body is an instrument. Nothing more. It is not me, and nothing it does or has done defines or changes what I essentially am.

The body is like a guitar. If I walk onto a stage in front of thousands of people and, on the first chord, I realize the guitar is out of tune, how do I respond? The answer, again, will be a response to my question: Who am I now?

If, in a moment of madness, I believe that I am the guitar, I will feel shame, guilt for not having tuned it beforehand, and that moment will become a trauma that haunts me for the rest of my life saying: "you are not good enough."

If, on the other hand, I do not identify with the guitar and simply see it for what it is, an instrument, my response will probably be to stop for a moment, laugh, apologize, tune it, and try again.

In the same way, what my body did or did not do in the past does not define what I am. It makes no difference if I gave a thousand concerts with the guitar out of tune. That says nothing about the quality of the guitar itself, or about me as a musician. It only says that I did not yet have ears to hear, and that is not something to feel guilty about, but a natural process of maturation.

Returning to my present dilemma: I observe this moment, which is all that exists. I observe the ego's voice deploying its blind response: stories of guilt, shame, helplessness, anger. I observe everything from the distance the Spirit offers and ask: Who am I really? Am I this? Am I the out-of-tune guitar?

But I am not that. I am Spirit, I am a holy child of God, eternally innocent. What defines the center of my identity is the love of God, and from there I choose to forgive: I was mistaken in the past about who I was, and that led me to act erratically in the world. But now I am free to recognize the truth and act from there.

I take my body out of the ego's hands and give it to God. Change of owner. Here you go, God, I let You resolve this situation.

The body relaxes, the forehead softens, the mind empties.

Whatever happens next is not my concern, I surrender that to You as well.

Once again I have remembered and fulfilled my only function: to stop lending my ears to the ego and the noise of its illusory chains, and to listen to the Voice of God, which never ceases to say: "Not only have you been forgiven, but the effects of your errors have been completely cancelled. You are free."

With all my love,

Antonio
