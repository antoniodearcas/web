51. Ice or Water

Where does this longing point to?

51. Water or Ice

This Monday, I stopped and took a step back.

Things are shifting at very deep levels, and the hustle of daily life had been overwhelming me too much to pay attention to what truly matters. I once again experienced sanvega, a Sanskrit word that describes a feeling of spiritual urgency—sanvega grabs you by the shoulders and shakes you while shouting: "Wake up! You can't keep living like this. You're scattered across the surface, and there, you cannot fulfill the longing of your heart."

I decided to listen to that voice instead of the one pointing at my to-do list, saying, "Look at everything you have to do! There's no time to stop!"

I ignored that anxious voice. Its perspective is too narrow to truly understand anything. Its vision is that of an ant, and in that moment I was feeling the eagle wanting to spread its wings. So instead of going to class and doing "what I had to do," I walked out the door and took flight.

That day, the Boston winter gave a brief respite, and it was pleasant at the park. I was walking slowly among trees and geese when something struck me so powerfully that it shook my entire body: I realized that I cannot find what I am longing for because what I seek is myself.

This longing does not point to something that can be found, because anything I find will be something limited and separate from me. No, this longing points to something unlimited and one with me. This longing points to an infinite self.

An image comes to me: the human mind is like a bowl of ice. At first, this longing for union drives the bowl to try to fill itself with the entire ocean, but it soon realizes that the ocean cannot fit inside. Then, in a moment of clarity, it understands that the only way to achieve that union is to let itself fall into the ocean and dissolve into it.

The ice of Antonio’s structure is cracking and dissolving, and through those openings, I see that my identity extends far beyond this limited personality. It is no longer about a deeper or more mature self. It is something of an entirely different quality, though of the same substance: I am not the structure of ice; I am the water of the ocean.

The next day, I realized that this shift in perspective was settling into my mind when a friend asked me how I was doing, and my response was, "I am being revealed," with the I in that phrase not referring to Antonio, but to that infinite I that encompasses everything.

I was never good at small talk.

But then, I forget again. A few days ago, a qigong classmate asked me if I was Christian, and I answered: "I don’t know. I grew up in a Catholic family and that’s the tradition I was brought up in. But I stopped going to mass when I was 15, and although I have gone back now and then, I never truly returned. On the other hand, I constantly turn to Jesus, and his influence is transforming my life. I don’t know if that makes me Christian."

My friend asked me about my identity, and I spoke to him about the bowl of ice.

Then our qigong teacher walked into the class, and it occurred to me to ask him: "J., do you consider yourself Christian?"

J. looked at me and, without a moment’s hesitation, said: "No. What I am is neither conceivable nor perceivable."

That’s how the ocean answers.

With all my love,

A.
