On Self-Esteem

And the blocks we place on our flourishing in the world.

A few days ago I announced that I will be giving an intimate and contemplative concert in Madrid on Friday, March 20. If you’re interested, you can get your tickets here.

In this letter, I explore what has unfolded within me since then: the resolution of a paradox that, if left unresolved, threatened to lead me into an involuntary poverty in the name of spirituality.

It has been six years since I last organized a concert in Spain, and this small event has been like lifting a mirror. In it, I have seen my self-esteem and the reasons why I had been blocking, at the root, my professional path and my success.

I have spent the last five years devoted to dissolving the ego: studying, observing its mechanisms and invoking God again and again so that the illusion of being a separate individual might gradually dissolve. That seemed to encapsulate the whole path: to deny oneself.

But there are many paths, and the path of negation is only one of them.

Lately, however, I have been running into a paradox. My vocation is no longer only to go inward and undo, but also to go out there and build. How do those two things fit together?

I have realized that the key element is self-esteem. I have begun to suspect that perhaps the most decisive factor for success in the world is the way we see ourselves, because others can only see us as we see ourselves. On that depends whether people buy our services, our paintings or vote for us as president. Other factors such as skill or intention come afterwards.

The paradox was clear: is it possible to work on dissolving the ego, that illusion of being an individual self, and at the same time cultivate high self-esteem?

My previous definition was that self-esteem is the self-concept one holds. How can I cultivate a good self-concept if I dedicate myself to denying the very idea of self-concept? From that limited definition, having high self-esteem was synonymous with having a big ego, an elevated idea of oneself, at the expense of truth.

But today a more powerful definition came to me from the work of Brian Gibbs: self-esteem is the way in which I hold the experiencer. The experiencer, in this case, would be Antonio: the person who lives the experience. The “I” that holds him is deeper: consciousness, or the soul.

Within your soul lies the capacity to let the love of God pass through, or to block it. God is always shining upon us, but we place conditions that block it. The shadow those blocks cast appears as guilt, shame, fear, anger… in other words, when we choose to block the love of God, we have chosen to punish ourselves.

To help you explore this within yourself, I offer a series of affirmations.

I allow myself to love myself despite not having been productive today.

I allow myself to love myself despite arriving late to the meeting.

I allow myself to love myself despite not having the income I would like.

I allow myself to love myself despite not taking care of my body as I would like.

I allow myself to love myself despite that mistake I made.

I allow myself to love myself despite not being clear about what I want to do.

I allow myself to love myself despite not receiving the approval of ___.

I allow myself to love myself despite not achieving the results I desired.

I allow myself to love myself despite my physical appearance.

I allow myself to love myself despite the things I do to sabotage myself.

All those conditions, and many more, are the clouds that darken and lower our self-esteem. The solution, then, is to become aware of when we are punishing ourselves, and choose to forgive ourselves. In this way the clouds gradually dissolve, and the love of God shines more and more upon our person, which begins to flourish, feeling increasingly free to be itself.

And if you want to accelerate the healing process of your self-esteem, I recommend that you become an entrepreneur! The fear of starting something is the fear of looking directly at the cruelty with which you have been holding yourself. The fear is not of failing, but of the way you will punish yourself if you do not achieve the expected results.

This understanding came to me while organizing the concert next Friday. By deciding to do something like this, I raised a mirror that allowed me to observe my self-esteem. I know that the number of tickets I sell will be a consequence not only of the promotion I do, but mainly of my self-esteem, of how much success I believe I deserve. Reality is non-linear, and “being” weighs infinitely more than “doing” or “having.” Self-esteem belongs to the level of being, and it is worth working on it.

I have also seen that self-esteem, like money, is something neutral: everything depends on what it is used for. If I have extremely high self-esteem and use it to win elections and lead my country into war and ruin, then it would have been better to have low self-esteem. But if what I want to do with my life is to put my gifts at the service of God, of others and of myself, then having low self-esteem is a huge obstacle. God wants me to have high self-esteem, first so that I myself know that I am loved unconditionally, and second so that I can bring that love into the world without obstacles.

Yes, having extremely high self-esteem can be the same as being perfectly humble. Because humility is not hiding and choosing smallness, but allowing the love of God to pass through you and make your life flourish, and the lives of all those around you. To be humble is to allow the infinite potential of God to express itself through us.

And then God will decide, if He wishes, whether that greatness is expressed through chosen poverty, like Saint Francis of Assisi, or through extraordinary success, like Dr. Hawkins. In my case, poverty doesn’t seem to be an option because I want to found a family and bring my art into the world. So, Father, I place myself in your hands. Show me the blocks I still hold so that I may let them go, and cultivate within me a great self-esteem that I can place at your service.

Amen.

With all my love,

Antonio
