66. I am not in the world. I am in God.

Change the context, and the meaning changes

“It is not what the eyes see, but that by which the eyes see—know only that to know Brahman.”
— Kena Upanishad, I.4

The meaning of something depends on the context from which it is viewed. It’s like observing the same object under different lights. Unfortunately, humans are not good at being aware of context; it’s our great weakness. That’s why we constantly misinterpret what happens. That’s how manipulation in the media works. The smaller or more distorted the context, the more suffering the meaning brings.

Today I want to lift my gaze toward the infinite context—where suffering dissolves.

When I open my eyes, the context seems to be this: a world of matter floating in a void. We appear in it, inside a body, separate from other bodies, and sooner or later, the body dies. If there is anything beyond, it belongs to the realm of faith or belief.

But when I close my eyes, I see something else. I no longer appear in a world. Everything appears in consciousness, and there, everything is one. Perceptions, sensations, emotions, thoughts… are modulations of a single substance: consciousness.

The more I contemplate this, the more the separation between the things of the world fades. I still perceive them the same way, but the meaning has changed—because the context has changed. The senses still show a world of separate forms. But behind them, a subtle field emerges where I see everything as interconnected. It’s as if a character in a film began to realize that all their adventures were actually happening on a screen. After the initial shock, they would go on doing what they had to do—carry the ring to Mordor or search for Nemo—but the meaning of what was happening would change radically.

What I’m beginning to see is that the true context of our existence is not the world, but the Presence of God.

The body appears in the world.
The world appears in consciousness.
Consciousness appears in God.

Body.
Mind.
Spirit.

I am not in the world. I am in God.

More and more, I no longer feel I exist in an indifferent and hostile world. As I stop projecting, I understand that every bit of hostility perceived out there is a reflection of something within me. And that’s where it can be healed: by choosing peace and love—that is, by choosing to see from the infinite context of God.

How different everything becomes when seen under that light…

I remember that, back when I still considered myself an atheist—a few months before the epiphany I experienced at 21—I had a first glimpse of this. I was alone at home, eating something delicious while it snowed outside. Good music was playing. It was a happy moment, a moment of fullness, and I felt grateful.

Then the question arose: To whom or to what am I grateful?

A window opened in my mind toward the infinite context.

Only in that wider context does gratitude make sense. To be grateful is to appreciate that one has received a gift. And in order to receive something, there must be a giver. We can thank chance, luck, Newton’s laws if we wish… but that doesn’t do justice to what we feel in the heart:

“This gift can only come from love.”

With all my love,
A.
