68. Seeds to Water

Patience, respect for the other’s freedom, absence of judgment, and loving creativity.

In my last letter, I spoke about the importance not only of desiring good things, but also of desiring them in a good way. I spoke of the reality pointed to by the term “Holy Spirit,” and how in Buddhism that same reality has been expressed.

Today I want to return to Christianity, and explore the Holy Spirit a little further—not as something external to us, but as the part of our mind that is at peace, beyond all conflict, and that truly knows how to love. I want to explore some qualities of the Holy Spirit to help us find them within ourselves. Doing so is a step toward restoring our inner peace, and that is the best thing we can offer the world: to bring peace a little closer to those around us.

But before we begin, two things:

First, this is not a call to force ourselves to be virtuous, but an invocation of qualities already within us, like seeds waiting to be watered. Each point describes Love from a different angle.

Second, you can use this letter as a tool to view, from a new perspective, something in your life that is challenging—something you'd like to change. It probably has to do with a pattern of behavior, either yours or someone close to you. Let the following qualities help you see that situation in a new light.

Infinite Patience

Ask yourself: Could I live with this forever? If it's physical pain, can I accept it and fully adapt as if it were going to be there forever? If it’s someone else’s behavior, can I accept this person just as they are now, without expecting them to change?

Suffering is demanding the present to be different. The path to peace does not lie in changing the world, but in accepting it. Only what we accept can be transformed. Or, put another way: what you resist, persists.

Ask yourself: Can I stop resisting and fully accept this situation exactly as it is, as if it were going to be this way forever?

Absolute Respect for the Other’s Freedom

God created us free. That means nothing in this universe, not even God, can override our will. For example, we may wish to heal, but if our will is not open to change, nothing external can help us. We may desire peace, but if our will is not to choose peace above everything else, nothing external will bring it.

I’ve often observed in myself that what seemed like a desire to help another was actually an attempt to change them because I couldn’t accept what their behavior was triggering in me. My desire wasn’t pure—it didn’t come from love, but from discomfort. Absolute respect for the other’s freedom means trusting their path, their pace, and their right to make mistakes.

Ask yourself: Am I trying to help with compassion, or am I trying to relieve my own discomfort about what the other person is making me feel?

Absence of Judgment

The ego is a thought system built on one premise: we are guilty, and therefore do not deserve to be loved. What we are supposedly guilty of, it calls sin. By believing in its reality, we come to think guilt is an inevitable part of our essential nature.

The Holy Spirit, on the other hand, does not see sin as real, nor does He perceive us as guilty. To Him, we are eternally innocent. What we call sin is merely an error in perception that needs loving correction, not a crime deserving punishment.

The ego always assumes destructive behavior comes from malicious intent, when it actually stems from ignorance, confusion, fear, or unconsciousness. As Hanlon’s Razor says, “Never attribute to malice that which is adequately explained by stupidity.”

Ask yourself: Can I see the wound or the ignorance behind the behavior, instead of judging it as malice or guilt?

Loving Creativity

It’s easy to take a stand against something that bothers us, to adopt an anti-whatever stance. But that’s like trying to put out fire with more fire. The Holy Spirit doesn’t attack error; He creates something new based on truth. He doesn’t fight darkness; He simply offers a luminous solution.

So, when I look at this situation that hurts me, I invoke the Holy Spirit, source of all inspiration, and I ask: Is there an alternative I haven’t seen? A luminous solution that isn’t a reaction to the problem, but a creative response to it? Help me see this painful thing with love.

Then I open my mind to receive, without hurry, and listen in silence. The answer will come today or tomorrow, not when it pleases Him to answer, but when I am ready to listen.

Within us lies all the wisdom in the Universe, waiting for us to seek it with honesty. Seek and you shall find.

I wish you, patiently, to seek peace.

With all my love,

A.
