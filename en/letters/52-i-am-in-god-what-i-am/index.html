52. I am in God what I am...

...and my being is will.

I am in God what I am,

and my being is will,

which, persevering in time,

exists in eternity.

This is the beginning of a poem by Rubén Darío, and it perfectly expresses what I've been contemplating this week.

On Thursday, during qigong class, we practiced a very simple exercise called Lachi: with our hands in front of the abdomen, as if holding an invisible ball, we inhaled slowly expanding that energetic ball, and as we exhaled slowly, we contracted it, feeling how the energy grew and concentrated between the palms. We did this for about 30 minutes, standing and with eyes closed. That night, I wrote this in my diary:

"Today I saw what I am in an incredible way. To begin with, I didn’t move the body at any moment—the body simply followed the energy, like a flag follows the wind. I stayed in that state for about 30 minutes, clearly observing that I am not the body; I am the energy that directs the body. From that perspective, I could see that I was everywhere—inside and outside the body. Depending on how I moved the energy, the body moved like a puppet.

But toward the end, I asked, “And who directs the energy?” Then I saw that, beyond the energy, as if coming from outside Creation but guiding it, I am Will. At that moment, the body began to shake, and I declared, “My will is that there be stillness,” and the body returned to a harmonious and gentle movement.

That Will was what directed not only this body, but all the bodies in the room and, ultimately, the entire universe. However, I had the option to “get in the way” and manipulate that Will in a manner that only affected my body—a will whose nature was to resist the greater Will. The result of choosing this individual will was felt as a disconnection, and the resulting movement was less beautiful and harmonious."

I am experiencing a tremendous identity crisis, and my ego is desperate because it can no longer find walls to cling to. Yet, I realize suffering is optional. If I identify with that cornered ego—with its fear, shame, guilt, anger, depression, pride…—then I suffer. But I am not obligated to make that choice.

At some point in childhood, I began to identify with the little body of that child they called Tono, and that’s when the illusion of separation began. But now I’m beginning to understand that if I say “Your Will is my will,” what I’m really saying is “Your Self is my self.” If I say “Thy Will be done” but still consider myself to be Antonio, a separate and mortal being, then my words are empty.

Yesterday, walking on the way to a rehearsal, I realized something:

I know—at least intellectually—that my true self is the infinite and eternal Self that is one with God. Now I’m beginning to see it in my direct experience, too. So why do I keep identifying with something less than that, something changeable and mortal? Choosing that is choosing suffering.

And then, spontaneously, my sense of identity shifted from Antonio—something that is born, changes, and dies—to the permanent Self, forever safe in the Mind of God.

Then there was peace.

I saw that my ego will still go through a thousand crises, but what I really am cannot be hurt. The most terrible thing that can happen in this world can’t even scratch me.

Still, my mind is not yet fully unified in that truth. There are still long stretches in which I forget, and go back to identifying with Antonio and his problems. Then I sing the same lines Rubén wrote at the end of his poem:

Lord, faith is dying!

Lord, look how I suffer.

Miserere! Miserere!

Take my hand, O Lord…

With all my love,

A.
