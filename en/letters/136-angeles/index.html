136. Ángeles

Help always comes.

The story of Nimbo is full of moments where the most gracious Providence made itself known. Here is one of them.

It was late March and there were four souls on the beach. In one hand I carried my hulking camera with its enormous tripod, in the other a beach bag with a mirror, water, and other items that turned out to be useless, my guitar over my shoulder, and dressed as if going to an Indian wedding. Naturally, my ego was walking behind me, kicking me in the butt and throwing sand in my hair. I felt alone and a little ridiculous, but I knew I had a mission, so I pressed on with a firm-clumsy step across the sand.

I set the camera in the exact spot where the Will wanted it, facing the approaching sunset, and stood in front of it to play Nimbo.

I had not reached the second chorus when I saw a woman approaching with a warm smile. I do not think she realized I was recording, because she walked into the frame and stood there watching me play, inaudibly beneath the sound of the sea. “Well, this take is not the one...” I laughed, and started talking to her.

She told me she had come over because she had felt the light that was with me. She told me she was in Cantabria those days looking for a premises for Cáritas. Her name was Ángeles, and in her smile I saw Christ coming to help me.

I explained that I was filming a music video and invited her to join. She agreed with delight, although she was sorry not to be better dressed. I told her she was perfect: with that scarf on her head like a turban and her walking stick she looked like a biblical shepherd. I filmed several shots of her alone, smiling at the camera, and finally joined the frame to play Nimbo for her. When the second-to-last verse arrived, I felt that instead of singing I should hug her, and that is what I did. And so the heart of the video was captured.
