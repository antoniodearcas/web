Nº 63 · May 18, 2025

I Choose Peace

Or how to change the world from within

The enemy is not “the others,” but that which lies within you that makes you see your brothers as enemies.

This is the message that keeps coming back to me these days.

It comes to me through Gandhi, who says: “The only devils in this world are those running around in our own hearts. That is where all our battles ought to be fought.”

It comes to me through the film Conclave: “When you say we must fight, what do you think we are fighting against? Against the men who, in their confusion, committed those terrible acts? No, my brother. The battle is here (placing his hand on his heart), within each of us. If we give in to hatred and fear, if we speak of sides instead of speaking of every man and woman […]”

I also remembered a similar idea I read from Thich Nhat Hanh: “When others see two sides at war, I do not take sides with either. I take the side of peace.”

Today I want to go deeper into this, because I see how much it’s needed—in my life, and I believe in everyone’s.

The first idea—fundamental to understanding what peace is and how to bring it into the world—is that this world is only a world of effects. I repeat: there are only effects here. What we call “the world” is just the projection of the movie. The script, the projector, and the director are located elsewhere, in a deeper place: the mind. Trying to change the world without looking inward is like trying to change the movie by scratching the screen. That’s why trying to change our behavior—or that of others—without looking beyond what the body does rarely works. We’re only interacting with effects, not with causes.

An example: many people try to give up sweets, but they do so without giving up the idea that sweets bring them something valuable. So they go on diets, hold out for a while… and then fall back into it with double the craving. They’ve tried to change the behavior (effect), but not the cause of the behavior (the belief). If they let go of the belief, the impulse would vanish on its own. Once the cause is removed, the effect disappears instantly.

The causes of everything that happens in your life and in the world exist at the level of ideas—the level of the mind. Hence the immeasurable value of understanding and training the mind. A Course in Miracles, which is the most powerful document I’ve found for doing this, puts it like this:

“The world you see is a witness to your state of mind, the outer picture of an inner condition.”

What happens in this world is a collective play, co-written by all our minds. It is a reflection of what lies in the collective psyche.

Now, what does it mean to know and train the mind?

Our mind is like the hardware of a computer: it is innocent and unchangeable. What we can do—and we cannot avoid doing it—is install an operating system. The ego is the one that comes pre-installed, factory settings, the default human thought system.

The ego is the thought system born from the idea of separation, along with all the beliefs that stem from it: lack, death, survival, war, racism, superiority, inferiority, violence, punishment, victims, guilt, suffering…

The world you see is the result of the ego’s activity. And not just what appears in the news. Literally everything our perception presents as separate is a product of the ego and its beliefs.

But the ego is not who we are. We are the mind, not the thought system. If we truly knew our mind—recognized its innate purity and perfection—we would immediately reject all the madness the ego suggests. That’s why the ego keeps us hypnotized, looking outward, and fears that we turn inward. This is also why all wise traditions say, in one way or another: “Know thyself.”

The only real way to change the world is to change our mind. If we haven’t done that first, everything we do will be like Don Quixote charging at windmills.

Now, the ego will say: “But what effect can a single person have in a world of billions? What impact can I possibly have if people like X and Z have much more power than me and use it for evil?”

They may have more power in the world in the sense of holding more influence over others’ behavior. But all minds are equally powerful. We are all equal in essence. We are all Children of God, created equal. Think of Jesus, Buddha, or Krishna—three individuals just like you and me, whose minds, of the same nature as ours, just did not buy into the ego’s illusions. Thousands of years later, their thoughts still transform the minds of those who adopt them as their own. A single mind firmly established in truth changes the world far more powerfully and enduringly than millions of minds in service to the ego.

Also, though bodies appear separate, at the level of mind there is no division. Each mind is a constant teacher of whatever it believes. That’s why it’s important to choose our company wisely—minds quickly attune to one another.

Minds ruled by the ego may unite in their crusade against the windmills, but each will do so for its own selfish motives. That kind of union, being rooted in fear, has limited power. They can only resort to force—and force, sooner or later, runs out. There is no unlimited supply of money or soldiers.

Only minds guided by love can truly unite. Two minds aligned in truth become one mind. The power of that is unstoppable.

Your mind is just as powerful as that of X and Z—and just as powerful as the mind of Jesus or Buddha. Your thoughts also affect everyone you relate to, and even beyond. Our minds are not limited by space. Our minds are not limited at all.

So, in the face of erupting conflicts in the world, what can you do, you who bear a divine mind?

The next time you face a conflict, you can say inwardly: “I choose peace.” The ego will jump with all sorts of reasons why this is insane, and why attack is a much better choice. To that say “I choose peace.” What you are choosing is what is called “the peace that passeth all understanding.” There’s nothing to understand, only a choice to be made.

Are you having an argument with your brother and feel the ego’s pull of violence? “I choose peace.”

Are you going through illness and fear tempts you? “I choose peace.”

Do politicians spread messages of hatred and tempt you to see your brothers as the enemy? “I choose peace.”

Does your partner say something hurtful and you’re tempted to respond with pride? “I choose peace.”

Is your boss unpleasant and you feel tempted to believe you’re worthless? “I choose peace.”

Are you troubled by what’s on the news? “I choose peace.”

Choose peace within yourself, no matter what is happening in the world.

By “choose peace,” I don’t mean sitting idly by. Nor do I mean joining protests raging for peace.

It’s not a moralistic call to correct anyone’s actions (I’m getting tired of tilting at windmills). This letter is just an invitation to cultivate an inner state—a reminder that this option is always available. The peace of God is within you, waiting to be chosen over the ego. Then, if you feel called to be an activist for peace, in whatever form, that’s a good place to start. How can you bring peace into the world without knowing it first within yourself?

Choosing peace is a choice made at the level of cause, not effect. And yet, by choosing it, the effects (your actions) will inevitably begin to move toward peace as well.

Jesus was the great teacher of this idea: even in his Passion, he did not lose that inner posture. Part of the message of that great mystery seems to be: nothing that happens in this world has the power to take away your peace—unless you give it that power yourself.

Choosing peace is reclaiming sovereignty over our mind, and no longer handing it over to the ego to drag us into conflict.

By doing this, little by little, an essential truth is revealed:

That the ego is not you. That you are the constant peace that has always been there—unchanged and unchangeable—beneath the noise of the world.

Choosing peace is remembering who you are.

To end with something inspiring, a quote from Thomas Merton, which serves as a guiding star. After choosing peace countless times, his mind finally opened to the reality of what we are, which he expressed like this:

“Then it was as if I suddenly saw the secret beauty of their hearts, the depth of their hearts where neither sin nor desire can reach, the core of their reality, the person that each one is in God’s eyes. If only they could see themselves as they really are. If only we could see each other that way all the time, there would be no more war, no more hatred, no more cruelty, no more greed… I suppose the big problem would be that we would fall to our knees and worship each other.”

With all my love,

A.
