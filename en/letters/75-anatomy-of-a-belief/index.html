Anatomy of a Belief

Are you aware of the lenses coloring your world?

What is a belief and how does it affect our lives?

A belief is an idea we accept as true. That much is obvious. What comes next is not so obvious, but it is of supreme importance if we want to be free.

When we accept an idea as true, it becomes installed in our mind.

The mind is not just our thoughts; it also includes sensations, sensory perceptions, and all kinds of mental formations (intentions, habits, emotional tendencies…).

Since we do not perceive reality directly, but only through our mind, the beliefs we hold shape every aspect of our experience.

You can imagine them as filters. If reality is like sunlight—transparent, containing the entire electromagnetic spectrum—beliefs are like colored sunglasses.

For example, the belief “The world is pink” could be like wearing pink glasses. By putting them on, you would have edited your direct perception of reality in such a way that it only shows you the pink frequency. Your entire experience of the world would appear pink, thus confirming your belief that the world is, self-evidently, pink.

That’s why it’s so difficult to doubt our own beliefs, and so easy to doubt those of others. Your entire experience of the world confirms your own beliefs. Not because reality is the way you think it is, but because you are filtering reality to only show you what you want to see.

If we don’t understand how our mind works and how beliefs operate, we fall into the trap of believing too much in our own beliefs. That is pride: the resistance to having our view of the world corrected. Those are the bars that lock us in our own world, doubting anything that might help us break free.

Now, if that wasn’t mystical enough, this surely is.

Reality is only One. There are as many ways of perceiving it as there are minds with beliefs, but all those “individual worlds” share the same root. All minds spring from the same source—the same Unlimited Mind. They are like myriads of flowers blooming from the same tree.

Or better yet, look at the forest in the image above. It seems to be full of different trees, doesn’t it? Here’s another photo, taken closer up.

What you’re seeing is, in fact, a single tree. It’s called Pando, and it’s the largest living organism in the world. That whole forest of aspens is a single organism, one DNA, but with many trunks. This is an invitation to question not only the reliability of our perception, but also our very conception of what a human being is.

Recognizing that unity of all parts—that unique identity beneath the apparent multiplicity—is to see with Love.

Everything in this Universe arises from that Love, from that Unity, from that one Reality, from that Unlimited Mind. If we could access Reality, we would see that it is, indeed, like Pando: one single Being, with all its apparent facets joined in perfect love.

But we don’t perceive that perfect love. We perceive separation—and from there arise conflict, judgment, lack, fear, and all the forms of suffering in the world. That perception of separation says nothing about the true nature of the world, but it says everything about the state of our own mind. As A Course in Miracles says, the world is a reflection of an inner condition.

If one wishes to reclaim the unconditional love and unshakable peace that are our inheritance, the path lies in questioning each and every belief we hold. Those that are true will remain intact. Those that are partly true will be cleansed and returned to us, wholly true. And those that are false will be dissolved. In doing so, the world we perceive will begin to transform accordingly—becoming increasingly luminous, harmonious, interconnected, beautiful.

Doesn’t this sound a bit like the idea of the Last Judgment, where “the good” go to heaven and eternal life, and “the bad” are cast into the flames? The Last Judgment is not a day at the end of time when God judges us, but rather each step on this path. “The good” and “the bad” are not people, but beliefs: some aligned with Truth, others born from error. The Last Judgment is every moment of this process of discerning what is true in our mind and what is not. That is the path home—the Home we never truly left, but which we lost sight of behind a thousand dark glasses.

With all my love,

A.
