123. Locutions

When one doesn't seem to get it, He speaks loud and clear.

In my last letter I wrote about how the thoughts "I think what you need is silence" and "Don't plan" guided my entire life into a desert. The letter was dense, and I did not want to overload it. But I left out something so beautiful that today I am dedicating this letter to it.

When I stepped back from my mentors and in general from all the voices that claimed to know what was best for me, more space opened up in my life. Without thinking too much about it, I devoted that space to studying the life, figure and message of Jesus, and my days began to unfold more and more in his company. There came a point when I began to experience, once again, what Saint John of the Cross called locutions: interior conversations with Him.

One day, quietly sweeping the dry leaves from the terrace and feeling very lonely, I asked:

— Lord, where do I belong?

I meant which spiritual or religious group was most suited to me. His answer was simply:

— In me.

That answer was not merely a thought. It had a profound effect on my soul. It was as if, in my solitude, anxious branches had grown in my soul reaching outward for contact. When I received that answer, I felt all those branches being cut, and my soul being drawn back to its center, where Christ shone as my true home and my true company.

Another time, walking through a park at the end of the day, I felt Him take my hand and join my walk. That gesture alone filled the air with a love that made me cry. Whatever worry or loneliness had been in my mind dissolved in the substance of his Presence. I said:

— Thank you for being here with me.
— I am always here. Thank you for being here with me!

And so began a conversation in which he answered my questions one by one. Like the experience I described above, his answers were not only words. They were more like infinite thoughts where one part, the crest of the wave, was verbal, but underneath there was an oceanic response, an ineffable energetic movement that illuminated some aspect of reality. By directing my attention in that way, the truth, always consoling, produced a corrective effect on my soul.

Sometimes his answers were brief:

— Lord, did you suffer?
— For everyone.

Others were more elaborate:

— Am I doing well not planning and living day by day?
— Yes, you are on the right track. {In a non-verbal way, he reminds me that, at this point, planning would come from a limited part of the mind, and that cannot be the will of God, which is total.}
— But sometimes one has to plan things.
— Yes, but those plans will come from wholeness and surrender, not from fear trying to control. Trust, rest in the will of the Father. It is much simpler than you want to make it. Your difficulty lies in accepting reality: because it is so hard for you to accept things as they are, you try to manipulate what happens so that things occur according to your preferences. If you learn to accept, that desire for control gradually becomes irrelevant.

And others were entirely non-verbal:

— Lord, sometimes I feel very alone.
{He directs my attention to a tree, and shows me something like "You are one with all life. There is only one life." In entering into communion with the tree, and through it with all life, solitude revealed itself, once again, as something simply impossible.}

Today he reminded me of an idea I had almost forgotten during these months of doubt and uncertainty: the one who asks questions is the ego, and I am not the ego.

So, as I gradually accept the reality that Jesus is always with me (and with everyone), the flowers of my initial fascination, laden with questions, are drying out, and in their place other fruits are growing: above all, the trust that I need not worry about anything. My life is His, He is my Shepherd, and it is not my role to tell him where we are going or how. His Presence is enough.

Instead of questions, He now simply sits beside me, and that instantly dissolves any suffering.

And if I am walking and feeling alone or afraid, He takes my hand. My half-closed hand, held in a hand of light, teaches me not to forget that He is always here with me.

And so we walk home, together and in silence.

With all my love,

Antonio
