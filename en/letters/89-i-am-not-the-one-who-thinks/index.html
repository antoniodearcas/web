Nº 89 · November 16, 2025

I Am Not the One Who Thinks

I Am

One month away from finishing my path in this monastery called Berklee College of Music, I reflect on what I’m discovering in this final semester. It isn’t easy to express, not only because it is essentially ineffable, but also because it directly negates what is familiar and obvious to all of us. And yet, once seen, it becomes undeniable. In previous letters I already spoke about the illusion of free will. Today I expand on that idea.

There is a proverb that says: “Before Enlightenment: chop wood and carry water. After Enlightenment: chop wood and carry water.” What this means is that the only thing that changes is the subject of the action: Who chops wood and carries water? Who am I?

I have discovered that my true will is God’s Will. I know that is the truth, and when I remember and embody it knowingly, I see that it is God who chops wood and carries water, not I, as a personal and independent entity. It is the entire universe that is writing these words. When I remember this, I see that everything is given to me from Love. I see that I don’t have to do anything or think anything. I can remain in complete silence, in an in-between point between the Creator and His Creation.

Imagine a pianist playing a wonderful piece. Christ is the pianist’s body. We are the fingers. The body does what the mind dictates; it has no ability to choose any note other than the one the pianist wants to play. A single finger, even less so.

And yet, as a finger, I hear the piece being played, and I feel anxiety about having to decide the next note, or I regret that supposed mistake from a few measures ago. My thoughts arise from a center that seems to say: “I am a separate individual, abandoned to my own fate, and if I don’t think, plan, and try to control what I do, nothing will happen, everything will be a mess, and I will end up dying.”

But I am not that. That’s just an enormous mistake in perspective. That’s the only sin, and one that does not ask for punishment, but for gentle correction.

I’m beginning to feel that the safest path is to stop thinking completely from that place. I can let go of all my thoughts, and it will be like pulling weeds from my garden, or clearing obstacles from the river so the water can flow. That is how the harmony of the piece is restored, previously distorted by a rebellious finger.

I am not the thoughts that pass through my mind. The source I come from is very different: I did not create myself; I am the fruit of my Creator. Only He knows me fully and, being one with Him, His love for me is perfect. I am, in fact, an extension of His love. Creation follows only His loving Will.

I can choose to be guided by the thoughts that pass through my mind—thoughts based on a lie—or I can choose to become very still, go beyond them, and surrender trustingly to the Will that is creating me in this very instant from love.

If I believe my thoughts, I will be denying myself access to love, which is the awareness of this unity with everything. The result is the appearance of an individual, separate, disconnected existence. From that mirage, all one can do is search for love, which of course hides in illusory places like the future or the past, always in what is not here and now. What remains is a life devoted to seeking.

But there is also the possibility of recognizing that separation itself is false. Then one begins to recognize that all the thoughts that pass through our mind come from separation and are illusions. The process of letting go of all identities that do not include the whole then begins. None of them are true; we were never something separate.

In the end, one recognizes one’s true self again in silence. And there the future and the past disappear. Time disappears. In its place remains the eternal now. Only the Presence remains—the Presence that is me and that is God. As Meister Eckhart said: “The eye with which I see God is the same with which God sees me.” I am not my Creator, but I am not different from Him either. Like the pianist, his body, and one of his fingers. How terrible the piece would sound if the hands insisted on having their own will without being aware that there is a score the whole orchestra is playing. Letting go of the ego’s thoughts is returning to the harmony of the piece that wants to pass through you; the piece that was written for you from the deepest love.

I speak of all this as if saying that I can do this or that. Saying “I can” is a manner of speaking. It implies that I have a will of my own, and that doesn’t seem to be the case. This is the natural process of spiritual growth in the human being: from the belief in separation and the ability to choose, to the knowledge of unity and the rest found in total trust. That is the apparent journey of the soul. But at no point was the separation real; it was only a limited perspective necessary so as not to fry a baby’s nervous system with all the information in the universe. It is a natural protection system, and with the right work, we can expand beyond it.

As I train my nervous system and mind to hold more and more, I begin to have glimpses of the truth: there is only unity. Everything has been, is, and will always be in the hands of the Will. The path is already drawn. All decisions already made.

Therefore, I now recognize that the true path is to get used to inner silence and stillness. When I seek that, in that moment my apparently individual will has joined the true Will. In those moments I am present, and although my body may ache or my mind may be restless, there is a sense of tenderness and peace: “Ah, at last, I am here with myself. Why didn’t I do this sooner?”

This does not mean remaining motionless like the plants in my apartment though. With the right perspective, it is possible to maintain inner stillness in the midst of a battle.

However, when we occupy our mind with the things of the world, looking outward, the illusion is unleashed again: that we are an individual being in charge of our own life. The trick the ego uses to achieve this is very clever. In reality, there is only one presence in the universe: the Presence of God. We cannot escape it, because nothing exists outside it. But we can deny it. And how does one deny the most basic fact of existence? By changing the Presence’s name.

Instead of being the Presence of God in us, we call it Antonio, Marta, or John. If “I am” is the Presence of God, all we have to do to conceal it is simply finish the sentence: “I am Antonio.” Then we will continue to experience the same reality, but we will interpret it in a completely distorted way.

As always, what I write in 2,000 words was described far more eloquently by someone else in 100. This is the first poem of the Tao Te Ching:

A Tao that can be defined
is not the constant Tao of all things.

A name that can name something
is not the constant Name of all things.

Heaven and earth begin without a name.
Everything in the world is born from its name.

Those who never seek
will meet the mystery of the Tao.

Those who always seek
will meet its surface.

Mystery and surface are identical,
but names make them distinct.

This identity is very hard to understand;
it is the gateway to the subtlest enigma.

So, after all this complexity, what is there to do? For now, my strategy is condensed in these words:

Pray, relax and leave the rest to God.

With all my love,

A.
