58. Coherence and Incoherence

I am never upset for the reason I think

56. What are emotions?

In my last letter, I spoke about how emotions are a form of incoherent energy. The more incoherence there is in the waves of the lake, the more chaotic its surface becomes, and the less clearly it reflects. Today, I want to explore this idea of coherence and incoherence, because it’s a helpful tool for understanding ourselves.

These terms, believe it or not, come from quantum physics. They are used to describe the behavior of a quantum system.

When a system is coherent, all the waves are in phase, and that’s when the magic of the quantum world happens:

  • Superposition of states: Schrödinger’s cat is both dead and alive at the same time.

  • Quantum entanglement: two particles become entangled and behave as one, even if they’re on opposite sides of the universe.

  • Wave-particle duality: light is both a wave and a particle at once.

This is what explains the inexplicable: synchronicities, telepathy, intuition, premonitions... They are glimpses into a world of mysterious coherence. We’ve all had experiences like these. They clearly defy logic — and yet they are. That’s why Richard Feynman, one of the greatest physicists of the 20th century, said: “If you think you understand quantum mechanics, you don’t understand quantum mechanics.”

So, when does a system lose its coherence? When it interacts with its environment. The system then collapses, that ideal quantum state "breaks down," and the system begins to behave classically (which is how most of us perceive our day-to-day reality).

Imagine a choir singing in a silent room. Everyone hears each other perfectly and sings in tune, in perfect harmony. That is a coherent system: isolated and in perfect balance. They’re immersed in a state of supernatural beauty. The singers have forgotten what day it is, who they are, or what they have to do afterward.

But suddenly, an ambulance goes by, construction starts in the room next door, a baby cries, and a phone rings playing “tell me what you want, what you really really want…”

What happens then?

The singers get distracted; they disconnect. They stop functioning as one instrument. The system loses its coherence. Instead of being in a state of unity and mystical timelessness, now it’s Tuesday. One is hungry, another wants to complain to the construction workers. Everyone is frustrated and trying to hear themselves and each other with little success.

What a stark contrast between the world of coherence and the world of incoherence.

What I just described is exactly what happens with our minds. This is not an analogy. We are quantum systems, and all its paradoxical laws speak of what we are.

Whether we trust in modern physics, the words of Jesus, or the lyrics of Bob Marley, we know — even if only intuitively — that the Universe is one. And yet, we don’t perceive it that way. Quite the opposite: we perceive a world of separation, made of limited and impermanent objects, among which we include ourselves.

This happens because our untrained minds are in a state of incoherence. And in a way, that’s a good thing. Opening up to coherence all at once would be absolutely catastrophic. Our nervous system already has enough to handle just processing what happens in our small, separate lives — let alone suddenly accessing all the information in the Universe.

Our mind, in its incoherent state, acts like a filter. That filter protects us from infinite overload and the strangeness of the quantum world. The downside is that the more incoherent the mind, the more opaque the filter becomes, and the darker, more limited, and more illusory our perception of the world is. In a word: alienation.

On the other hand, the more coherence there is, the more light passes through the filter. And with it, we draw closer to a deeper perception of the world’s beauty, to the recognition of truth, and to the direct experience of the unity of love. In a word: communion.

William Blake, the English poet, painter, and mystic, said it this way: “If the doors of perception were cleansed, everything would appear to man as it is — infinite.”

What does all of this have to do with emotions?

The key lies hidden in this wonderful quote by Carl Jung: “He who looks outside dreams; he who looks inside awakens.”

Earlier we saw that a system is coherent when it’s not affected by external forces. But the moment it interacts with its environment, it loses balance.

If you are emotionally agitated and look outward, your distorted perception will show you an illusory world. This bad dream will invite you to lash out in anger at someone, to cling to the idea that others are to blame for your suffering, to treat people as objects out of desire or pride, or to flee from situations out of fear or shame… This is what Buddha called maya — the illusory world perceived as reality by minds in a state of incoherence. Interacting with maya is living as the drifting log I mentioned in my previous letter.

But we are not a drifting log — we are waves endowed with the full power of Poseidon. If our will is to be turbulent, so we shall be. But if instead we choose to become still, like a mirror unmoved by the waves around us, then that is what we will become — if that’s truly our only desire.

There are a thousand paths toward coherence, but all of them require taking responsibility for what we feel. That is one of the great laws of how to work with emotions: it’s in your garden — no matter how it got there, it’s your responsibility.

“I am never upset for the reason I think,” says A Course in Miracles, precisely to help you let go of the story emotions are trying to tell you (“It’s their fault!” or “If you do this, something terrible will happen!”). By letting go of the story — knowing it’s an illusion — we create space in our awareness to do something far more coherent: to observe the energy that is here and now, somewhere in your body.

That’s where the truth of the emotion lies — without stories or labels, just pure energy floating among the body’s sensations.

Everything begins with that act of turning your attention inward — toward the reality of what you're feeling. The word conversion might refer to that turning. It is the first step toward coherence, toward correct perception, toward inner freedom, toward regaining sovereignty over our own kingdom, toward the un-covering of who we truly are.

That’s all for today. Here's a quick summary:

  • Our mind filters reality to protect us from quantum overload. As a result, our perception becomes incoherent with reality.

  • Coherence is a state of energetic harmony. The more coherence we experience, the more internal balance, clarity, and truthful perception we gain — and the less we suffer.

  • The more incoherence, the more agitated our inner world becomes, and the more inaccurate our perception (i.e. separation, confusion, suffering). Incoherence arises from interaction with the external world.

  • Emotions are a form of energetic incoherence. When we project them outward, we increase incoherence. Only by looking inward do we begin to awaken and restore coherence.

  • Coherence requires emotional responsibility: letting go of mental stories and observing the energy present in the body.

With all my love,

A.
