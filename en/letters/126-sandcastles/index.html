126. Sandcastles

What survives the tide?

There are many kinds of sandcastles: large, small, built by children's hands, by architects' hands, some swept away quickly by the tide, others lasting weeks, and in the end all of them surrendering to entropy. Forms that appear, endure for a time, and dissolve. But the sand, the substance, that remains.

At the Council of Nicaea (325 AD), the Creed was drawn up, describing the divine filiation of Jesus Christ by saying that He and the Father are of the same substance. Saint John also opens his gospel speaking of how Christ is the creative principle of the Universe.

Christ is the sand of which the Universe is made. But unlike sand, he is not an inert substance. He is consciousness itself, the light that illuminates the world and allows you to know it in this instant. He is the subjective element, that constant "I" at the center of your experience that wisdom calls us to investigate.

And then, here we are, you and I: human beings of flesh and blood, with a screen in front of us, sitting here in a world that appears to be made of matter. We are sandcastles that are born, change, and dissolve. At this level we give ourselves names: Antonio, Juan, Betül, Jesus... names that seem to encapsulate that "I" inside a body. Everything outside the body is not "me."

But when one closes one's eyes and investigates that "I" in depth, it does not take long to discover that, in reality, it does not refer to the castle but to the sand. Essentially we are sand: the consciousness that is at once unchanging and the substance of the changing world.

The only difference between Jesus and us is that when Jesus said "I," he was referring exclusively to the sand. He accepted nothing temporal, nothing subject to entropy, as a limitation of his identity. He stayed only with the eternal, fully accepting his divine nature as the Son consubstantial with the Father. That does not mean he rejected his human nature. On the contrary, it reached its fullest expression in that boundless I.

The question is: who are you and I? Are we the castles, or are we the sand?

If I say I am the castle, I have chosen to identify with the body. That is the ego, faithful believer in the god of separation, which tells me that at the end of this limited life death awaits. The one who puts his mind in service of the ego sees only castles, and the wars and romances that play out among their inhabitants. The one who looks that way never notices the omnipresence of the sand.

But if I say I am the sand, I have chosen to believe in the God of Jesus, and then death is just one more change, not an ending. I still see the castles, but they are no longer defined by their variable forms, only by the one sand. The one who looks this way sees only Christ. The tide will come and carry the castle away, but the sand remains.

In saying I am the sand, I am no longer looking at the human body with which I tend to identify. I am looking at the substance of which my entire experience in this moment is made. There are other people in my experience now. I am writing from an airplane. I hear a baby crying, the morning light comes through the windows: clouds, blue sky, the Mediterranean Sea, all made of the sand of consciousness, all "I," all radiant Christ.

The body of Antonio appears, changes over years, and one day will disappear. The same will happen with the voice that sounds in my mind and claims to be me. But that voice is not me. It is only a name written in the sand, a strange poem about time fears, longings, and blueberries. Castles. Forms that arise and dissolve. But what remains? The sand, consciousness, the timeless I that we share with Jesus and with the Father.

Jesus told us: Deny yourself.

He also invited us to pray saying: Hallowed be Thy name (referring to the Father).

Both ideas converge: I am the eternal sand, not the temporary castle.

Cleanse your name, your sense of "I," from what appears in your experience. That comes and goes. But I am always I. Stay only with what remains. Return to the recognition that eternal life is already here, and it is you, dressed up as time.

The body will still be yours, but now it will be guided by an integrated mind, not a divided one. Your daily life will be guided by the Holy Spirit, not by Legion.

Your steps through this world will write a love poem in the sand, a call to the other castles to recognize: you are me. And when the tide carries the castle away, nothing essential will have changed, nothing essential will have been lost. The sand will remain. The Son, at last without disguise, will open his eyes again to the happy face of the Father.

With all my love,

Antonio
