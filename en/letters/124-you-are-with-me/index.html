124. You Are With Me

Founding a monastery of two

Almost four years ago, my heart opened to a more direct communication with God. It was a period so difficult and profound that I felt the moment had come to leave for a monastery. But the Voice, which always speaks through all things, told me clearly: no, my path was to remain in the world and start a family.

Almost three years ago, Betül came into my life. Our relationship began in Boston. She was studying at Harvard and her lab was a seven-minute bike ride from my apartment, so we spent a lot of time together. Coming from Turkey and Spain respectively, the United States gave us the opportunity to create our own culture, tailored to both of us.

The heart of that culture was silence, which from the very beginning accompanied us on our dates and wrapped itself around our conversations. This silence was unusual, neither uncomfortable nor empty: it was filled with peace and felt strangely familiar. That helped me recognize, very early in our relationship, that she was the person I was going to marry. In that silence I would look at her thinking: "here we are, two strangers, united by a mysterious peace that can only come from God. It is like an arranged marriage, but arranged not by our parents, but by God himself."

A few months later, she began to feel curious about that enormous book I read every morning: A Course in Miracles. She came from Islam, but as she gradually came to know what was in my heart, she began to feel that call that Jesus makes not only through truth, but primarily through affection. Shortly after, without me saying a word, she picked up the book and did the first lesson. That was how we began our shared contemplative practice. The peace we had already been sensing took on the name of Christ, and without realizing it, we founded a monastery of two.

A year and a half ago she finished her PhD at Harvard and returned to her native Istanbul. The morning after dropping her at the airport, her absence in my apartment was like a silent thunderclap. I threw myself fully into that emptiness and, like Gandalf seizing his sword as he fell against the Balrog, I took up the sword of truth and launched myself against loneliness. The account of that battle is in my letter "61. Love and Attachment. Sky and Clouds."

What I did not tell in that letter was that, in defeating the monster of loneliness, my mind opened to the fact that loneliness is, in reality, impossible. I already knew that my consciousness was non-local, but now I accepted it as my everyday reality. For that reason, I never fully experienced what a long-distance relationship actually is. The year and a half that followed her departure we have enjoyed a non-local relationship, one in which we recognize that our presence is not limited by space. That omnipresent presence, always perfectly comforting, which never let us be overcome by loneliness or the sense of separation, was Christ himself shining in our hearts.

She calls me canim, "darling" in Turkish. I call her the same. And we both know that, in reality, canim is the light in the other, and there is only one light.

That recognition led us beyond the classic "I love you," and we began using the phrase "you are me." It says the same thing, but strips from the expression everything that looks like love and in reality is not.

One day, Betül went a step further, by accident. Wanting to express, from Istanbul to Madrid, that presence which transcends space, she tried to say "you are with me" in Spanish. But instead of saying "estás conmigo" she said "eres conmigo." "Estás” is a reference to being somewhere, bur “Eres” refers to pure being. You are with me, not here or there, but in being. You are me.

That is the rock on which Jesus is teaching us to build. A love cultivated in the cross, in forgiveness, in the yes to the storm that leads to the transcendence of conflict and separation. A love not based on emotion, not subject to space or time. It includes without exception, and eternally, every living being. So I apply the phrase not only to Betül, but also to you who are reading this: eres conmigo.

With all my love,

Antonio
