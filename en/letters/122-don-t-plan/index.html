122. Don't Plan

The hardest thing is to do nothing

Lately I have been praying the Our Father often, contemplating each phrase as the complete prayer it is.

Thy will be done on earth as it is in Heaven.

What happens when one surrenders one's personal will?

"Your task is not to seek for love, but merely to seek and find all of the barriers within yourself that you have built against it."
- A Course in Miracles

The last time I was in Istanbul, Betül told me "I think what you need is silence."

After that, I began to feel an aversion to Instagram and abandoned my work there. I also stepped back from the mentors I had been working with, and mainly from their group sessions.

The idea "Don't plan" also returned, this time with a clarity and weight that left no room for negotiation. What I mean by that is that one day, after finishing breakfast, I stood up to put the dishes on the sink when, out of nowhere, a kind of syllogism crossed my mind:

I am what I am.

What is, is the will of God.

Therefore, what I am is the will of God.

This did not arrive simply as a thought. It was more like being struck by lightning, like putting my fingers in a socket and, in doing so, having a momentary vision of the entire electrical system of the world. Except that instead of the electrical system, I felt I was in communion with Creation, that is, with the will of God.

From that perspective, it was absolutely evident that I do not have an individual, separate, autonomous will. The individual will, by definition, would be the will of something that is not God’s will, and therefore the will of an illusion.

That experience shook me deeply. "Don't plan" was no longer just an invitation. It was an inevitability.

So I fell into a deeper level of self-abandonment, and my mind filled with fears and judgments. Those fears and judgments had always been there. I had been feeling them for years, pulling the strings from the shadows. Now, this surrender to inactivity provoked a resurgence of all that negativity, trying to force me back into action. What had once been a mafia operating in the shadows was now filling the streets with shootouts in broad daylight.

At the core, the fear was of being incapable of doing anything worthwhile with my life. All these gifts and talents going to waste under the command of a weak and scattered will. I put this under the microscope in my recent letter "120. Change of Owner."

During this difficult period I kept guiding the morning meditations and observed that there, and only there, that level of abandonment made sense. The more I let go of any desire for control and rested in the emptiness of the present moment, the more beautiful and healing the meditation I guided became.

The days passed, and my conscious mind did not know what to do or when to do it. Many days I did nothing, or at least nothing visible or practical. Some days I was incapable of the simplest tasks: replying to a message or making myself a meal. I was living firsthand the meaning of Jesus's words: "Without me you can do nothing."

Yesterday morning I went through another wave of fear and guilt. You have to pass through it. The gate of heaven is covered in thorns. I repeated once again the machete-prayer: "Holy Spirit, help me to see this with Love."

In the afternoon, Betül came home, as joyful and radiant as ever, and said "Okay, let's organize the afternoon: first we'll go return this package, then we can go to a café to work, then etc etc." I listened to her, happy on one side, a little tense on the other. "Ah! She's planning!" But I noticed such joy, simplicity and love in her words that it was evident this was good, and after two months of not planning anything at all, I surrendered to her plan and made it mine too.

The afternoon unfolded around it. I say around because sometimes we followed it perfectly, and other times we drifted. But it did not matter because everything was good, everything was held in love.

We ended up painting with watercolors some flowers that had been given to Betül for the engagement, and at the end of the day, on my way to brush my teeth, a lightbulb went on in my mind and I understood:

The entire process I just lived through with "don't plan" had as its purpose to take aim at my ego: all the dark and unreal identities that used to run my days. When I planned, I did it from demand, from the fear that wants to control, from desire that is never satisfied. I was in the hands of a blind tyrant, and this period has been a general strike to remove him from the throne.

Today, however, I could feel how Betül planned from intelligence, love and joy. It was clear that if we followed the plan it would have been a balanced and pleasant afternoon, and if we did not, it would have been perfect too.

This, once again, seems like the most obvious thing in the world when explained, but understanding something intellectually is one thing, and being it is another entirely. To be love, you have to let go of everything that is not love. You have to align with the truth, even if reluctantly, as in my experience of the syllogism, and let the creatures of the night stir before returning to the nothingness from which they came. The path is not easy, nor does it pretend to be. Salvation and the cross are inseparable.

Yesterday I discovered that, after two months clearing shadows, there was finally space in my mind to live a little closer to what Augustine captured in his beautiful commandment:

Love, and do what you will.

With all my love,

Antonio
