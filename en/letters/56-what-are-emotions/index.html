56. What are emotions?

Let's talk about energy

This photo is from several springs ago. It was 2021, in the midst of the pandemic, and at that time, emotions—especially anxiety—overwhelmed me to the point where I became completely dysfunctional.

That situation led me, for the first time, to ask myself: What are emotions? I realized that, although they played a decisive role in my life, I actually knew nothing about them. The first thing I did was search on the internet “What are emotions?” and I started digging. What I found didn’t help much, and it took me some time to begin finding truly valuable answers. But I suppose the most valuable thing at that moment was simply asking the question.

Emotions are among the main causes behind the chaos and suffering we see in the world. They distort our perception until it becomes completely illusory, making us fight each other like Don Quixote against windmills. In doing so, they twist our will until it becomes incoherent, useless, powerless. And yet, at 26 years old, no one had ever clearly explained to me what emotions were, or given me a model that could help me learn to navigate them. I was like a shipwrecked sailor at the mercy of the waves.

After five years of intense exploration and development, I’m still in stormy waters—such is the world. But with the right information and training, my mind has changed a lot. I used to feel like a victim. I felt I was on the side of effects, with the causes lying beyond my power. Now I feel more and more on the side of causes, with greater authority and creative capacity over the state of my inner world and my life.

In today’s letter, I want to share what I would have liked to find the first time I searched “What are emotions?” online. In that sense, it’s like a letter to my past self. I’ll combine ideas from several sources I’ve been exploring: modern science, Eastern wisdom, the work of Dr. Hawkins, A Course in Miracles, and my own observation. It’s a broad topic, so I’ll divide it into several letters.

I’ll start at the beginning: emotions are energy. So before speaking specifically about emotions, let’s talk about energy and some of the ways it manifests in our lives.

Everything in this universe is made of energy.

Even if we don’t initially perceive it that way, quantum physics has shown this to be a fact. At the atomic level, what we perceive as solid matter is actually made up of 99.99999% “empty space” (in quotes because that “empty space” is full of electromagnetic fields). The remaining 0.000001% consists of subatomic particles, which are not solid matter either, but rather concentrated energy fields, constantly vibrating and interacting.

From this point of view, we are more like clouds of energy than solid objects.

That shift in perspective (from Newtonian mechanics to quantum physics) is essential for understanding what we are. We’re not logs of wood drifting in a stormy sea. We are a wave—inseparable from all other waves, and interacting with them at every moment.

Now, the energy we’re made of, just like light, can manifest in a variety of different colors. All colors come from white light, and all forms of energy come from a primordial energy. In China, it's called Qi; in India, prana; in the West, we usually refer to it as “life force” or “vital energy.”

Emotions are a specific type of that energetic expression. Other relevant types of energy that make us who we are include the energy of thought, creativity, sexuality (jing), and spirit (shen).

The energetic cloud we are is made up of all these energies, and depending on the proportion in which each one appears, we behave in different ways. Let’s briefly look at what each one is.

Right now, for example, it's spring; jing energy (sexual energy) is at its peak: flowers bloom, skirts get shorter, birds sing, boys make their moves. But this energy doesn’t only drive reproduction—it’s also responsible for the body’s vitality and all healing processes. It can also transform into creative energy, allowing us to move forward with projects or feel more inspired.

The energy of thought is much more powerful than we like to admit. It creates our perception of the world and determines the energetic quality of our lives and each of our actions. Our thoughts are not private in the sense that they are not energetically separate from everything else. If the thoughts we hold are aligned with love or truth, that is the energy we are bringing into the world. If our thoughts are resentful or fearful, for example, then that is the world we are creating. In that sense, every thought is a prayer, and it has a powerful effect on how our life unfolds. That’s why knowing the mind—and feeding it with what we want to cultivate in our lives—is of absolute importance.

Creative energy is what gives rise to new things. This includes everything from a poem or a song to a perception or a baby. Our place in the universe is very high, because we share this creative capacity with the Creator. We can use creative energy in alignment with truth, love, and beauty, or we can use it to fabricate illusions. What we can’t do is stop creating—because we are creativity itself, and our mind, which is the tool we create with, never sleeps.

Spiritual energy is the most powerful and the most subtle. Creative energy gives rise to perceptible things, it creates forms, and is tied to the individual who makes them. Spiritual energy does not give rise to perception; it doesn’t produce anything new—it reveals something eternal, something that is always there. And unlike creative energy, spiritual energy is experienced as something impersonal, something that clearly transcends the individual. It recognizes the fundamental oneness of the Universe and extends the Love and Peace of God.

Returning to the central theme of this series, emotions are another type of energy. Perhaps their main characteristic is that they are incoherent.

Imagine that energy is like waves on the surface of a pond. By incoherence, I mean that if there are many waves coming from many different places (like on a rainy day), the surface will end up in a chaotic state, where waves collide and create interference. That pond will be unable to offer a clear reflection of the sky. This would be an emotionally turbulent state—like what one experiences when feeling anxious or confused. This state is analogous to noise.

In contrast, a state of coherence would be one where the waves are synchronized, aligned, moving together in harmony. They no longer interfere with each other, but rather collaborate, reinforcing one another and creating clear, beautiful geometric patterns. A pond in that state will reflect the sky clearly and beautifully. In emotional terms, this would be a state of calm, flow, mental clarity, and balance. This state is analogous to music.

At the end of the path, the state of total coherence is stillness and silence. That lake becomes a mirror of the sky. That is the state of peace and enlightenment. It is what Pythagoras called the Music of the Spheres.

I’ll leave it here for today. Since this is a lot of information to digest, here’s a summary:

• Everything in the universe, including ourselves, is energy in constant interaction.

• We are like energetic clouds made up of different types of energy (thought, sexuality, creativity, spirit, emotion).

• Emotions are forms of incoherent energy that distort our perception and will.

• The lack of emotional education creates internal and external chaos, causing suffering and incoherence in our actions.

• Understanding emotions empowers us, moving us from the role of victim to that of conscious creators of our lives.

With all my love,

A.
