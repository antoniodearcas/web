132. To Row, or Not to Row

Today I understand why I was wrong.

A few days ago, a false and enormously influential belief was revealed and dissolved in my mind. It has to do with work, but above all, with the worker.

THE EXPERIMENT

At the beginning of my path, I adopted a phrase of Jesus as the thesis of what my life was going to be: "Seek first the Kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you."

The Kingdom of God is where the will of God is done; where He reigns. If there is no other will, sin cannot exist. The Kingdom is Heaven, and it is where God keeps our original innocence intact. It is, therefore, a transcendent reality, not in the future, but eternally now.

But Jesus also said that his righteousness had to be sought. In Spanish, Jesus uses the word “justice.” This is perhaps even harder to understand than the Kingdom, since it derives from It but is also in relation to this world.

After years of praying for guidance on this, I arrived at the understanding that the justice Jesus was referring to was the Justice of God. The only thing the world's justice and God's justice have in common is that both define it as "to each what is due according to their nature."

Where they differ radically is in what that nature is: the world says we are mortal sinners who deserve judgement, severe correction if not punishment or death. God says we are His Son, eternally innocent, and therefore deserve only love, which is all God gives and can give. His famous Last Judgment is an infinite and eternal "I love you, you are still as I created you."

That definition made justice also something transcendent and disembodied: remember that you are still innocent and God loves you.

The phrase of Jesus was reduced to this: Seek first the Kingdom (= your innocence) and also his righteousness (= your innocence), and all these things will be added to you.

A clear example of how human beings simplify the message of Jesus to comfortably maintain our beliefs and worldly ways. With that interpretation, all I had to do was look within and know I was loved. Having done that, I could relax and wait for "all these things to be added."

RESULTS

To that search, real but incomplete, I have dedicated these last 7 years, with considerable success, because the one who seeks finds, especially when

single-mindedly seeking something they already have eternally.

Recognizing original innocence as the only truth about myself has brought great blessings.

But, despite having recognized that inner truth, in my daily life there was still a great deal of suffering. I felt called to do something where the greatness of God would shine, but without knowing how to get there. I asked for guidance and surrendered, but nothing happened. My days were well described by the quote from Epictetus: "there can be no peace for the man who has seen what he could become."

Finally, I discovered the belief that was preventing me from moving forward: the idea that effort is unnecessary when one is in God. Everything flows with the river of existence, and it is God who acts. Everything happens easily, everything arrives miraculously.

I have spent years building a spirituality based on that.

It is not entirely false: my best songs arrived with the greatest ease. The Esferas letters often did too; after writing so many there is hardly any effort anymore. The recording of the album, for example, arrived miraculously once the blockages were undone, as if served on a silver platter.

But what about its release?

I was now being called to new and difficult things: building a community, sharing on social media, publishing my music, creating a home, a professional career. In a word: building. And building requires effort. But every time I felt resistance, I abandoned the action and fled back inward, interpreting the resistance as "I have drifted from the will, this is the ego."

In essence: when the Wind was strong, I used the billowing sails to advance without effort. But when the Wind seemed to stop, instead of rowing, I abandoned the action and turned inward, seeking to realign with Him. Not so much out of laziness, but out of fear of feeling that the Wind had disappeared.

Saint John of the Cross said: "During the time of the dryness of this night... spiritual persons suffer great trials, not so much from the aridities they endure, as from the fear of having lost their way, thinking that all spiritual good is over for them and that God has abandoned them."

THE CORRECTION

In these last few weeks, the Holy Spirit guided me to maintain a daily practice, working energetically on my solar plexus, the center of action and of "I can." All kinds of guilt, grief and shame were purged until, finally and thanks to God, the wall of pride that defended my incorrect position was brought down. As the light of truth entered, I saw my error, wept for it and for the time lost, and then it dissolved.

I now see that when the Wind disappeared, God was not asking me to abandon my action, suffer and eventually turn back to Him. He was teaching me that, even when I do not feel it, I still belong to Him and He continues to guide my actions. For this it was necessary that my soul abandon all action, saying "Father, I am not capable, take care of it yourself." Again in the words of Saint John, addressed to the soul that suffers in this way:

"Let it not be afflicted by this, but consider it a great blessing, for God is freeing it from itself and taking the matter into his own hands."

What I now see is that whether I feel the sweetness of his presence or not, my action comes from Him. If the action happens in a state of grace, I give thanks for the wind. If the action happens in pure effort, I give thanks for the strength. In either case, what drives me is always God himself.

I now see that the question is not whether effort is needed or not. The question is not whether to act or not. The question is: who is it that acts? I know that in reality there is only one will, and it is the will of God. If I am at a point in my life where it is time to lay brick upon brick, who am I to tell the Creator that, if He loves me, this should be able to happen without effort? Should I wait for gravity to disappear in order to rise, or should I go for it now with all that I’ve got?

CONCLUSION

I no longer perceive effort as the sweat of punishment presented in Genesis:

"By the sweat of your face you shall eat bread, till you return to the ground, for out of it you were taken; for you are dust, and to dust you shall return."
— Genesis 3:19

As my identification with the dust lessens, I am beginning to recognize in myself the effort of the Gospel, of the good news:

"I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me."
— Philippians 4:13

The effort is still there, always necessary while we walk through the world. But its meaning has changed for me: it no longer comes from the force of the ego, but from the strength of God shining through me.

Same reality, two different names that mark the huge difference between feeling in exile or at home.

I now see that the righteousness Jesus was referring to is not only the loving Judgment of God: it is letting Him drive me through the necessary challenges, giving me not only direction but also the strength to advance. It is ordering my life and my character according to my nature.

It is to stop resisting my destiny, which is the cause of suffering. As I stop resisting, I see that my destiny is not in my hands, and I only have to say "Yes, Lord," and lay the next brick.

In the Old Testament, the pinnacle of spiritual development was defined as being a just man: saying yes to the will of God and making it one’s law and refuge. Doing what corresponds to one's nature: if I am a painter, I paint; if I am a carpenter, I work the wood, knowing that that pleased a hidden God.

In His Words, Jesus says that to live well one must add the good news to the Old Testament. He does not abolish the law, he completes it by giving it its ultimate end: God himself. But not only discovering the Kingdom within: also living today accordingly.

Not only the Spirit; also character.

Not only the eternal; also the temporal.

Not only the true; also the good.

I now understand why Aristotle said that the ultimate goal of life was the development of character. Fifteen days ago I would have said, blind with pride, that Aristotle did not look high enough to see the true goal. Now I hear his words. I look at my life, which is a reflection of my character, and humbly recognize the path: keep working on this letter until it is finished, do the push-ups I have to do, practice piano until smoke comes out, sow, tend, make whatever efforts are required. All of that, without ever forgetting where the effort that drives me to grow comes from.

It is perfectly compatible to be in a state of grace and make a great effort. One only needs to recognize who the Doer is.

God does not abolish gravity. He gives us the strength to rise against it.

I have been called to fulfill several missions; that is my logos, my reason for being in this world, the end that guides my actions and my days.

How can I know the Logos, the ultimate reason, Christ, without first accepting my personal logos?

Now I accept it. My work is no longer only to seek God; now it is to let Him shape me: with hammer blows if I am to be a great sculpture; under great pressure if I am to be a diamond.

With all my love,

Antonio
