62. To rest

or to bow to the demands of the ego

This Friday I finished my seventh semester at Berklee. Today, Saturday, I sat down to write a letter. I decided to write it by hand, since I’m developing a new handwriting style. I wrote three pages that looked like this. If writing Esferas is already intense, doing it in cursive and without any cross-outs is a titanic task. By the time I finished, I felt exhausted.

It’s been an intense semester, and today I clearly felt the need to rest. Still, there was the temptation to push myself to keep going: transcribe the letter to the computer, edit it, create the email, and repeat the whole process in English. At least two more hours of intense focus. I felt like I should do it. But I don’t trust “shoulds.”

I remembered that only the ego uses force to get things done, and I considered not sending anything today. Then came the voice of fear, of self-demand, of supposed responsibility. But God doesn’t threaten; Love neither demands nor forces. So, ignoring that fearful voice, I chose to rest. The complaints grew louder—I watched them calmly and said “no.” Silence followed, and I went to sleep.

Curiously, a few hours later, I ended up writing this—but from a very different place. After an afternoon of sleep, meditation, and walking around the park, I came home and felt the desire to share. Share what? A couple of photos and an idea: there’s no need to force anything. Only the ego tries to impose its will, but the ego isn’t real. The wisest thing is to let go of what you thought you had to do and trust that, if it’s truly for your good, it will happen effortlessly—and if not, it’s better that way.

Today, for me, the true will was that I rest and, once rebalanced, write this. A good reminder. Thank you. Now, back to resting.

With all my love,

A.
