79. I Celebrate You

In case you forgot

The first week of September comes to an end, and many of us have been busy running here and there, picking up the rhythm again after the slow pace of the summer. Today I want to bring up a mental pattern that, in moments like this, tends to intensify, agitating our minds, and I’d like to propose a practice to help recenter it.

We have a cognitive bias that makes us focus exclusively on what is not right and on how much is still left to do. This is useful for survival, and also for staying stressed and on autopilot. Yet every single day we make efforts to move our lives forward. Efforts we take for granted, as if, because they are necessary and routine, they were unworthy of celebration.

I celebrate you, my friend, and I celebrate all the efforts (small, great, and titanic) you’ve made this week so that your life could move ahead.

I invite you to celebrate yourself as well, and here’s an original way to do it. Think of someone dear to you, and think of all the ways they work to make their life go well. Write to them now and tell them you celebrate their daily effort. Let your celebration of yourself be the celebration of another, and watch how joy and gratitude multiply when shared in this way.

The principle behind this practice is the following: all the love we seek is always available, but we have forgotten where it is and how to claim it. We live immersed in an infinite embrace, yet we do not feel it. We are like fish dying of thirst in the middle of the ocean. If we do not experience that love, it is only because we are denying it to ourselves, consciously or unconsciously.

To open ourselves to receive this love, which is our inheritance, there is no need to go out and get it. All we have to do is accept it, and the way to accept it is by giving it.

If you want abundance, create abundance in another’s life.
If you want to feel joy, bring joy to another.
If you want to free yourself from guilt, forgive everyone.
If you want to celebrate your life, celebrate the lives of others.

In the world of the ego, where everything is separate, giving means having less.
In the Kingdom of God, where all is one, giving means receiving.

I invite you to experiment with this idea today and see its effects for yourself.

With all my love,

A.

P.S. And as for all that is still left to do, let us hold fast to this idea from the Tao: “Nature does not hurry, yet everything is accomplished.” Have a wonderful week!
