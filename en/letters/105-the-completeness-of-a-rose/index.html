105. The Completeness of a Rose

It is now, and now, and now as well.

As one gradually lets go of ideas about what one should have been, what is, or what will be, what we truly are begins to reveal itself. And what joy it brings!

And no, it is not the best version of oneself. It is something of an entirely different nature: the leap from the limited to the infinite, from the imperfect to the perfect.

The mystery is that we do not have to change in order to be that.

We already are.

We only have to remember it.

Here is a reminder.

Think of the evolution of a rose: a seed hidden beneath the earth germinates. A shoot emerges in search of light. As it grows, it forms a bud that fills with red petals. One day, the flower opens. Soon after, it begins to wither. The petals fall and the stem bends. Finally, seeing that it has fulfilled its purpose, life lets it go.

There are two ways to perceive this process.

One says that the only thing truly beautiful is the rose in full bloom. Everything else is preparation or decay. Ninety-eight percent of its life is merely a transition. After all, no one would buy a sprout or a withered rose, right?

The other way says that each moment is perfect and complete in itself. Each moment is like a sculpture of a rose, only infinitely more beautiful. A sculpture is made of stone; a rose, of quantum mysteries and breath of life. Such is the depth of the phenomenon “rose” that one could attain enlightenment simply by opening fully to it. Something like that happened to the monk Mahākāśyapa when the Buddha, before all his disciples, held up a flower in silence.

If that is the case with a rose, what must it be like with us?

Have you ever stopped to contemplate the wonder that a human being is?

If for a moment you forgot that you are one of them and looked with the eyes of an alien anthropologist, how would you see the humans around you? What kind of miracle is this?

How did a bacterium in the ocean come to become a being capable of understanding that gravity curves space-time, composing celestial music, perceiving the colors of the rainbow, and feeling beauty?

How can an animal body intuit the presence of Spirit, freely choose to go against all its survival instincts, and face pain and fear guided by love and faith, the substance of God’s promises?

What kind of creature, apparently made of flesh and bone, contains the totality within itself and can come to recognize it consciously?

No, my friend, you are not late, nor are you in the process of becoming anything.

The idea that your life is a transition is only a limited interpretation of something infinite.

You already are what you are.

And you are perfect now, and now, and now as well.

How you feel today is not a mistake.

What happened in the past was not a mistake either.

Nor will the future be.

Everything is perfect.

The entire universe is an inconceivable sculpture, so vast and complex that our mind had to invent space-time in order to contemplate an infinitesimal fraction of it.

You are part of that miracle. You are the rose that appears, grows, opens, and withers.

You are also the eternal life that plays at being a rose, for the joy of experiencing the completeness of every stage of its unfolding.

In the eyes of the ego, yes: you are late, you ate too much for dinner, and you probably should have done eight more things this morning in order to deserve feeling satisfied with yourself.

But that is only a story; shadows projected upon the leaves of the rose. Do not occupy yourself deciphering them.

Simply contemplate: you are eternal life experiencing itself as a human.

With all my love,

Antonio
