91. Guilt and Desire

Two tools to dissolve their causes

The reason it is so difficult to keep the mind at peace is that it is filled with desires. These desires point to things of this world and keep us trapped in the illusion of separation, lack, guilt, pain, and fear. This letter sheds light on the hidden structure of this problem and offers a way to undo it.

Our true Will is to know Reality: to recognize ourselves in the Love that creates and sustains everything. Everything else is not will, but the ego’s desires. Will points to what already is; desires point to what is not, to what we believe we lack. If our unified will is to recognize the One, desire arises from a supposedly incomplete self that seeks to complete itself. What we consider our likes and preferences are nothing but what we believe will complete us.

But that self who desires is a mirage, an optical illusion in which the light of consciousness seems to split in two: the one who desires and the desired object. In truth, you are already complete, but by believing in the reality of desire you buy into the idea of separation and lack. By definition, you can only desire what you think you do not have. This illusion perpetuates the division in your mind and your will.

We often deal with desire through guilt, as if it protected us from giving in to temptation. But guilt is just a poorly placed bandage over an open wound: it does not resolve the inner split, it only hides it by creating another problem, making it harder to reach the real problem and to heal it. Now we not only desire from lack, but punish ourselves for it.

The way I’ve found to undo this emotional and metaphysical knot has two facets: undoing guilt and undoing the illusion of lack.

Undoing Guilt

Guilt is the desire to punish or be punished. It is a painful energy and therefore already a form of punishment, which we sustain because we think we have done something wrong or harmful. Guilt is the intention to deprive ourselves of love, believing we do not deserve it.

The solution is to let go of the intention to punish ourselves, and choose instead to embrace ourselves unconditionally. It is choosing to see ourselves as innocent, even when our mind insists we are guilty. This shift in perception requires the help of a higher power, because it is necessary to transcend our mind and its mechanisms of survival and attack.

All that is needed is to invoke the Holy Spirit and ask Him to accompany us, to show us the Truth, and to teach us to look with Love. Do this consistently, and you will have transformed what you once used to punish yourself into a path of Salvation.

In summary: when you feel guilt, ask the Holy Spirit to stay with you, and notice how a loving presence reveals itself within you. Then, as Saint Augustine said: “Love, and do what you will.”

Undoing Lack

Desire comes from the sense that something is missing. To undo this mirage, ask yourself, “To whom does this desire arise? To me. And who am I?” Your mind will then illuminate the answer. Whether you consciously see something or not is irrelevant. The fact is that every time you look inward instead of outward, you are resolving the cause of the problem.

In my experience, when I ask “To whom does this desire/thought/emotion arise?” I feel that the flashlight of attention suddenly turns inward. Sometimes there is only silence and a pleasant sense of transcendence. Other times, what I find is a painful energy I wasn’t aware of a second earlier, yet it was there, manifesting itself as a desire to eat something, or check my phone, or whatever it might be. By bringing light to the pain, it dissolves in a catharsis. All it wanted was to be seen and embraced. We need nothing but love, because we are nothing but love.

Here it’s also of tremendous relief to invoke a higher power as well to process the pain for you. You don’t have to heal alone. In fact, you cannot heal alone, because you are not alone. Therefore, when the question “Who am I?” reveals the pain, you can use an invocation. I usually say “In the name of Jesus Christ, let there be light!”

If you use those tools consistently, it will be enough. But if you want to go deeper into the mechanism behind desire, keep reading. (Spoiler alert: yes, I’m about to deny your existence as an individual self, so if that’s too much non-duality for today, don’t push it.)

When we ask the question “To whom does this desire arise?” I said that sometimes there is simply silence, and other times there is pain. Let’s look at what lies behind each one.

Silence reveals that there is no one there who desires, just as there is no water in a mirage. The illusion is that, even if the thought says “I want that cookie,” the supposed “I” who is the subject of that sentence can never actually be found. When we inquire, “To whom does this desire arise? To me. And who am I?” we never find anything we can point to and say: that is what I am. The answer is always silence.

With practice, what once seemed like absence begins to reveal itself as presence. What once seemed dark begins to shine with its own light. In reality, there is no incomplete being who desires. The subject of your experience, the “I” at the center of your experience, is not the human being you experience as limited, imperfect, and incomplete, but a soul that already has everything, even if it has forgotten it. Turning inward in this way is slowly remembering your true nature.

The fear that this brings up is part of the same illusion. What you are is forever safe in the Love and Peace of God. Nothing that happens in this world can alter that fundamental fact. Not even death.

As for the painful energy, that is the hidden face of desire. What is desired is always something pleasurable. That means the other half of that duality, something painfu, is close by. That pain is within you, and when you identify with it, it becomes “the one who seeks pleasure.”

The intensity of a desire is proportional to the intensity of the pain from which one hopes to escape by pursuing pleasure. When you look outward, you experience lack; when you look inward, you experience healing.

Where that pain comes from is irrelevant. Looking for its causes in the past is just another strategy to avoid resolving the pain now. Pain is the feeling of not having what is desired now. By identifying with the pain, you buy into its narrative of desire at the cost of your true, complete identity.

This is not a call to asceticism or rejection of the world. Buddha recognized that that is not the way, and only discovered the fullness of his true Self by embracing the Middle Way: neither aversion nor attraction toward the things of this world.

This is an invitation to remember that you are already complete, and that the one who experiences lack is not you but an energy you mistakenly identified with. That energy can be dissolved completely. By working on it, you reclaim the full enjoyment of what you already have, which is everything.

And if a desire arises, it will arise from that fullness, from the wish to share what you have, not from lack or the urge to fill a void. It will be an expression of the Self, not of the limited “me.”

All it requires is to stop looking toward the object of desire and turn your attention back toward the supposed subject who desires:

“To whom does this desire arise? To me. Okay, and who am I?”

With all my love,

A.
