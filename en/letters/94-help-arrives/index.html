94. Help Arrives

Letting myself be taught not to trust only in my own strength.

As I learn to surrender and trust, I see that God is not something abstract or separate from the daily matters of this world. Saint Francis de Sales said that the measure of divine providence for us is the trust we place in it.

Today's letter recounts the events of my last day in Boston. Feeling exhausted, I had no choice but to trust, and providence clearly accepted my invitation to intervene.

The last few days in Boston were a clear mirror of the state of my mind. I saw how deeply I can trust, and the ways in which I don’t. Peace and fear alternated like the blue sky and the clouds. That alternation is an optical illusion of our earthly perspective. The sky is always there and always the same. The clouds come and go, always in different shapes, but always with the same function: to hide the sky.

On Tuesday, I was flying to Spain in the afternoon, and I woke up feeling somewhat overwhelmed. This time my suitcases weren’t for a short trip, but for moving all the clothes, objects, and books I had accumulated over the past four years that I wanted to keep. Six suitcases in total, all different sizes, but all overweight. After three days of hard work and little sleep, both my mind and body were exhausted.

As soon as I woke up, I sat down to contemplate my lesson from A Course in Miracles: “I am blessed as a Son of God.” Then I prayed for strength, knowing that asking for a virtue is a way of remembering it and, therefore, activating it. After that, I threw myself into the whirlwind.

I left the house, and on my way to my first destination of the day, a stranger joined me on my walk. She was having a coffee, just hanging out in the street at 23°F, when she noticed the flowers I was carrying. She told me she liked them very much and started walking with me, telling me how she enjoyed drying flowers and doing crafts with them. I listened, gradually opening myself to her unexpected presence. She told me how her mother had a wonderful touch with plants and that she had a beautiful garden. In the center of the garden, a statue of Jesus rose, extending His hand as if saying, “Come to me, all who are weary and burdened.” She told me that it was there her mother had taught her to pray. I asked if there was a specific prayer, and she answered:

Our Father, who art in Heaven,
hallowed be thy name.
Thy Kingdom come.
Thy will be done
on Earth as it is in Heaven.
Give us this day our daily bread.
Forgive us our trespasses,
as we forgive those
who trespass against us.
And lead us not into temptation,
but deliver us from evil.

“Amen,” we both said together, as we reached the point where our paths split. She looked at me and said, “You have a blessed day.”

“Thank you, you too,” I said to Christ, as He walked away in the body of the “stranger.”

A couple of hours later, I returned to the apartment. It was almost time to leave, and I still had to put a lot of things out on the street for anyone who wanted them, and also carry the suitcases down four flights of stairs without an elevator. Then my friend Ricky arrived, having offered to take me to the airport. I told him the suitcases were heavy and that it would be better to carry them down one at a time, sharing the load. He looked at me with a little smile and said, “Don’t worry, I’ve got this.” Then Christ, in His herculean body, lifted two suitcases (one 15 kg in one hand and another 32 kg in the other) and began carrying them down all by himself as if it were nothing. He did the same with all the others.

“Thank you, Lord.”

While Ricky handled the heavy lifting, two more friends joined and helped me carry everything else to the street. At one point, climbing the stairs, one of them asked, “How are you feeling?” I said, “This morning I was exhausted, but now I feel like I’m being carried.” “Floating up the stairs,” he said. I laughed and replied, “More like the stairs are floating down for me!” It was as if I were outside my body, while a greater force handled it effortlessly.

Then, on the way to the airport, I said to Ricky, “Several of these suitcases are overweight; I hope the person at the counter me will be kind.”

When we arrived, the woman at the counter called me over, and I said, “Good morning, how are you?” as I dragged one of the heaviest suitcases. I caught her incredible response in passing; I put down the suitcase, looked at her, and said, “Did you say you’re blessed?” She smiled and said, “Yes, I am blessed, and you too.” I began to laugh and said that, without a doubt, it was true. “Amen,” said Christ in the body of the Iberia employee.

All the suitcases were overweight, but only one did she say, “My friend, this one is too heavy.” I removed two books, gave one of sales to Ricky, and one of Rumi’s poems to her.

After finishing check-in, I said to Ricky, “I feel like I’ve been spending the whole morning hanging out with Jesus.”

Ricky smiled and said, “I think it’s a reflection of what you give.”

We hugged, and I thanked him once more. But this time I did not only give thanks to Ricky, but to the One who was listening to my words through him; the One who had been helping me all morning.

Thank you.

With all my love,

A.
