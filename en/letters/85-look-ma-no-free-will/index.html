Nº 85 · October 19, 2025

Look Ma, no free will!

The truth shall set you free, but the first sip is bitter.

The truth shall set you free, but the first sip is bitter.

This week, in qigong class, I caught a glimpse of one of those truths. The first impact was devastating (for the ego), and then came a deep relief. Here’s my attempt to put what I saw into words.

On one side there is the self, the little human we normally identify with. On the other side, there is the Self, the part of our mind that is universal and shared with all of Creation. That Self is also known as Christ, Buddha, or atman.

The difference between the two can be seen through the analogy of a movie. We can follow the main character, Frodo, for example, on his journey through Middle-earth. But we can also realize that every instant of that journey is appearing on a screen. The story undergoes all kinds of transformations, but the screen remains unchanged.

The spiritual path is like Frodo’s journey. The difference is that the goal isn’t to destroy the ring, but for Frodo to realize that he is the screen.

That’s what I saw yesterday in qigong class. We were holding a difficult posture when Joe said: “You’re holding the posture with your mind.”

When I heard that, there was a shift of dimension, and I saw that the true difficulty of the posture wasn’t the physical fatigue it caused, but my mind’s resistance to sustaining it. That was what we were really working on. Seeing that, I understood that my whole life had been like that, but I hadn’t noticed. I had spent my entire life tending to the body, without paying much attention to what my mind was doing.

I saw that I am actually the mind, but not the individual mind trapped in Antonio’s body, rather the Mind: the screen upon which the entire movie of existence appears.

When I opened my eyes, I saw some clouds in the distance. I stared at them very intently, as if it were a matter of life and death. Then I looked at Joe and said this statement as a question:

“Joe, I have the same control over Antonio as I do over the shape of those clouds.”

Joe looked at me and, smiling tenderly, said: “That’s right.”

The will that gives form to those clouds is the same will that gives form to Antonio’s life. There is no individual will, that’s the illusion of the ego, the illusion of a separate self. There is only one Will, which in this very moment directs both the words I choose and the yellow leaves falling from the tree outside the window.

The illusion of the separate self is based on the belief that we have the capacity to decide. Our thoughts are constantly trying to decide whether it’s better to do this or that, or looking back, wondering whether it would have been better to do something else. Future and past. But after this revelation, I see that those thoughts mean nothing; they are just noise that distorts our vision and our true will.

So what remains if I have no free will? What do I cling to if what I thought I was has turned out to be a mirage?

This is where the concept of the observer comes in. When one meditates, that’s what one is cultivating: the ability to remain steady as the observer. From that point of view, the world, the body, the thoughts, and everything perceptible appear like a movie. But the observer is separate from all that, like someone watching the film.

The observer clearly sees that it is not what it observes. Identifying with that is very liberating, because then one understands that nothing that happens in this world can truly affect you, just as nothing that happens in the movie can affect the screen. Not even the death of the protagonist. From there, it becomes clear that what we really are transcends space and time.

Today, while having breakfast, I watched my mind trying to decide what to do next. But remembering the other day’s revelation, I realized there was no need to think about it. The clouds don’t think about what shape they’ll have in ten minutes. The autumn trees don’t plan which leaves to let fall today and which to save for tomorrow. In the same way, my whole life is in the hands of a will that transcends my individual mind. My individual mind knows nothing, and if I cling to it, my behavior will be as incoherent as its thoughts.

Earlier I said the first impact was devastating. When my mind discovered that free will is an illusion, it thought: If I let go of the part of me that plans and tries to control what I do, I’ll end up doing nothing, like a vegetable!

Facing that fear was the bitter sip. But fear is an illusion. On the other side, I discovered that what remains is the freedom to be what I am. By no longer worrying about what I have to do, I’ve started doing it. These have been the three most productive days of the semester. And on top of that, I feel like I’m on vacation, the same level of carefree ease.

This week, a great cloud dissolved, and now my true Self shines a little brighter, guiding my body with a wisdom that is not mine, but which I follow like a sheep follows its shepherd—moment by moment, trusting in Him. Whatever happens to Antonio, whatever he does or doesn’t do, has always been in God’s hands. Now I’m beginning to accept it, and the relief is immense.

Gladly I place all my tasks, worries, and my entire future in His Hands. And little by little, I return to the place that has always been mine, the peace of mind that was promised, the peace that is my true Home.

With all my love,

A.
