Freedom Begins By Taking Responsibility

Freedom begins by taking responsibility

This is something I find to be true over and over again. Some aspect of life in this world first appears as an unbearable burden, and I resist and lament for a while. But eventually, if God wills, a moment comes when the gradual expansion of my consciousness leads me to see that this strategy (lamenting) is not coherent and it causes me suffering. I am complicating my own life. I don’t know how to do it better, and I need help.

So I pray, or turn to someone who loves me, or I say, “I am not a victim of the world I see.” By saying that, I am declaring, “I take responsibility for what happens in this aspect of my life.” In doing so, and here is the key, the power needed to properly handle this new responsibility returns to me (I had given it away in exchange for the bitter satisfactions that the position of the victim offers).

From this new position of greater power, I see that what once seemed like an unbearably heavy burden now offers me a path to freedom, accompanied by joy and a sense of lightness.

Perhaps these infinite and eternal words of Jesus allude to the process I’m describing:

"Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy and my burden is light." [Mt 11, 28-30]

Another very valuable perspective is the one Dr. Hawkins expresses in his class “Vision” (Feb 2005) when speaking about what to do with the resources and gifts one has.

“It’s not greed, you see? If you demonize the ego, you get paralyzed with guilt. No, you have a responsibility, especially if you’re destined to become enlightened, to be the steward of your assets. Forget about winning. Get out of the solar plexus, which is about winning and greed and fear of losing. And even if you succeed you end up with guilt. Why? Because your increase means somebody else’s decrease.

No, you’ve got to recontextualize your assets. What are your assets? Time – how many years you have left, how many hours in the day – assets, financial, personality-wise… If you’re 6 feet 7, and fast on the court you have an obligation to respect that capacity and that talent. So, your talents, abilities, assets, instead of greed, now you respect them, you’re grateful for them and you handle them with respect and honor. You owe that respect to yourself and to your destiny. You can’t waste years playing around at the casinos and things when there’s far more important things to do.”

Thank you, Christ.

Thank you, Doc.

Thank you for reading.

With all my love,

A.
