117. Surrendering to Commitment

Why we avoid it, and what we actually find when we accept it

Why are we afraid to follow our calling?

Why do we resist choosing what we know is good for us and for others?

Total commitment is not a matter of effort, but of surrender. To commit fully is to surrender completely to something, to let something be. That is why, when you commit totally, it becomes instantly easy: it is no longer you who carries it out, but God himself.

The fear of commitment, therefore, is really the fear of God; the fear of his will being done in your life.

The world speaks of fear of commitment, but what is truly frightening is not commitment, but its absence. Because the one who resists commitment is the ego, the illusion of having an individual will capable of deciding on its own. That is inseparable from fear, because it is the belief of being alone, facing an antagonistic world. A mind like that is fragmented and turned against itself. A mind like that is its own enemy. Without commitment, we live in doubt, in distraction, trying this and that, following a mind that does not know what it wants or where it is going.

Because the mind does not know. The best it can do is "know about," and that is not knowing. There is no certainty or true knowledge in it. Certainty belongs to the heart. And one can only truly commit to what one knows is true, and that is only known in the heart. Committing to something that is not true is like committing to a cloud. Commitment is either with the truth and is total, or it is not commitment.

When we commit to the truth, what we are really doing is surrendering to it. And when that happens, everything within us that opposes the truth will be triggered. That is the paradox of commitment: commitment to love leads us to experience our judgments and resentments. Commitment to peace shows us our conflicts. Commitment to certainty triggers doubts and confusion. Commitment to God and to others confronts us with our greatest enemy: the illusion we have made of ourselves. In short, commitment to the truth confronts us with everything that opposes the truth.

And what opposes the truth?

Fear: the fear of not being enough, of not being worthy of being loved, of the world discovering that we are in reality something dark, something guilty, something shameful, something unforgivable.

When you commit to the truth, all of that comes to the surface. The fear of commitment, therefore, is really the fear of facing our own deepest fears. But not facing them does not make them disappear; they simply stay in the shadow, blindly guiding our lives, keeping our minds wrapped in darkness.

That is why I walk hand in hand with Jesus, because he descended into those depths and passed through them, opening a path of light. To walk hand in hand with Jesus is to remember the Christ in you. It is to pass through hell knowing that you are invulnerable. You are still as God created you. No crucifixion can harm you. Salvation already is, and now at last you are claiming it.

And so the kingdom of fear ends and one opens to the Kingdom of God.

The commitment to that future which once terrified you is now revealed as surrender to the will of God.

The will of God is peace, joy, abundance and love.

The will of God is his Creation, his Being lovingly extended.

The will of God is you, the true you.

With all my love,

Antonio
