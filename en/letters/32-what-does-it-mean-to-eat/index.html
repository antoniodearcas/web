32. What does it mean to eat?

This morning, while having breakfast with Betül and listening to Molly Drake (Nick Drake’s mother) sing in the background, a recurring question came back to me:

What does it mean to eat?

In Spanish, the verb “to mean” is significar, and “meaning” is significado. My question was therefore “what does eating signify?”.

Before sharing the answer I received, I want to talk about the meaning of the verb to signify. It comes from two Latin words: signum (which means sign, mark, banner—something that people follow) and facere (a verb meaning to make). When something signifies (means) something, it implies that we have attributed to it the quality of pointing to something beyond itself. Something meaningful becomes a bridge between the visible (the sign) and what it represents (the meaning).

This can happen in a direct and literal way: a STOP sign means you must stop. An icon with an envelope means Email. Each word in this letter is a sign pointing to its meaning.

But it can also be deeper and more abstract, becoming a symbol. A symbol says more than it says. It’s mysterious, and its meaning has no end. What does your country’s flag mean? What does a circle, the Christian cross, or the swastika mean?

The swastika is a good example for what I want to share. Originally, the swastika was a Buddhist symbol pointing to the cycles of life, the evolution of the universe, eternity, and also to the teachings of Buddha for achieving liberation. In its origins, it was a symbol of enlightenment.

Many centuries and misunderstandings later, the Nazis adopted this symbol to represent the supremacy of their “Aryan race,” the power of their nation, and their plans for domination. From that moment on, for everyone else, the swastika became a symbol of hatred, genocide, and terror.

Returning to the beginning of my letter, today I asked myself once again: What does it mean to eat?

My relationship with food has been difficult since I was born. After 29 years of traumatic relationship with it, this past spring, I finally decided to put some order in there, to bring light and love to where I perceived darkness and emptiness. It then became the focus of my spiritual journey because it was where I experienced the most suffering.

The day I decided to enter that dark cave, food signified trauma, illness, fear, and guilt. It was like the Nazi swastika to me—I would see it, and my hair would stand on end. But it also meant desire, representing the quick yet ineffective relief of the emptiness I felt. My meals flowed in that tension between desire and fear, which, unsurprisingly, made me feel bad afterwards and eventually led me to sickness.

In that first meal, I stood before the plate like a soldier before the battlefield and prayed for help. The answer to my prayer came immediately in the form of a shift in my perception. Where once was the enemy, the plate, I now saw the Teacher, the Friend, the Savior, who appeared radiant in the form of a steaming plate, saying to me: “This vision is the end of the journey and also the beginning. I will be with you every step of the way.”

And with that, I began to eat, without distractions, paying attention to the food and to my body, opening my trembling arms to anything that wanted to come out of that cave. And things came out, indeed.

Several months, storms, and miracles later, my relationship with food has changed profoundly. Along the way, I’ve let go of the meanings I had attached to food that caused me suffering. It’s becoming something increasingly neutral, less charged with emotions and stories. There’s less and less need to think about it. The meanings I was projecting onto the act of eating are growing thinner and thinner, like a layer of clouds so fine you begin to glimpse the blue sky barely hidden behind.

Today, as I looked at my half-finished bowl of oatmeal, I asked again: What does it mean to eat? The answer, like all revelations, is deeply personal and impossible to put into words. But that’s the challenge of Esferas, so here I go.

The bowl of oatmeal said to me:

You’ve heard that food is an act of communion with all of creation. The entire universe collaborates so you can have this bowl of oatmeal, strawberries, blueberries, and other fruits of the earth grown by hands other than your own. But that meaning, which you can summarize in two lines, isn’t enough for you—you who seek an experience, direct knowledge, not just a belief, however beautiful it may be.

You’ve understood that food is the excuse nature has created for us to gather. Food keeps us alive, but it also keeps us united. It is the fundamental way to create community. But that isn’t enough for you, who often eat alone.

Food can mean whatever you choose, and you can enjoy or suffer the fruits of your “creation” for as long as you wish. In your case, you had created a monster out of food, perceiving in it something that was destroying you.

In your suffering, you came to Me, and you’ve allowed Me to heal your mind enough to understand that any pain you bring to Me will become your path to salvation.

You lost your voice for years. You came to Me, and I healed you. Now, when you sing, it is no longer a separate individual singing—I sing through you.

You suffered in your relationship with food, and in your pain and fear, you learned to remember Me every time you sit at the table. Food has become an act of communion with Me, though not in the way you expected.

You were looking for a ritual, a prayer, some way to make the mundane sacred. Now you understand that the mundane is nothing (if you give it your own meaning) or everything (if you allow Me to reveal its true meaning to you).

This isn’t limited to food. Everything you give to Me will become an act of communion with Me. What causes you suffering is the meaning you have projected onto things. Come to Me, and I will cleanse your eyes so you can see things as they truly are, without separation.

By abandoning the meaning you have given to things, you will stop seeing them as limited and instead see Me, remembering that you are one with Me. This awareness of unity is love, and in that light, fear and suffering cannot exist.

All of this is my attempt to explain the mechanism of Salvation, as I perceived it this morning. But someone far more eloquent than I, Jesus, expressed it this way:

If you bring forth what is within you, what you bring forth will save you. If you do not bring forth what is within you, what you do not bring forth will destroy you.

This is Saying 70 from the Gnostic Gospel of Thomas, which my qigong teacher mentioned in class last week.

And this is where I am, friends. In my last letter, I barely managed to express that my perspective had taken another leap. These have been days of beautiful revelations, creative freedom, and much greater inner peace. The knot is unraveling quickly, and I’m starting to breathe (and eat) better.

With all my love,

A.
