109. We Can Only See in the Light

Are we the pot or the clay?

Today I sat down before a blank page and, instead of bringing something already thought out to write about, I simply said: "Lord, here I am. Guide me."

The result has made me very happy.

Thank you, Father, for this gift.

The Indian sage Ramana Maharshi attained Enlightenment spontaneously at the age of sixteen. One day, without being ill or anything of the sort, he suddenly felt, with absolute terror, that he was going to die. But instead of trying to flee or cry for help, he decided to investigate the experience of death. He lay down on the floor, went completely still as if already in rigor mortis, and asked himself: "This body dies. But what am I?"

He then recognized the "I Am" of God as his own identity, beyond all possibility of death. That awakening to eternal life came as a great flash of light.

We can only see in the light.

After that, he left his home, leaving behind a note that read: "I have set out from here in search of my Father and in obedience to His call." He boarded a train and made his way toward a sacred mountain called Arunachala, from which he would never again be parted.

After searching for a long time, his mother finally found him there, sitting, thin, and silent. In those days Ramana lived an ascetic life and did not speak. His mother urged him to come home with her, but he answered by writing this note:

"What is destined to happen will happen, no matter how hard you try to prevent it. What is not destined to happen will not happen, no matter how hard you try. Therefore, the best path is to remain in silence."

We can only see in the light.

But what is it that one sees in the light?

That you are not alone, and that what happens does not depend solely on you and your limited strength. You are immersed in the ocean that is the will of God. Your individual will is a small branch making its own leaves and fruit, but there is only one tree. There is only one Being. Ramana called it Atman; others call it Christ.

There is only one body: the Body of Christ, which includes the light that was created on the first day, the firmament on the second, the earth and its vegetation on the third, the sun, the moon and the stars on the fourth, the creatures of the sea and sky on the fifth, the land animals and the human being on the sixth.

The peace in the eyes of animals.

The total surrender of the trees that let all their leaves fall without lamenting, without even knowing that spring will come again afterward.

Everything shines with the same light, the same life and truth. There is nothing beyond that. Nothing can lie outside the infinite; nothing can be apart from its Source nor be of a different nature.

So what is our problem?

There is only one, and the young Ramana resolved it once and for all when he asked himself: "This body dies. But what am I?"

What one sees in the light is that the problem is already solved. Salvation is already complete; all is already forgiven. The body will die, but that is only a pot; the clay remains. The vessel says of itself: "Poor me, a sinner!"The clay is innocence.

The clay that forms us is no ordinary mud, it is the light of God. Clear the mind of ideas of separation, and all that remains is light without beginning, end, or division.

From there, you will see that your individual life follows the same loving will as all the rest of the Universe. You will see that everything is perfect as it is. Everything that happens is beneficial. The hand that shapes your days knows you and knows better than anyone what is best for you. Anxiety disappears and in its place shines trust. Fear disperses and wisdom remains. Anger gives way to compassion, and guilt to innocence. Joy envelops all things, and love reveals that all is one.

Today we celebrate the Resurrection, the glorious moment in which this truth redeemed the Universe. But that cosmic event is, at its heart, nothing more than the contrast between darkness and light. Only the light is real, and no one was ever born, nor will anyone ever die.

That is why, half a century later and already on his deathbed, one of his disciples said to Ramana:

"Bhagavan, you are leaving us."

To which He replied:

"Where could I go? I am here."

With all my love,

Antonio
