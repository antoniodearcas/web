40. End-of-Year Reflection: Relationships

5 questions about each area of your life to end the year with clarity

RELATIONSHIPS

1. What relationships have nourished me and brought greater meaning to my life this year?

2. How have I been a contribution to the well-being of others, whether through my time, love, or service?

3. Are there relationships I need to strengthen, heal, or let go of?

4. What have I learned about being a better friend, partner, companion, or family member?

5. What do I want to do next year to feel more connected with the people I value?

REFLECTION

This year, I discovered a powerful concept for understanding relationships: Rnanubandhana (ऋणानुबन्धन). It comes from Sanskrit, where "Rna" means "debt" and "anubandhana" means "bond." I asked ChatGPT to describe it in a paragraph:

Rnanubandhana is a concept in Vedic philosophy that describes karmic bonds between souls, formed through debts accumulated in past-life interactions. These debts are not necessarily material but can also be emotional, energetic, or spiritual, creating ties that transcend time and manifest as significant relationships in the present life — be they of love, friendship, family, or conflict. The purpose of these bonds is to balance unresolved karma, learn important lessons, and evolve spiritually. From this perspective, understanding and accepting these connections — through detachment, forgiveness, and compassion — allows one to release these debts and transcend the karmic cycle, moving closer to moksha or spiritual liberation.

Thanks, Chatty. I’ll take the mic back now.

This concept is rooted in two other ideas not included in Christian doctrine but embraced by other religions and worth exploring for the insight they provide: reincarnation and karma. I’m not asking you to accept and integrate them into your worldview but to use them as tools, just temporarily, to see your relationships from a new perspective.

These concepts suggest that nothing in this universe is accidental or random; everything follows a law and is exactly where it needs to be at any given moment. Combined with the Christian understanding that God is infinitely wise and loving, we can deduce that absolutely everything that happens to us in this world is for our maximum benefit. Every moment offers the optimal conditions for our spiritual growth, no matter how difficult or uncomfortable it might seem.

The concept of rnanubandhana helped me understand how this knowledge applies to relationships. No encounter is casual or insignificant. From the most meaningful relationships (your parents, children, or partner) to interactions with the cashier or the stranger asking for directions to the subway, everything happens to facilitate an exchange of energy in accordance to this universal law.

Shortly after discovering this concept, I went to a party. A yogi friend (devoted yoga practitioner) invited me, and all his friends there were yogis as well. One of them sat next to me—a big, smiling guy with a body like a Greek sculpture and radiant energy. Evidently, he was an athlete, and he worked as a physical trainer. At one point, I said to him:

"You know, this week I discovered a concept that says no encounter is random. You and I have sat together and started talking because there’s an exchange that had to happen. It might be complete with this conversation, and we may never see each other again, or it could go far beyond today."

"I can feel it!" he replied.

Shortly after, he wrote to me, saying he had just returned from an inspiring trip and wanted to cultivate his creativity by learning to play guitar. He suggested that in exchange for me teaching him music, he could train me physically.

This is the friend I mentioned in my last letter about the body. In our first training session, my relationship with my body changed forever, unlocking a new level of awareness. Since then, we’ve met a couple of times a month, and after every encounter, we leave feeling elevated, with new insights and simple yet powerful tools.

But he hasn’t just taught me to teach music or how relate to my body with more love—he has also taught me about friendship itself.

On one side of the spectrum, there are “friendships” that, after spending time together, leave you worse off: more tired, sad, angry, confused, or physically, mentally, and emotionally drained. Relationships that consume and unbalance your energy. Relationships based on mutual reinforcement of unconscious behaviors, justifying and strengthening each other’s negative patterns, in exchange for the other doing the same for you.

On the other side, there are friendships that genuinely seek each other’s good. Instead of muddying the water, they clear it. Instead of thickening the clouds, they dissipate them. If you’re limping, they walk with you, heal you, and teach you to walk better so you can continue your journey without depending on them.

These virtuous friendships act as a mirror that reflects two things: what makes you suffer, recontextualized from a broader perspective that brings relief, understanding, and compassion; and also the light within you, magnified and strengthened.

The other day, at my sister’s wedding, I read a passage from Saint Paul’s Letter to the Colossians that summarizes this well:

"Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly; teach and admonish one another with all wisdom." [Colossians 3:16]

Now, what to do with the relationships that are on the darker side of the spectrum, the ones that feel more like a heavy chain than a clear mirror? The first step is to identify them, and it’s easy: Is there someone who often comes to mind, provoking inner arguments and disturbing your peace? There you have a nice karmic bond.

The next step is to undo it, and there are several ways to do this.

Jesus didn’t speak about karma, but he did center his teachings on forgiveness. Forgiveness is the key to undoing the bond. By forgiving, the karmic debt is settled, and both souls are freed, especially the one who forgives. The other person doesn’t even need to know—though their mind will also gain in clarity, as our minds are not separate.

Forgiveness is effective when it genuinely changes the way you perceive the other person. If your perception hasn’t shifted—if you haven’t gone from seeing a demon to seeing an innocent soul—then the forgiveness wasn’t real or complete.

The tool I’ve used to learn and practice this is A Course In Miracles. Don’t let the title put you off. By “miracles,” it doesn’t mean multiplying loaves and fishes. It defines a miracle as a shift in perception that takes you from fear to love. Sometimes, this spiritual and mental shift is so powerful that the body heals as a result (hence the reputation of miracles). But what always heals is the heart. If you want to bring order and harmony to your relationships, this is a quick and powerful way. A word of warning: spirituality might sometimes seem all about flowers, robes, and flutes. A Course in Miracles is more like heavy metal—or a sword. The invitation is there.

Once forgiveness has been granted and the karmic bond undone, you are free to decide whether it is better to continue the relationship or leave it behind. This time, that decision will come from discernment, not resentment.

The idea, then, is that we must love everyone, but that doesn’t mean we need to hang out with everyone.

To conclude, I’ll share a haiku I wrote after going through this process with one of the most challenging relationships of my life, finally leaving it behind in peace.

Two currents meet

in opposite directions ~

Whirlpool

With all my love,
